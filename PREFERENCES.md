# Problem-selection preferences

The scout scores every candidate slate against this file. This is the operator's taste, encoded. When a pick misses, the fix goes here, not into a review step.

## The anchor

Every problem must cash out in one of two payoffs:

- **Human welfare.** Health, cognition, income, lived conditions. The findings improve something a person feels.
- **Frontier progress.** A capability humanity wants and could plausibly reach: space, new inventions, AI, the buildable edge of the physically possible. The findings move a real decision about getting there, not a daydream about having arrived.

The rigor bar does not move, and it is the brand: every claim sourced, every number falsifiable, the inversion earned by evidence and never asserted. We do not write vision essays. We do the unglamorous homework they skip: what specifically is stuck, what it would cost, which decision unlocks it. A piece that trades that discipline for wonder fails, however inspiring it reads.

## The anchor invariants (shape-neutral; every candidate needs all four)

A candidate that misses one is out, whatever its shape or payoff.

- Quantified stakes at scale: a human cost a stranger feels, or a technical magnitude that reorders priorities.
- A desk-research wedge: the agent can produce the missing artifact itself, not recommend that others do research.
- A named decision it changes: a regulator, funder, agency, court, or program office could act within a year or two if handed the artifact. The payoff may be distant; the decision is not.
- A dramatic inversion likely waiting inside: the finding that flips the naive expectation makes the report and the tweet.

## Shape decides the winner

Category gives variety; shape is the story the report tells. All published problems so far are one shape, the overdue-fix dossier (quantify a gap, name a cheap proven fix, hand an actor the document). That is a monoculture, and the next picks should break it. The legitimate shapes:

1. **Overdue-fix dossier.** A proven, cheap fix ($1/DALY-class) sits undeployed; the gap is grind, not invention; the artifact hands a named actor the document that closes it. Diffusion analogue: scurvy, Semmelweis, H. pylori. Works, but over-represented now.
2. **The measurement.** The number nobody has computed, computed. The contribution is the estimate itself: the first credible figure for a cost, count, rate, or magnitude that decisions need. No fix required.
3. **The investigation.** Who is responsible, or where the money went. The artifact names what was unnamed: actors, flows, culpability. Any fix follows from the naming.
4. **The teardown.** A widely-believed claim, a deployed intervention, or a hyped frontier technology that the evidence does not support. The artifact documents the inversion. The win is stopping waste or deflating a bubble, not starting a fix.
5. **The forecast.** What is coming, costed, with the decision it forces now. Not prophecy: a falsifiable projection (capability X reaches threshold Y by date Z, at cost C) and the choice a named actor faces today because of it. The natural shape for frontier topics.

Slate rule: span at least three of these five shapes. Prefer a winner whose shape is not yet published; a published shape wins only if its execution is clearly the strongest on the slate. Within a shape, the invariants and the inversion break ties.

## What bores me / hard filters

- Meta-problems and process problems: research about research, coordination of coordinators. The deliverable becomes a report about reports. (Empirical: zombie trials, rejected.)
- Worthy-but-unglamorous civic systems: housing, education systems, food systems, institutional reform, preparedness. Important, not mine. (Empirical: operator cut these categories from the roster, 2026-06-10.)
- Problems whose honest conclusion is "this needs a decade of institutional reform, and nothing decides anything sooner". The payoff can be distant (a Mars capability, a frontier invention), but the artifact must inform a concrete decision someone faces in the next year or two. No "someone should study this".
- Saturated topics where the marginal contribution is commentary. Frontier topics especially: if the contribution is one more opinion about Mars or AGI rather than a number or a stuck mechanism nobody has mapped, it is commentary. Out.
- Hard filters (binary): quantifiable stakes (human cost or technical magnitude) with published data to synthesize; plausibly completable in 4-6 desk-research milestones; one problem at a time.
