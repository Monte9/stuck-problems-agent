## Executive summary

Satellites ended the technical anonymity of methane super-emitters years ago; institutions have preserved it by choice. UNEP's IMEO archive now publishes per-plume notification and response flags: of 11,607 oil-and-gas plumes detected since January 2024, 50.5% triggered a MARS notification, but only 13.4% of notified plumes ever drew an operator response [M2 §1]. UNEP still declines to name responsible parties [EOGT-26]. This brief, built on four source-verified artifacts [M1–M4], supplies the names and pairs each with the lever that reaches it.

Three cases define the enforcement map:

**Sonatrach (Algeria) — reached by the EU import standard.** The largest peak-rate event in the 2024–26 public record is the Hassi Messaoud complex: 649 ± 339 t CH4/h, 286 detections since January 2024, operator feedback recorded on one [M2 row 1]. Algeria supplied 14.4% of EU gas imports in 2024; Sonatrach is not an OGMP 2.0 member and has no plausible route to the MRV-equivalence demonstration that Article 28 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 requires from 1 January 2027 [M3 §3–4]. No other supplier pairs this event record with this much EU exposure.

**Türkmennebit and Türkmengaz (Turkmenistan) — reached by MARS escalation and EU diplomacy, not trade.** Five named IMEO sources — a handful of flares and gas-disposal units — emit roughly 0.25 Mt CH4/yr; operator feedback is logged on two while emissions persist into 2026 [M2 rows 9, 17, 18; M4 §6.1]. Notification currently produces paperwork, not repairs. The Regulation's Article 31 super-emitter rapid-reaction mechanism and the EU–Turkmenistan channel are the instruments [M3 §4].

**US LNG sellers Venture Global, Freeport and Sempra — reached only through Brussels after the 2025 US rollback.** The US Waste Emissions Charge was overturned and federal compliance deadlines deferred to January 2027 [GMT26 p. 68, via M4 row 1]; the US answered 9 of 369 MARS notifications [EOGT-26]. Unlike Cheniere, these exporters are not OGMP members [M3 §3]; Article 28 is the only binding instrument that reaches their cargoes.

The counter-example proving the mechanism works: **Pemex (Mexico)** was notified at Ku-Maloob-Zaap — the largest oil-and-gas entry in the January 2026 IMEO top-50, est. 165,626 t/yr — responded, and the source left the top-50 by May 2026 [M2 row 19].

Economics is not the obstacle: ~30 Mt of upstream abatement pays for itself at 2025 prices, concentrated where MRV is weakest [GMT26 p. 9; M4 §1, §5]. What is missing is enforcement infrastructure that attaches names to consequences. Before January 2027, DG ENER must deliver the Article 30 transparency database (slipped from February to ~September 2026) and refuse the "stop-the-clock" deferral industry demanded on 20 April 2026 [M3 §4]; UNEP should publish the operator names its own data already implies; funders should close the attribution gap that keeps half the league table anonymous. Every recommendation below is checkable within 12 months.

## 1. The league table: who leaks, and who ignores the phone

M2 ranks the 30 largest publicly detected oil-and-gas super-emitter events and persistent sources of 2024–2026, from a full dump of the IMEO plume archive (24,005 records) and the Carbon Mapper API (2,969 oil-and-gas plumes), naming operators in 16 of 30 rows via Global Energy Monitor asset records and Carbon Mapper's published 2025 attribution [M2 §2, §4]. Four response patterns emerge, and they — not emission rates — are the enforcement-relevant finding:

1. **Never notified.** The largest US detections (Permian and Barnett TROPOMI events, 409–439 t/h, the latter quality-flagged) carry no MARS record at all [M2 rows 6, 8].
2. **Notified, ignored.** Iran's Marun–Karanj–Parsi belt: 355 detections, 306 notified, zero operator feedback — the largest fully ignored notified complex in the archive; operators are NIOC subsidiaries [M2 row 24]. A 542 t/h West Siberia event was notified on 2025-09-22; no feedback [M2 row 2]. Egypt's EGY_S_055: 32 notified plumes, zero feedback [M2 row 29].
3. **Notified, acknowledged, never fixed.** Türkmennebit's Goturdepe complex: ~320 detections, 260 notified, feedback on 146 — still emitting May 2026 [M2 row 9]. Esenguly source TKM_S_024 logged feedback on 136 plumes yet remained the #1 oil-and-gas entry in the May 2026 top-50 [M2 row 18].
4. **Notified, fixed.** Pemex at Ku-Maloob-Zaap (above) and a Türkmengas facility near Üçajy with both operator and government feedback after a 488 t/h detection [M2 rows 19, 3].

Aggregates match: 1,200 MARS notifications drew 15 replies in 2024 (1%); 2,203 drew ~12% in 2025; the US response rate, 9 of 369, is among the worst on record [EOGT-24; EOGT-26]. UNEP's top-50 product publishes coordinates, source type and response flags but "does not identify the responsible party by name" [EOGT-26]. The names in M2 came from public records that anyone — including UNEP — can join.

## 2. The import lever: what binds in January 2027, and who cannot comply

M3 covers 90.9% of the EU's 277.9 bcm of 2024 extra-EU gas imports across 17 named countries, each matched to intensity evidence, OGMP 2.0 status, and the M2 event record [M3 §1–3].

**The legal machinery** (Regulation (EU) 2024/1787, full text verified [REG; M3 §4]): importers already report producer identity and MRV/LDAR practices annually (Art. 27, since 5 May 2025). From **1 January 2027**, Art. 28 requires importers to *demonstrate* that contracts concluded or renewed since 4 August 2024 cover only hydrocarbons under producer-level MRV equivalent to the Regulation — with OGMP 2.0 **Level 5** reporting named as an equivalence route (Art. 28(5)). Intensity reporting follows from August 2028 and maximum intensity values for new contracts from August 2030 (Art. 29). The Commission owed a transparency database by 5 February 2026 (Art. 30) — reportedly slipped to ~September 2026 [WC] — and a super-emitter rapid-reaction mechanism by the same date (Art. 31), under which it can formally request remediation information from any country. Member-State penalty regimes (Art. 33) mostly do not exist yet [GMT26 p. 56].

**Who cannot comply:** roughly 29% of 2024 import volume came from countries with essentially no OGMP/equivalent-MRV coverage of production — Russia, Algeria, Egypt, Mexico, Peru, Equatorial Guinea — before counting US LNG sold by non-OGMP exporters Venture Global, Freeport and Sempra [M3 §1]. These supplies have no obvious route to a 2027 demonstration. Meanwhile the EU's import portfolio averages ~1% upstream methane intensity, five times the 0.2% the IEA calls achievable [GMT26 pp. 10–11].

**The fight:** a Eurogas-coordinated industry paper (20 April 2026) demands a "conditional, time-bound deferral" of Articles 28–29, acceptance of OGMP Levels 4–5, and "limited assurance" verification [EUROGAS]; an industry-commissioned Wood Mackenzie analysis claims 43% of gas imports "at risk" [IOGP]. The Regulation was excluded from the omnibus simplification package; the Commission instead circulated certification and "trace-and-claim" implementation options that "do not alter the regulation's core requirements," and stated it has "no plans to grant exemptions" [EN26; WC]. Twenty-four US lawmakers urged the EU to hold the line [EN26]. The Russia phase-out (long-term LNG banned 1 January 2027, pipeline by late 2027 [SPRU; M3 §4]) removes the least transparent supplier on the same timeline — and supplies the security-of-supply argument industry deploys against Art. 28, since replacement volumes are mostly US LNG of mixed MRV coverage [M3 §3–4].

## 3. The cheapest fixes nobody makes

The IEA's 2026 numbers: ~75% of oil-and-gas methane is abatable with existing technology, and nearly 30 Mt of upstream abatement is self-financing at 2025 prices [GMT26 pp. 8–9]. M4's country reconciliation adds three policy-relevant facts:

- **The cheap abatement sits where MRV is weakest.** Of the ten largest country opportunities, at least six (Russia, Iran, Turkmenistan, Venezuela, Algeria, Iraq) have no OGMP producer membership and no binding methane regulation [M4 §1.3]. Three of them — Algeria, Russia, Egypt — are documented EU suppliers with documented MARS non-response, flagged jointly by M2 and M3 [M4 §5].
- **Single facilities are percentage points of national totals.** Pemex's KMZ complex (~0.17 Mt/yr) was ~6% of Mexico's oil-and-gas methane; TKM_S_047 emits an estimated 87,214 t/yr; Pakistan's PAK_S_001 (persistency 1.0 — emitting on every valid satellite look) is ~3% of the national total [M4 §4; M2 rows 17, 25]. The event record is not noise on the inventory; it is a target list.
- **"No net cost" has not produced action, and in the US it mostly doesn't apply.** North America's self-financing share is below 15% because cheap domestic gas undercuts the payback [GMT26 p. 69], and that shortfall now meets a federal enforcement vacuum [M4 row 1]. The IEA's own theme is that abatement "has not caught on as widely as it could" [GMT26 p. 41, via M4 §8.1]. Economics identifies the opportunity; only enforcement and named accountability convert it — Mexico's KMZ repair happened in a country with a <15% no-net-cost share, after a notification with a name attached to it internally [M4 row 13].

## 4. Recommendations (named addressees; each checkable within 12 months)

1. **DG ENER:** publish the Article 30 methane transparency database — including the per-country list of producers/exporters and their OGMP/ZRF status (Art. 30(2)(d)) — before 1 January 2027, eliminating the slippage from the 5 February 2026 deadline [REG; WC]. *Check by 2027-06: database publicly online with country-level producer lists.*
2. **DG ENER:** activate the Article 31 rapid-reaction mechanism against the named persistent sources already in the public record — Hassi Messaoud (Sonatrach), TKM_S_024/_047 (Türkmennebit/Türkmengaz), EGY_S_055 — and publish the notifications and any replies [REG; M2 rows 1, 17, 18, 29]. No such notification appears anywhere in the M1–M4 record as of 2026-06-12. *Check: first published Art. 31 notifications.*
3. **The European Commission (Energy Commissioner's cabinet):** formally reject the 20 April 2026 "stop-the-clock" demand and the OGMP Level 4 dilution, while finalizing the certification and trace-and-claim guidance already circulated to Member States [EUROGAS; EN26]. *Check: Articles 28–29 unamended and guidance issued before 2027-01-01.*
4. **National competent authorities of the five largest importing Member States (DE, IT, ES, NL, FR):** adopt Article 33 penalty regimes — most Member States still lack them [GMT26 p. 56] — and publish aggregate statistics from the Art. 27 importer reports they have been receiving since May 2025. *Check: count of Member States with penalty regimes in force; first published Art. 27 aggregates.*
5. **UNEP IMEO:** add an operator-name field, with a stated evidence tier, to the top-50 emitters product and the plume API. M2 demonstrates this is feasible from public records alone (16 of 30 rows named, tiered A/B/U) [M2 §4]; UNEP's anonymity policy [EOGT-26] is a choice, and the 13.4% response rate is its measured result [M2 §1]. *Check: a 2026–27 top-50 release or Eye on Methane annex carrying operator names.*
6. **Global Methane Hub and EDF (as OGIM stewards):** fund the attribution pipeline M2 could not complete — a spatial join of OGIM v2.7 operator records against the IMEO and Carbon Mapper plume archives — and publish it as an open dataset. This is the single biggest improvable gap in the league table; it would name the currently unattributed US rows [M2 §6.3]. *Check: published open named-operator attribution dataset.*
7. **CATF (with the IEA/CCAC Iraq engagement) and Carbon Mapper funders:** commission targeted Tanager/GHGSat tasking over Venezuela's Orinoco belt and offshore producers — the hemisphere's largest unmeasured opportunity (~6× average intensity, 13 logged detections) [M4 row 6, §6.7] — and publicly cost the Pemex KMZ repair as the replicable MARS success template [M2 row 19]. *Check: new published Venezuela point-source records; a published KMZ case study.*

## 5. Counterarguments

**"The importer requirements are unworkable — simplify or defer."** Industry's case: Wood Mackenzie (commissioned by IOGP) puts 43% of gas imports "at risk"; the intensity-methodology delegated act is not due until August 2027; the transparency database has slipped; penalty regimes are missing [IOGP; M3 §4, §6.9]. Partial merit is conceded: January 2027 will bind less in practice than on paper [M3 §6.9]. But the remedy industry demands — deferral of Arts. 28–29 and acceptance of OGMP Level 4 [EUROGAS] — would remove the only deadline that makes measurement-reconciled (Level 5) reporting commercially relevant, precisely when ~29% of supply has no equivalence route [M3 §1]. The Commission's own answer is the right one: certification and trace-and-claim options that ease compliance mechanics "without altering core requirements," with "no plans to grant exemptions" [EN26]. Even 24 US lawmakers asked the EU not to weaken the rules [EN26]. The infeasibility argument, examined, is an argument that the Regulation is working: supplies that cannot demonstrate MRV equivalence are the regulation's target, not its collateral damage.

**"Naming operators is irresponsible under attribution uncertainty."** The objection is real: no plume dataset carries an operator field [M1 §4.4]; nine of M2's named rows rest on tier-B inference (state monopoly or concession, not facility registry); Carbon Mapper itself hedged the only US name as "most likely" Energy Transfer [M2 §6.3]. The response is methodological, not rhetorical: M2 names only with a public asset record (tier A) or a documented single-operator concession with the inference basis stated (tier B), labels everything else unattributed with reasons, and publishes every underlying URL [M2 §4]. Tiered, evidence-cited naming with correction invited is the standard practice of financial and sanctions research; the alternative — UNEP's institutional anonymity — yields a measured 13.4% operator response rate [M2 §1]. And the objection is being overtaken by law: Art. 27 already obliges importers to identify producers and exporters to EU authorities [REG]. Attribution is becoming a compliance duty, not a journalistic liberty.

**"The 2025 US rollback makes this moot — there is no enforcement hook left."** The premise is correct and must be stated plainly: the Waste Emissions Charge has been overturned, EPA compliance deadlines are deferred to January 2027, the administration seeks to delay federal methane reporting "until 2034", and MethaneSAT — the mission designed to measure basin intensities — was lost in orbit in June 2025 [GMT26 p. 68 via M4 row 1; CSIS; M1 §4.2]. None of these pre-2025 conditions can be invoked as live instruments. The conclusion is inverted, however: the rollback is the strongest argument *for* the EU lever, which is indifferent to EPA's posture. US LNG took 56.0% of EU LNG imports in 2025 [E2 via M3 §2]; Art. 28 binds those contracts at the importer, in Europe. Measured US loss rates — 2.43% of production across six regions, ~3× the EPA inventory [SHERWIN] — predate the rollback and establish the problem independently of federal reporting. The exporters outside OGMP (Venture Global, Freeport, Sempra [M3 §3]) now face exactly one binding methane constraint anywhere: Brussels. Losing it would leave zero.

## 6. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Inherited observational bias.** Every M2-derived claim ranks what satellites can see: deserts, onshore, mid-latitudes. Russia (256 plumes seen in 2025 over the world's #2 modeled emitter), Venezuela, Nigeria, Iraq and all offshore production — including Norway, 30.5% of EU imports — are under-observed [M4 §8.2; M3 §6.6]. Turkmenistan's dominance is partly retrieval-surface physics [M2 §6.1]. A clean-looking supplier may merely be invisible.
2. **Attribution residual.** Tier-B names could be wrong in detail (which Turkmen state concern; which NIOC subsidiary); the Energy Transfer attribution is third-party and hedged [M2 §6.3]. This brief's recommendations would survive any single misattribution; its credibility might not — hence recommendation 6.
3. **MRV status is not destiny — the brief's own evidence cuts both ways.** Pemex, a non-OGMP state company, responded and abated [M2 row 19]; Algeria's super-emitter count fell in 2025 without EU enforcement [GMT26 p. 4 via M3 §6.9]; the US has the most measurement on Earth and a 2% MARS response rate [EOGT-26] — more MRV does not mechanically produce mitigation [M4 §8.7]. The causal claim here is narrower: named accountability plus a lever correlates with the only documented fixes.
4. **January 2027 will underdeliver on paper obligations.** Delegated acts due August 2027, database slipped, most penalty regimes absent [M3 §6.9; GMT26 p. 56] — an enforcement-first reading of the date overstates immediate bite. The recommendations therefore target the *infrastructure* (database, penalties, first Art. 31 notifications), not assumed prosecutions.
5. **Quantities carry stacked uncertainty.** Peak rates have 40–52% errors and cannot be annualized [M2 §6.2]; the ~29% no-equivalence-route share depends on a 2026-06-11 OGMP membership snapshot and on Eurostat partner-of-consignment data that misstates molecule origin for transshipped LNG [M3 §6.1–2, §6.8]; abatement volumes mix Climate TRACE levels (~2× IEA) with IEA regional shares [M4 §8.4]. Rankings and patterns are robust; absolute tonnages are indicative.
6. **Currency.** All underlying verification is dated 2026-06-11 [M1–M4]; no new data was fetched for this synthesis. OGMP membership, the simplification fight, and the Art. 30/31 implementation status are moving targets; statements about them are as of that date.
