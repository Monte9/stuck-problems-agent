## 1. Headline findings

1. **17 named exporting countries cover 90.9% of extra-EU gas imports in 2024** (252.7 of 277.9 bcm, computed from [E1]; method in §2). The remainder hides in Eurostat's "not specified" (13.8 bcm, 4.9%) and regional aggregates — itself a transparency problem for a regulation that requires importers to identify producers.
2. **Roughly 29% of 2024 EU gas imports came from countries with essentially no OGMP 2.0 / equivalent-MRV coverage of their production** — Russia (13.6%), Algeria (14.4%, transport-side membership only), Egypt, Mexico, Peru and Equatorial Guinea (0.6% combined) (sums computed from [E1] + [OGMP]); adding the unquantified share of US LNG sold by non-OGMP exporters (Venture Global, Freeport, Sempra) pushes the true figure higher. These are precisely the supplies that cannot plausibly demonstrate Article 28 MRV equivalence by 1 January 2027.
3. **The EU's import portfolio averages ~1% upstream methane intensity** — five times the 0.2% the IEA says is achievable with existing technology ([GMT26] pp. 10–11: "around 1% for the European Union and United Kingdom").
4. **Four of the EU's named 2024 suppliers appear in the M2 super-emitter league table with documented non-response to MARS notifications**: Algeria (M2 row 1: 286 detections at Hassi Messaoud, operator feedback on 1), Russia (M2 rows 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, 15, 23, 26), Egypt (M2 row 29: zero feedback on 32 notified plumes), and the US (M2 rows 6, 8, 30; 369 MARS notifications, 9 responses). Mexico (M2 row 19, Pemex) is the counter-example: notified, responded, and the source left the top-50 by May 2026.
5. **The January 2027 obligations are under active lobbying attack but still legally intact as of this writing**: a joint industry paper of 20 April 2026 demands a "stop-the-clock" deferral of Articles 28–29 ([EUROGAS]), the Commission has floated certification and "trace-and-claim" implementation options but states it has "no plans to grant exemptions" ([EN26]), and 24 US lawmakers have urged the EU **not** to weaken the rules ([EN26]).

## 2. Method for volumes and coverage

Eurostat `nrg_ti_gas`, annual 2024, reporter EU27_2020, unit mio m³ ([E1]). "Total gas" = `G3000` (includes LNG); LNG subset = `G3200`; pipeline = G3000 − G3200. Reported TOTAL across all partners is 330,639 mio m³; intra-EU partner flows (re-exports between member states) sum to 52,696 mio m³; **extra-EU imports = 277,944 mio m³ ≈ 277.9 bcm**, the denominator used for all shares. The 17 named country rows sum to 252,676 mio m³ = **90.91% coverage** (done-criterion threshold: 90%). Residual: "not specified" 13,758 (4.95%), "other European countries" aggregate 7,804 (2.81%), "other Near & Middle East" 2,541 (0.91%), remaining small partners ~1,165 (0.42%). These are gross flows on a partner-of-consignment basis — see Limitations §1–2.

2025 figures shown per row are from Eurostat's news release of 25 March 2026 ([E2]) and Statistics Explained ([E3]): in 2025 Norway took 52.1% of gaseous-state imports, Algeria 17.4%, Russia 10.4%; for LNG the US took 56.0%, Russia 13.9%, Qatar 8.9%, Algeria 6.6%; LNG volume rose 24.4% while pipeline volume fell 5.3% vs 2024.

Intensity basis labels: **measured** (peer-reviewed measurement campaigns), **modeled/measurement-informed** (IEA GMT 2026: "estimates based on measured, satellite, and inferred data" — [GMT26], source notes), or **none public**. The IEA publishes per-country intensities only as charts (kg CH₄/GJ) and behind a free-login CSV; numeric per-country values are therefore quoted here only where the report text states them.

## 4. The EU Methane Regulation: what actually binds importers, and the state of the fight

All article citations: Regulation (EU) 2024/1787 of 13 June 2024, in force 4 August 2024, full text fetched from EUR-Lex this session ([REG]).

**Already binding:**
- **Art. 27(1):** since **5 May 2025** (then by 31 May annually), importers must report Annex IX information — producer/exporter identity, country of production, MRV and LDAR practices — to national competent authorities; Member States forward it to the Commission (Art. 27(2)). The IEA confirms this took effect in May 2025 ([GMT26] pp. 26, 56).
- **Art. 30:** Commission must establish the **methane transparency database by 5 February 2026** — including, per country, a list of producers/exporters to the EU and whether they belong to OGMP or ZRF (Art. 30(2)(d)(i)). Reported launch has slipped to ~September 2026 ([WC]).
- **Art. 31:** global methane monitoring tool by **5 August 2026**; super-emitter **rapid reaction mechanism by 5 February 2026**, under which the Commission notifies events to any country "within or outside the Union" and may request information on remediation — a treaty-grade version of MARS notification, aimed at exactly the non-response pattern documented in M2.
- **Art. 33:** Member States must have **penalties** (fines clawing back economic benefit; periodic penalty payments) — yet "few Member States have set up penalty regimes to date" ([GMT26] p. 56) and at least nine were behind on naming competent authorities ([CSIS]).

**Binding 1 January 2027 (the importer cliff):**
- **Art. 28(1):** importers must **demonstrate** that contracts concluded or renewed on/after 4 August 2024 cover only oil/gas/coal "subject to monitoring, reporting and verification measures applied at the level of the producer that are equivalent to those set out in this Regulation."
- **Art. 28(2):** for pre-4-Aug-2024 contracts, importers must show "all reasonable efforts" toward equivalence and justify failures annually.
- **Art. 28(5):** equivalence = independent third-party verification plus producer-level quantification equivalent to Art. 12 — **or OGMP 2.0 Level 5 reporting** — or a third-country MRV framework the Commission deems equivalent. This makes the OGMP column in §3 the de facto compliance map: supplies from non-OGMP, non-regulated producers (Russia — moot after the phase-out; Algeria's Sonatrach; Egypt's EGAS; Pemex; Venture Global/Freeport volumes) have no obvious route to a 2027 demonstration.
- Later steps: **Art. 29(1)** — annual **methane-intensity reporting** by importers from **5 August 2028** (methodology delegated act due 5 Aug 2027); **Art. 29(2)** — **maximum methane intensity values** for new contracts from **5 August 2030**.

**The "simplification" fight (current state, dated):**
- 20 April 2026: Eurogas-coordinated joint industry recommendations demand "a conditional, time-bound deferral of Articles 28 & 29" ("stop-the-clock"), acceptance of OGMP 2.0 **Levels 4–5** (not just 5) as equivalence, "limited assurance" verification, and voluntary certification schemes where producers can't be identified ([EUROGAS]). An industry-commissioned Wood Mackenzie analysis circulated by IOGP Europe claims up to 43% of EU gas imports (87% of crude) are "at risk" from 2027 ([IOGP] — industry-commissioned figure, not independently verified).
- The Methane Regulation was **not** included in the Commission's omnibus simplification package; instead the Commission circulated implementation options to Member States — third-party site-level certification, and a "trace-and-claim" digital-ID system — which "do not alter the regulation's core requirements" ([EN26], 6 Feb 2026; [WC]). EU energy ministers have endorsed certification and trace-and-claim as workable compliance routes ([GMT26] p. 26).
- US pressure runs both directions: the US Energy Secretary pressed Brussels to delay and make the rule "more U.S.-friendly" (Sept 2025, via [CSIS]), while 24 US lawmakers urged the EU in February 2026 to keep the rules and **not** exempt US operators if domestic standards lack accuracy or enforcement; Commission spokesperson: "no plans to grant exemptions," "standing by the law's ambition" ([EN26]).
- Eleven European oil and gas players have separately called for postponement of the equivalence requirements (Agence Europe headline, paywalled — listed for completeness, content not verified [AGE]).
- Interaction with the Russia phase-out: the stepwise ban (short-term LNG from 25 Apr 2026; long-term LNG 1 Jan 2027; long-term pipeline by 30 Sep–1 Nov 2027, penalties up to €40m or 3.5% of worldwide turnover) removes the EU's least-transparent supplier on the same timeline as Art. 28 — strengthening the security-of-supply argument industry uses against the methane rules, since replacement volumes are mostly US LNG with mixed MRV coverage (§3) ([SPRU], [CONS], [EP]).

## 6. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **"Partner country" is not molecule origin.** Eurostat records the reported country of origin/consignment; transshipped and re-exported LNG, swap deals, and confidential flows distort attribution (M1 gap #6). 4.95% of 2024 extra-EU imports are literally "not specified" [E1] — and an EU import standard that depends on producer-level attribution inherits exactly this gap. The 90.91% coverage figure is therefore coverage *of what Eurostat can name*.
2. **The monthly/annual discrepancy is direct evidence of the attribution problem.** The monthly 2025 dataset attributes only 0.8 bcm to Azerbaijan and 12.7 bcm to Türkiye, because TAP-routed Azeri gas is booked at the Türkiye border; the annual dataset corrects this (12.3 bcm Azerbaijan, 1.1 bcm Türkiye in 2024) ([E1]/[E1m], computed this session). Any 2025 country statements here rely on Eurostat's own published shares [E2], not on my monthly aggregation. A "trace-and-claim" compliance system will have to beat the EU's own statistical office at this game.
3. **Russian pipeline volumes look low on a partner basis.** Eurostat's 2024 Russia figure (37.8 bcm total, 17.8 pipeline) is below route-based trackers' estimates of Russian pipeline arrivals (Ukraine-transit + TurkStream); part of the gap plausibly sits in the 13.8 bcm "not specified" [speculative — consistent in magnitude but not decomposable from public data]. I did not adjust the numbers; the table states Eurostat as published.
4. **Gross vs net.** These are gross physical imports per reporter, summed across EU27; they include volumes later re-exported (e.g., via UK/Belgium) and so exceed net EU consumption-basis imports. Shares are robust to this; absolute "EU dependence" claims should use net figures.
5. **Intensity values are mostly not measured.** Only the US has a peer-reviewed national-scale measurement [SHERWIN] — and it is from 2019–2021 campaigns, predating both the EPA rules and their partial rollback; current US intensity is genuinely uncertain in both directions. IEA per-country intensities are "measurement-informed" models published as charts; numeric values sit behind a free-account CSV I could not retrieve by automation (iea.org 403s all clients — M1 gap #5). Where the report text states only a multiple ("3–5×"), the table quotes exactly that and nothing more.
6. **Satellite visibility is anti-correlated with EU supply share.** Norway (30.5% of imports, offshore, high-latitude) is nearly unobservable to the M2 detection stack, while Algeria and Egypt (desert) are maximally observable. The M2 cross-reference column therefore over-indexes desert exporters. Norway's "clean" rating rests on IEA inventory work and national reporting, not on independent satellite verification — a structural gap the Art. 31 monitoring tool will inherit.
7. **OGMP membership ≠ MRV quality.** Art. 28(5) requires OGMP **Level 5** (measurement-reconciled), not membership. This session verified membership only; per-company reporting levels live in IMEO's *An Eye on Methane* annex (PDF behind a Cloudflare challenge — M2 source register) and were not extracted. Industry is lobbying to soften exactly this distinction (L4–5 acceptance, [EUROGAS]). A row marked "member" may still fail equivalence in 2027.
8. **Membership lists shift.** The OGMP capture is a 2026-06-11 snapshot of a paginated page; companies join and exit (and exits are not announced). Absence claims (Sonatrach, Gazprom, Pemex, QatarEnergy was absent until recently — joined per the current list) are as of this date only.
9. **Counter-evidence to the dossier's framing.** (a) The Pemex case (M2 row 19) shows a non-OGMP, state-owned supplier responding to MARS and abating — MRV-membership status is not destiny. (b) The IEA notes *improving* trends in two of the dossier's problem countries (fewer super-emitter detections in Algeria in 2025; [GMT26] p. 4). (c) The industry claim that 2027 equivalence is infeasible is partly grounded: the methodology delegated acts (Art. 29(4)) are not due until Aug 2027, the transparency database has slipped ([WC]), and most Member States lack penalty regimes ([GMT26] p. 56) — so an enforcement-first reading of January 2027 overstates what will actually bind in practice. (d) Wood Mackenzie's "43% of imports at risk" is industry-commissioned and unverified, but the OGMP coverage analysis in §1 (~29% of 2024 imports with no plausible equivalence route, before counting non-OGMP US LNG sellers) points in the same direction, suggesting the compliance gap is real even if the magnitude and framing differ.
10. **2025 full-year partner detail is not yet authoritative.** Annual `nrg_ti_gas` ends at 2024; 2025 statements rest on Eurostat's news-release shares [E2][E3] and norskpetroleum [N1]. The table's volume backbone is 2024, which the spec permits ("2024 and/or 2025").
