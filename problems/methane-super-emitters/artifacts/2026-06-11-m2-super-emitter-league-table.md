## 1. Headline findings

1. **MARS notification status is now queryable per plume.** Of 11,607 O&G plumes detected 2024-01-01 or later in the IMEO archive, 5,864 (50.5%) carry `notified=true`, but only 788 (13.4% of notified plumes) carry `feedbackOperator=true` (computed from the API dump, 2026-06-11). This is plume-level, consistent with UNEP's notification-level statistics: 1,200 notifications and 15 replies (1%) in 2024; 2,203 notifications and ~12% response in 2025 ([Eye on Global Transparency, 2024-11-15](https://eyeonglobaltransparency.net/2024/11/15/unep-reports-one-percent-reply-rate-to-methane-emission-notifications/); [Eye on Global Transparency, 2026-04-23](https://eyeonglobaltransparency.net/2026/04/23/unep-describes-methane-mitigation-successes-transparency-issues-remain/)).
2. **UNEP still refuses to name operators.** Its top-50 list gives coordinates, source type, and response status, but "UNEP does not identify the responsible party by name" ([Eye on Global Transparency, 2026-04-23](https://eyeonglobaltransparency.net/2026/04/23/unep-describes-methane-mitigation-successes-transparency-issues-remain/)). The naming below is therefore this artifact's added value, done by matching coordinates to public field/operator records (mostly [Global Energy Monitor wiki](https://www.gem.wiki)) and to Carbon Mapper's named 2025 analysis.
3. **The worst documented pattern is "notified, acknowledged, never fixed."** The Goturdepe complex (Türkmennebit, Turkmenistan) logged ≈320 plume detections since January 2024, 260 of them notified, with operator feedback recorded on 146 — and was still emitting in May 2026 (IMEO API; IMEO top-50 IDs TKM_S_029, TKM_S_203). Iran is the mirror image: the Marun–Karanj–Parsi belt logged 355 detections, 306 notified, **zero** operator feedback.
4. **The United States is among the least responsive jurisdictions on record:** 369 MARS notifications, 9 recorded responses ([Eye on Global Transparency, 2026-04-23](https://eyeonglobaltransparency.net/2026/04/23/unep-describes-methane-mitigation-successes-transparency-issues-remain/)).

## 4. Methodology

**Ranking basis.** Primary table: peak observed single-overpass emission rate during 2024-01-01 → 2026-06-11 (both one-off events and persistent sources qualify; persistence is reported in its own column). Secondary table (Section 3): IMEO's own persistence-weighted half-year average — i.e., the ranking uses **both** rate and persistence, presented as two orderings rather than a blended score, because blending single-overpass peaks with annualized means would manufacture false precision.

**Pipeline.** (1) Full IMEO plume archive (24,005 records) fetched 2026-06-11 from `methanedata.unep.org/api/plumes`; filtered to `sector="Oil and Gas"`, `tileDate ≥ 2024-01-01`, non-null flux (11,607 plumes). (2) Carbon Mapper plumes fetched via documented API, filters: `plume_gas=CH4`, `sectors=1B2`, `qualities=good`, datetime 2024-01-01→2026-06-11 (2,969 plumes). (3) Plumes grouped into sources on a 0.05° grid (~5 km), with manual merge of adjacent cells for named complexes (stated per row, e.g. "within ~10 km"). (4) IMEO top-50 snapshots joined by coordinates to provide official source IDs, types, annualized estimates, and notification/feedback flags. (5) Cross-checks between IMEO and Carbon Mapper records at the same coordinates noted per row (e.g., rows 3, 19, 26, 27).

**Attribution evidence standard.** A row names an operator only with: (tier A) a public field/asset record at the coordinates naming the operator — all such citations here are Global Energy Monitor wiki pages with their own underlying references, verified by fetch this session; or (tier B) a documented single-operator concession or state monopoly covering the location, or a published third-party attribution (Carbon Mapper's named 2025 analysis), with the inference basis stated in the row. Everything else is "Unattributed" with a one-line reason. Distances between plume coordinates and named fields were sanity-checked against the GEM record locations; tier-B rows state residual ambiguity. No row's operator rests solely on this author's map-reading: every A/B row carries at least one external URL.

**MARS columns.** "MARS-notified" and "Response status" come from per-plume `notified`/`notificationDate`/`feedbackOperator`/`feedbackGovernment` fields in the IMEO API and the per-source `Plumes Notified (Y/N)` / `Feedback Received (Y/N)` columns of the official top-50 file — both published by UNEP IMEO. ## 6. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Detection bias by satellite coverage.** This table ranks what satellites can see, not what exists. TROPOMI needs cloud-free, low-albedo-contrast, mostly onshore scenes; EMIT covers only ±54° latitude (M1, CMR metadata); offshore, equatorial-cloudy, and high-latitude basins (most of offshore Norway, UK, much of Russia above ~60°N except where Tanager looked) are systematically under-sampled. Turkmenistan's dominance (3,551 of 11,607 archive plumes) is partly real and partly because the Karakum desert is a near-ideal retrieval surface. A country absent from this table is not thereby clean — and IMEO's own blog says exactly this about its top-50 ([BLOG]).
2. **Rate-estimate uncertainty is large and rank-order near the top is not robust.** Stated 1σ uncertainties on the top rows run 40–52% (e.g., row 1: 649 ± 339 t/h). Rows 1–8 are statistically indistinguishable in places; a peak-rate ranking is indicative, not a measurement. Single-overpass peaks (rows 2, 5–8, 12) also capture transient blowdowns that may have lasted hours — they are not comparable to annualized tonnage, which is why Section 3 exists. The two bases would produce very different "worst" lists.
3. **Ownership/attribution ambiguity.** Tier-B rows (3, 4, 11, 17, 18, 24, 27, 28, 30) rest on monopoly/concession inference or a third party's "most likely" attribution, not a facility-level registry match. Specific risks: row 3's facility type is unknown (production vs. transmission changes which Turkmen state concern is responsible); row 24 spans three adjacent NIOC subsidiaries; row 30's "Energy Transfer" is Carbon Mapper's hedged attribution ("one of two facilities"), reported through press, not a primary Carbon Mapper document fetched this session. OGIM v2.7 (M1 #5) was **not** spatially joined here — its multi-GB GeoPackage exceeded session capacity — so North American events that OGIM could have named (rows 6, 8) remain unattributed. That is the single biggest improvable gap in this artifact.
4. **VIIRS-derived methane rates (rows 3, 4) are unusual.** VIIRS is not a methane spectrometer; IMEO publishes these records (presumably combustion/blowout-event quantifications) without method documentation in the API, and I did not locate their technical basis this session. The top-50 documentation PDF referenced by IMEO was not machine-readable. Treat those two rates as IMEO-published but method-unverified [speculative as to method, not as to publication].
5. **Quality flags.** Row 6 (Fort Worth, 439 t/h over a city center) is plausibly a retrieval artifact or an aggregate urban signal mis-tagged as a point source; it is included because IMEO publishes it with an O&G sector tag, and flagged because it would otherwise be the largest US detection ever published. Rows 1 and 6 share the same date (2025-11-08), raising the possibility of a TROPOMI processing anomaly that day [speculative].
6. **"Feedback recorded" ≠ mitigation.** UNEP logged operator feedback on 146 Goturdepe plumes and 136 Esenguly plumes while both kept emitting into 2026 — the response columns measure paperwork, not repairs. Conversely, absence of feedback in the API may undercount informal responses (UNEP's own mitigation-case count, 41, exceeds what the per-plume flags would suggest). Per-notification documents remain undisclosed by UNEP ([EOGT-26]), so response status here cannot be audited beyond the published flags.
7. **Archive completeness and stability.** The IMEO plume API is undocumented and was snapshotted once (2026-06-11); its archive ends 2026-05-11, so the most recent month is missing, and IMEO continually revises records (`lastUpdate` fields show 2026-02 reprocessing of 2023 plumes). The Carbon Mapper query used `qualities=good` only — relaxing it adds plumes but also noise. GHGSat's commercial archive and the lost MethaneSAT mission (M1 gaps #2, #3) are absent entirely; events seen only by those systems are missing from this table.
8. **License constraint.** The top-50 file is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial). Downstream M3–M5 artifacts and any published report must carry UNEP IMEO credit and stay non-commercial, or rebuild the equivalent ranking from the raw plume API (which carries no stated license at all — itself a legal ambiguity).
