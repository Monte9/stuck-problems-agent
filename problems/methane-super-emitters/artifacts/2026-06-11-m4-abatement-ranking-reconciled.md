## 1. Headline findings

1. **The IEA's 2026 numbers say roughly three-quarters of oil-and-gas methane is abatable with existing technology and ~30 Mt of upstream abatement pays for itself** at 2025 energy prices ([GMT26] pp. 8–9: ~85 Mt of fossil methane abatable, >50 Mt upstream O&G, "Nearly 30 Mt of upstream oil and gas emissions could be abated at no net cost"). Where that potential sits, by country, is exactly what the login-walled database hides — this artifact reconstructs the country ranking from open sources and documents every join.
2. **The observational record and the model agree on the worst offenders but disagree on visibility.** Turkmenistan (IEA: intensity "one of the highest in the world", >1/3 of all MARS O&G observations — [GMT26] p. 61) and Iran (>730 satellite-detected O&G events in 2025 — [GMT26] p. 65) are extreme in both views. Russia and Venezuela are the opposite case: top-3 model emitters that satellites largely cannot see (256 plumes over Russia in 2025; Venezuelan production cloud-covered/offshore — [GMT26] pp. 58, 61).
3. **The cheapest abatement is concentrated where MRV is weakest.** Of the ten largest opportunities ranked below, at least six (Russia, Iran, Turkmenistan, Venezuela, Algeria, Iraq) sit in countries with no OGMP 2.0 producer membership and no binding methane regulation (M3 table; [GMT26] pp. 61–67). Three of these — **Algeria, Russia, Egypt** — are documented EU suppliers with documented MARS non-response (flags in §5).
4. **North America is the structural exception to "no net cost":** the IEA puts the no-net-cost share of its abatement potential below 15% because low domestic gas prices make captured gas worth less than the fix ([GMT26] p. 69) — a warning against assuming the 35 Mt global figure is uniformly self-financing.
5. **Independent estimates disagree on levels by ~2×.** Climate TRACE's open per-country series — used here for ranking because it is the only complete open one — totals 154.7 Mt CH4 for global O&G production+transport in 2024 ([CT]), against the IEA's ~78 Mt for oil + gas operations in 2025 ([GMT26] p. 15: oil ~44 Mt, gas ~34 Mt). Rankings below are robust to this; absolute Mt cells are not, and are labeled by source throughout.

## 2. Methodology (read before the table)

**Why two sources.** The spec asks for the IEA Methane Abatement Model's open data. What is actually open of it (verified this session): the report PDF with regional totals and percentage statements ([GMT26]), and the model code + assumption CSVs ([GITLAB]). The per-country emissions/abatement CSVs require an iea.org login that automation cannot complete (M1 gap #5, re-verified). The only complete, open, per-country O&G methane series is Climate TRACE ([CT]) — an M1-recommended M4 source. This artifact therefore: (a) **ranks** countries by Climate TRACE 2024 O&G CH4; (b) **corroborates** the ordering against the IEA's own within-region country ordering (the GMT26 regional charts list countries in descending emissions order, e.g. Eurasia: "Russia, Turkmenistan, Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, Azerbaijan" — [GMT26] p. 62); (c) **anchors levels** to IEA statements wherever the report text states a country number; and (d) flags every conflict in the divergence column.

**Derived cells (marked †).** Country-level abatable and no-net-cost volumes are first-order derivations, not IEA model outputs:
- *Total abatable†* = country O&G emissions × **75%** (IEA: "three-quarters of emissions from oil and gas" abatable with existing technology — [GMT26] p. 8).
- *No-net-cost†* = the IEA's regional no-net-cost share applied to the country: Eurasia **40% of O&G emissions** avoidable at measures that pay for themselves ([GMT26] p. 62); China **>90% of O&G reductions** ([GMT26] p. 61); MENA **~60% of reductions** ([GMT26] p. 67); North America **<15% of reductions** ([GMT26] p. 69); Central & South America **~70% of reductions** ([GMT26] p. 59); South & Southeast Asia **60% of reductions** ([GMT26] p. 72); sub-Saharan Africa **~70% of reductions** ([GMT26] p. 73–74).
Both inputs of every † cell are cited; the stacking risk (Climate TRACE level × IEA share) is addressed in Limitations §4. Where the IEA text states a country-level emissions figure, an IEA-anchored alternate is shown in the same cell.

**M2 columns.** "M2-observed events" = number of M2 league-table rows for the country (rows 1–30), total detections those rows report, and the sum of their peak observed rates in t CH4/h. Summed peaks are an **activity indicator, not a flux**: single-overpass peaks cannot be annualized (Limitations §3).

**Ranking basis.** Descending Climate TRACE 2024 O&G (production+transport) CH4. 2024 used rather than 2025 because the TRACE 2025 series shows a globally implausible 37% drop (97.1 vs 154.7 Mt — [CT]), consistent with an incomplete estimate year (Limitations §5).

## 4. What the two views disagree about (synthesis)

- **Satellites overweight deserts; models overweight production volume.** Turkmenistan, Syria, Yemen, Thailand and Pakistan all look worse in the observational record than their model share; Russia, Venezuela, Nigeria and Iraq all look worse in the model than in M2. Both can be right at once: the first group has visible persistent point sources, the second has invisible or non-plume (flaring slip, offshore, high-latitude) emissions.
- **The "no-net-cost" framing holds best exactly where governance is worst.** The Eurasia/MENA no-net-cost shares (40–60%) attach to countries with no OGMP producers and no penalty regimes; the US, with the deepest measurement record, has the *lowest* self-financing share (<15%) and a paused federal enforcement regime ([GMT26] pp. 62, 67–69). Economics alone will not clear this market — which is the policy point for M5.
- **One observed mega-source can be a material share of a national total.** Pemex's KMZ complex (~0.17 Mt/yr, M2 row 19), TKM_S_047 (~0.09 Mt/yr), PAK_S_001 (~0.03 Mt/yr): the event record is not noise on top of the model — at country scale, single facilities are percentage points.

## 5. Flags: high observed super-emitter activity + weak/no MRV (each supported by both M2 and M3)

1. **Algeria.** Observation: M2 row 1 — the league table's largest peak event (Hassi Messaoud, 649 ± 339 t/h, 286 detections, operator feedback on 1 of 286, still active 2026). MRV: M3 table — **Sonatrach is not an OGMP 2.0 member** (absent from the full member-list capture [M3, OGMP]); Algeria supplies 14.4% of EU gas imports with no plausible Art. 28 equivalence route (M3 §1, §4).
2. **Russia.** Observation: M2 rows 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, 15, 23, 26 — eight league entries including a 542 t/h event notified 2025-09-22 with no feedback. MRV: M3 table — **no Russian company on the OGMP list** (Gazprom, Novatek absent); the only Eurasian producer outside the Global Methane Pledge ([M3, GMT26] p. 61). (EU phase-out makes the import lever moot by 2027; the residual lever is buyer pressure in Asia — see M5.)
3. **Egypt.** Observation: M2 row 29 — EGY_S_055, 88 detections in 8 months, 32 notified, zero feedback; plus >80 country-wide events in 2025 ([GMT26] p. 65). MRV: M3 table — **no Egyptian state company (EGAS/EGPC) on the OGMP list**; only foreign shareholders of Idku are members [M3].
4. *(Beyond the M3 EU-supplier set, supported by M2 + GMT26 instead:)* **Turkmenistan** — 7 league rows, a third of all MARS O&G observations, and no Turkmen company among the Eurasian OGMP/OGDC participants the IEA lists (SOCAR, KazMunayGas, Uzbekneftegaz — [GMT26] p. 62); and **Iran** — 3 league rows, >730 events in 2025, zero recorded feedback on 306 notified plumes, no OGMP presence, sanctions-constrained ([GMT26] pp. 65–67; M2 rows 4, 20, 24).

## 6. Top 10 highest-leverage country actions

1. **Turkmenistan — fix the named five.** TKM_S_024, _047, _022, _029/_203, _367 (M2 §3) jointly emit an estimated ~0.25 Mt/yr from a handful of flares/gas-disposal units. Feedback is already recorded on two of them — the diplomatic channel exists (UNEP/EU Methane Abatement Partnership Roadmap, [GMT26] p. 64). Cheapest verified tonnes on the planet [derived from M2 §3 estimates].
2. **Russia — rapid-response where satellites can see.** The Eurasia LDAR case (80% of savings no-net-cost, [GMT26] p. 63) is unenforceable from outside, but Tanager/EMIT coverage of specific assets (Urengoy, M2 row 26) enables named-asset pressure via Asian buyers inheriting EU-style MRV expectations (M3 §4, CLEAN initiative [GMT26] p. 74).
3. **Iran — treat as flaring infrastructure, not LDAR.** 40% of global flaring sits in Iran/Iraq/Algeria with flares <20 km from pipelines ([GMT26] p. 67). Sanctions carve-outs for methane-abatement equipment are the binding constraint worth testing; the MARS channel is already saturated and ignored (M2 row 24).
4. **Algeria — the EU's strongest single lever.** Art. 28 equivalence pressure on Sonatrach (14.4% of EU imports, no OGMP membership, M3) attaches a contract value to fixing Hassi Messaoud (M2 row 1). No other country pairs this event record with this much EU exposure.
5. **Iraq — pipeline-tie-in projects over notifications.** No M2 events to escalate; the opportunity is the IEA/CATF/CCAC engagement channel ([GMT26] p. 66) funding gas-capture tie-ins.
6. **United States — don't assume self-financing.** <15% no-net-cost ([GMT26] p. 69) plus deferred federal compliance to Jan 2027 ([GMT26] p. 68) means abatement runs through state rules, voluntary OGMP/EU-export exposure (Cheniere is a member; Venture Global/Freeport are not — M3), and the EU's Art. 28 on LNG sellers.
7. **Venezuela — buy observation before abatement.** The largest unmeasured opportunity in the hemisphere (~6× intensity, [GMT26] p. 57); targeted tasking (Tanager, GHGSat) over the Orinoco belt would convert a model claim into named facilities, as Carbon Mapper did for Turkmenistan.
8. **Egypt — burn pits, with EU leverage.** Same Art. 28 logic as Algeria at smaller scale; the OGCI/GHGSat burn-pit finding gives a specific engineering target ([GMT26] p. 65; M2 row 29).
9. **Mexico — bank and replicate the KMZ precedent.** Pemex responded and the source left the top-50 (M2 row 19). Document the repair, cost it publicly, and use it as the template MARS success story for other state companies.
10. **China — let it self-finance.** >90% of O&G reductions at no net cost ([GMT26] p. 61) needs no external money, only inclusion of O&G methane in the national action plan's enforcement (currently coal-focused).

## 8. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **IEA model vintage and assumptions.** [GMT26] is the April 2026 edition with 2025 emission estimates; no-net-cost volumes are explicitly conditional on **average 2025 energy prices** ([GMT26] p. 8) and the IEA itself notes 2026 prices change the economics. The abatement model's technology-cost assumptions are public ([GITLAB]) but its country-level emissions inputs are not, so nothing here re-runs the model; regional shares are taken as published. "No net cost to producers" is a private-cost concept — it has empirically not produced action (the report's own theme, e.g. "Methane abatement has not caught on as widely as it could", [GMT26] p. 41), so the column measures opportunity, not likelihood.
2. **Satellite coverage bias (inherited from M2).** The M2-observed columns over-represent desert, onshore, mid-latitude countries and under-represent Russia (256 plumes in 2025 over the world's #2 modeled emitter), Venezuela, Nigeria, Norway and all offshore production ([GMT26] pp. 58, 61; M2 §6.1). Divergence notes labeled "observation blind/poor" mean exactly that: the model is unaudited there, not wrong.
3. **Event rates vs annualized totals are different physical quantities.** Σpeak rates (t/h) sum instantaneous single-overpass estimates with 40–52% uncertainties (M2 §6.2); they cannot be integrated to Mt/yr and are presented only as an activity indicator. Where annualized source estimates exist (IMEO top-50, e.g. 165,626 t/yr for MEX_S_003), those are used for the "share of national total" observations instead.
4. **The † columns stack two sources.** Total-abatable† applies the IEA's *global* 75% O&G share, and no-net-cost† applies *regional* shares, to *Climate TRACE* country levels. Three distortions follow: (a) CT levels run ~2× IEA globally (154.7 vs ~78 Mt — preamble; the gap is extreme for the US: CT 39.9 Mt vs EPA-inventory-anchored values 3–6× lower even after Sherwin's ×3 correction [M3, SHERWIN]), so absolute † values overstate Mt relative to the IEA's own model — the summed no-net-cost† across these 21 countries (~41 Mt) exceeds the IEA's global ~30 Mt upstream figure, a direct artifact of CT's higher levels; (b) regional shares applied to individual countries erase intra-regional variation (Iraq's infrastructure constraint, Iran's sanctions — both flagged in-row); (c) some regional no-net-cost shares are for total fossil including coal (C&SA, S/SE Asia, SSA), applied here to O&G-only values. The **ranking** is the deliverable; the † magnitudes are first-order context. Where IEA text states a country number, the IEA-anchored alternate is shown (Russia, China, Venezuela) and is the better level estimate.
5. **Climate TRACE's 2025 series was rejected** for showing a globally implausible −37% year change (97.1 vs 154.7 Mt, [CT]) — almost certainly an incomplete estimate year rather than a real decline; 2024 is used. TRACE values are modeled estimates (M1 §4), and its US O&G figure in particular should be treated as an upper envelope, not a measurement.
6. **Ordinal evidence from chart ordering is weak.** The claim that GMT26 regional charts order countries by descending emissions is an inference from their consistent arrangement (Russia→Azerbaijan; Iran→UAE; US→Mexico; Venezuela→Ecuador), not a stated convention [speculative as to convention; the orderings themselves are verbatim from the chart labels].
7. **Counter-evidence to this artifact's framing.** (a) Mexico (row 13) shows a weak-MRV state company abating after notification — observed activity + weak MRV does not always equal inaction, which cuts against reading §5 flags as destiny. (b) Algeria's 2025 event count fell ([GMT26] p. 4 via M3) — high-leverage targets can improve without EU enforcement. (c) The US has the most measurement of any country and still ranks #1 in CT — more MRV does not mechanically lower emissions, which complicates the MRV-first theory of change underlying M3/M5. (d) Two conflict states (Syria, Yemen) rank in the table on observational salience, but "no-net-cost abatement" is close to meaningless where there is no functioning operator to capture the gas value — those rows are accountability targets, not market opportunities.
8. **License note.** [GMT26] is CC BY 4.0 and [CT] is CC BY 4.0 — both publishable with attribution; IMEO top-50-derived numbers inherited via M2 remain CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial), as flagged in M2 §6.8.
