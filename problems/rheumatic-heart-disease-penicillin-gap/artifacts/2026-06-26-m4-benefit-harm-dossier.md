# M4 — Benefit-harm dossier: keeping secondary prophylaxis running after the injection-reaction advisory

**Problem:** Rheumatic heart disease — the penicillin gap
**Milestone:** M4 (ministry-facing benefit-harm dossier inverting the injection-reaction scare)
**Date:** 2026-06-26
**Author:** generator (autonomous loop)

## What this artifact is

A plain-language decision memo for a non-specialist health official (a program manager or ministry desk officer) who has heard that "people are dying from the penicillin injection" and is wondering whether to suspend a rheumatic-heart-disease (RHD) secondary-prophylaxis register. It puts the *documented* harm — exactly what the 2022 American Heart Association (AHA) presidential advisory reported — side by side with the *documented* benefit from the M3 cost-of-inaction model, shows the number-needed-to-treat versus number-needed-to-harm arithmetic so the asymmetry is legible, and lists the concrete administration practices that manage the small residual risk. **Bottom line stated up front: the AHA advisory did NOT say "stop prophylaxis." It said the fatal reactions are vanishingly rare, are concentrated in a specific, identifiable group (patients with severe/advanced valvular RHD), and that prophylaxis should CONTINUE for everyone else — with simple precautions, and with oral penicillin offered as an alternative to the highest-risk subgroup.** Every number below carries an inline source.

## (a) The documented harm — what the 2022 AHA advisory ACTUALLY reported

**The source.** Sanyahumbi A, Sable C, Karthikeyan G, et al., on behalf of the AHA. *"Penicillin Reactions in Patients With Severe Rheumatic Heart Disease: A Presidential Advisory From the American Heart Association."* *Journal of the American Heart Association (JAHA)* 2022;11(5):e024517, published online 20 January 2022. DOI 10.1161/JAHA.121.024517 ([PubMed 35049336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35049336/); [JAHA listing](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.121.024517)). Note the **title itself** — "in Patients With *Severe* Rheumatic Heart Disease." The advisory is narrowly about a high-risk subgroup, not penicillin in general.

**What reaction it describes — and the key correction.** Historically, deaths in the minutes-to-hours after a benzathine penicillin G (BPG) injection were assumed to be **anaphylaxis** (a classic allergic reaction: cough, respiratory distress, fast heart rate, somewhat delayed onset). The advisory's central new claim is that a growing number of these deaths **do NOT fit anaphylaxis**. Instead they look like **cardiovascular collapse** — immediate low blood pressure, slow heart rate, and fainting (a vasovagal/cardiovascular reaction) — in hearts that are already failing. In other words, the heart that is already badly damaged cannot tolerate the transient circulatory stress of the injection ([AHA advisory via Healio, 27 Jan 2022](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20220127/aha-advisory-oral-penicillin-safer-than-injectable-for-highrisk-rheumatic-heart-disease); [The Cardiology Advisor summary](https://www.thecardiologyadvisor.com/news/aha-guideline-advisory-penicillin-prophylaxis-rheumatic-heart-disease-rhd/)). This is supported by the underlying case series: of severe events reviewed, only ~3 met formal (Brighton Level 1) anaphylaxis criteria — "anaphylaxis is not a major cause of adverse reactions to BPG" ([Birru et al., *PLOS One* 2025, PMC12057857](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12057857/); precursor case analysis [de Dassel et al., *J Am Heart Assoc* 2019, PMC6590992](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590992/)).

**The population it flagged as higher-risk.** Explicitly, patients with **severe / advanced valvular RHD**: severe mitral stenosis, severe aortic stenosis or aortic insufficiency, reduced left-ventricular systolic function, or active heart-failure symptoms — *including asymptomatic patients whose echocardiogram already shows severe valve disease* ([JAHA 2022, via PubMed 35049336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35049336/); [Healio 2022](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20220127/aha-advisory-oral-penicillin-safer-than-injectable-for-highrisk-rheumatic-heart-disease)). The corroborating 2025 meta-analysis is blunt: **"All fatal reactions occurred in patients with severe RHD"** ([Birru et al., *PLOS One* 2025, PMC12057857](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12057857/)).

**The estimated incidence (how rare).** Two citable, quantified anchors:

- **2025 systematic review & meta-analysis** (9 studies; 11,587 participants; **154,760 BPG injections**): pooled **severe** adverse reactions = **1.1 per 10,000 injections** (95% CI 0.4–2.2), or 9.7 per 10,000 patients. **Fatal** reactions = **6 in total = 0.05% of patients (6 / 11,587)** — and all 6 were in severe-RHD patients ([Birru et al., *PLOS One* 2025, PMC12057857](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12057857/)).
- **Classic prospective benchmark** (International Rheumatic Fever Study Group, 11 countries; 1,790 patients; **32,430 injections**; 2,736 patient-years): anaphylaxis **0.2% of patients = 1.2 per 10,000 injections**; **fatal = 1 patient = 0.05% = 0.31 per 10,000 injections**. Its own conclusion: *"life-threatening allergic reactions are rare … the long-term benefits of such prophylaxis by far outweigh the risk"* ([International Rheumatic Fever Study Group, *Lancet* 1991; PubMed 1674296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1674296/)).

So a fatal reaction occurs on the order of **1 per ~2,000 patients** (both sources independently land near 0.05% of patients) and on the order of **~0.3 per 10,000 injections** (1991 figure). **Honesty flag:** the *AHA advisory text itself does not publish a single headline incidence number* — it argues from a case series of reports, not a denominator. The quantified rates above come from the surrounding peer-reviewed literature (1991 prospective study; 2025 meta-analysis), which is the correct way to put a number on it. These are the bounds the literature supports, not an AHA-stated figure.

**What the advisory recommended — it did NOT say stop.** It split patients into two groups:
- **Low-risk patients (the large majority):** **continue intramuscular BPG**, the standard, with simple vasovagal-prevention steps applied to everyone (part c below).
- **Elevated-risk patients (severe/advanced valvular RHD):** **oral penicillin "should be strongly considered"** as an alternative *if reliably available and affordable* — i.e. switch the delivery route for the few at genuine risk, do not abandon prophylaxis ([JAHA 2022, PubMed 35049336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35049336/); [Healio 2022](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20220127/aha-advisory-oral-penicillin-safer-than-injectable-for-highrisk-rheumatic-heart-disease)).

## (b) Side-by-side: benefit vs harm

Benefit figures are drawn from **M3** (`artifacts/2026-06-26-m3-cost-of-inaction-model.md`), which applies the GOAL-trial relative risk reduction (≈90.4%, derived from 0.8% vs 8.3% two-year progression; [Beaton et al., *NEJM* 2021](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2102074)) to GBD burden. Harm figures are from §(a).

| | **BENEFIT of running prophylaxis** (M3) | **HARM of running prophylaxis** (§a) |
|---|---|---|
| Headline magnitude | **~234,000 deaths/yr avertable** across just 5 counted countries (India, China, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal); **global base ~304,000/yr** (range ~118,000–321,000) — M3 | **~6 fatal reactions per ~11,587 patients** observed in the largest pooled series — Birru 2025 |
| Per-patient frequency | Prevents ~1 disease-progression event for every ~13 patients given 2 years of prophylaxis (GOAL NNT, §d) | ~1 fatal reaction per ~2,000 patients ever treated (0.05%, both sources) |
| Who it acts on | All patients with, or at risk of progressing, RHD | **Concentrated almost entirely in severe/advanced valvular RHD** — a flaggable minority |
| Direction of certainty | Large; softened by the latent→established extrapolation (M3 limitation A1) | Small; softened by under-reporting (could be an undercount) |
| Cost to deliver | Drug ~$0.11–$1.00/dose; ~$22M–$200M/yr global drug bill (M3 via CHAI) | The mitigation steps in §(c) are essentially free |

**Read across the top row:** the benefit is counted in the **hundreds of thousands of deaths per year**; the documented fatal harm in the entire pooled global literature is **single digits across ~155,000 injections**. The two quantities are separated by roughly **four to five orders of magnitude**. Suspending the program to avoid ~6 documented fatal reactions would forfeit a benefit measured in the hundreds of thousands.

## (c) Mitigation / administration practices (sourced)

These are concrete, low-cost steps a program can adopt tomorrow. They are the reason the program continues rather than stops.

1. **Risk-stratify by echo, then route the highest-risk patients to ORAL penicillin.** Patients with severe mitral/aortic valve disease, reduced LV function, or heart-failure symptoms should be **strongly considered for oral penicillin V** instead of IM BPG, where oral supply is reliable — the advisory's single biggest mitigation ([JAHA 2022, PubMed 35049336](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35049336/)). The corollary: **the vast low-risk majority keep getting the injection**, because for them IM BPG is both safer-adhered and effective.
2. **Administer supine and keep the patient lying flat for ≥5 minutes afterward**, to blunt the vasovagal/cardiovascular drop the advisory identified; combine with **pre-injection oral hydration (~500 mL water) and a snack** to reduce vasovagal fainting ([AHA advisory administration steps, via Healio 2022](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20220127/aha-advisory-oral-penicillin-safer-than-injectable-for-highrisk-rheumatic-heart-disease)).
3. **Minimize injection pain and inject slowly** (the advisory's pain-reduction recommendation), since pain and apprehension drive the vasovagal response; pain control is also the leading lever on adherence ([Healio 2022](https://www.healio.com/news/cardiology/20220127/aha-advisory-oral-penicillin-safer-than-injectable-for-highrisk-rheumatic-heart-disease); pain-as-adherence-barrier reviewed in [NZ adherence review, PMC8048926](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8048926/)).
4. **Supervised administration with basic resuscitation capacity on hand** (trained observer, recovery position, ability to manage a faint or, rarely, anaphylaxis) — standard for any IM penicillin program and the reason injections should be given at a facility/clinic visit rather than unsupervised ([International Rheumatic Fever Study Group, *Lancet* 1991, PubMed 1674296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1674296/); reaction-typing in [de Dassel et al., *JAHA* 2019, PMC6590992](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6590992/)).

**Genuine contraindication / higher-risk flag:** a documented true penicillin anaphylaxis history, and severe/advanced valvular RHD (route to oral). Ordinary fear of the injection is **not** a contraindication; it is an adherence problem the steps above address.

## (d) Number-needed-to-treat vs number-needed-to-harm (show the arithmetic)

**NNT (to prevent one progression event), from GOAL** — the trial's own 2-year latent-disease outcome:
```
Control progression  = 8.3% = 0.083
Penicillin progression = 0.8% = 0.008
Absolute risk reduction (ARR) = 0.083 − 0.008 = 0.075
NNT = 1 / ARR = 1 / 0.075 ≈ 13.3   → ~13 patients treated for 2 years to prevent 1 progression
```
([Beaton et al., *NEJM* 2021](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2102074); same derivation used in M3.)

**NNH (to cause one fatal reaction), from the harm literature** — two ways:
```
Per patient (both sources agree at 0.05%):
   2025 meta: 6 fatal / 11,587 patients → NNH ≈ 11,587 / 6 ≈ 1,931 patients
   1991 IRFSG: 1 fatal / 1,790 patients → NNH ≈ 1,790 patients
   → NNH ≈ ~2,000 patients ever treated per fatal reaction.

Per injection (1991 figure, 0.31 per 10,000 injections):
   NNH ≈ 10,000 / 0.31 ≈ 32,000 injections per fatal reaction.
```
([Birru et al., *PLOS One* 2025, PMC12057857](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12057857/); [International Rheumatic Fever Study Group, *Lancet* 1991, PubMed 1674296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1674296/).)

**The asymmetry, made legible:**
- **NNT ≈ 13** patients to prevent one disease-progression event.
- **NNH ≈ 2,000** patients (or ~32,000 injections) to cause one fatal reaction — *and that one reaction is almost always confined to a severe-RHD patient who can instead be put on oral penicillin.*
- **NNH / NNT ≈ 2,000 / 13 ≈ 150.** For every patient harmed, on the order of 150× as many are spared a progression event — and the harm denominator is *further* concentrated in a subgroup you can identify and reroute. The benefit is broad and frequent; the fatal harm is rare and targetable.

**Caveat on comparing the two:** NNT is from a *latent-disease progression* outcome over 2 years (not deaths), while NNH is a *fatal* outcome. They are not the same currency — see limitations. They are placed together to convey order-of-magnitude asymmetry, not to claim "150 lives saved per life lost"; the honest claim is "the documented fatal-harm rate is two-to-three orders of magnitude smaller than the per-patient benefit rate, and is concentrated where it can be mitigated."

## (e) Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Under-reporting of adverse events.** Fatal-reaction counts (6 in the 2025 pool; 1 in 1991) come from cohorts and case reports with no systematic global pharmacovigilance for BPG. The true fatal-reaction rate could be higher than 0.05% of patients — the advisory was *prompted* precisely because reports were accumulating faster than a passive system would catch. The asymmetry is so large that even a 5–10× undercount of harm does not flip the conclusion, but the harm figure is a floor, not a ceiling.
2. **Benefit and harm are measured in different currencies (the M3 A1 gap).** The benefit NNT (~13) is GOAL's *progression-of-latent-disease* outcome over 2 years, not deaths, and not measured in established symptomatic RHD; the M3 death figures extrapolate that effect to established-RHD mortality (M3 assumption A1, flagged as the model's largest). The harm NNH is a *fatal* outcome. Putting NNT and NNH side by side is informative for order-of-magnitude, but it is not a like-for-like ratio, and the death-benefit figure could be materially lower than M3's base case if the latent→established transfer is weaker than assumed.
3. **The harm is not zero, and it is real for a specific group.** The advisory exists because severe-RHD patients *have* died after BPG. The correct response is risk-stratification and the oral-route option (§c), not denial. A program that ignores echo-based stratification and injects severe-RHD patients unsupervised is the scenario the advisory warns against — the dossier argues for *continuing with precautions*, not for complacency.
4. **Applicability of the harm literature.** The 1991 study is 35 years old and the 2025 meta-analysis pools heterogeneous cohorts (wide CIs: severe-reaction 95% CI 0.4–2.2 per 10,000 injections). Population mix, RHD severity distribution, and observation quality vary; the per-injection NNH (~32,000) and per-patient NNH (~2,000) should be read as order-of-magnitude, not precise.
5. **Oral-penicillin mitigation has its own trade-off.** Switching high-risk patients to oral penicillin trades a tiny injection-reaction risk for a *higher adherence/efficacy risk* — oral prophylaxis is less reliably protective than supervised IM BPG, and the advisory conditions the switch on oral being "reliably available and affordable," which in many high-burden LMIC settings it is not. The mitigation is not free in protection terms; it is a route-of-administration hedge for the few, not a default for the many.
