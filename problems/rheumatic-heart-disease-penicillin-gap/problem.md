# Rheumatic heart disease: a pennies-per-dose injection in chronic shortage while it kills children

> Intake brief from the scout routine, 2026-06-25. Category: global health and disease (shape pick; rotation lap complete).
> Bottleneck type: misaligned incentives (the drug is too cheap to manufacture profitably) compounded by supply-chain and coverage grind nobody is staffed to map — against a backdrop of recurring stockouts and a safety-fear that suppresses use.

## The problem, in plain language

Rheumatic heart disease (RHD) starts as a childhood strep throat that, untreated, triggers
an autoimmune attack on the heart valves. It affects [over 40 million people and causes
more than 300,000 deaths a year](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12925792/),
almost entirely in low- and middle-income countries — and it kills the young. In endemic
regions, [1-year mortality reaches 17% among children and adolescents](https://www.acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/journal-scans/2021/11/14/00/07/secondary-antibiotic-prophylaxis-aha-2021),
driven by heart failure and by poor adherence to prophylaxis. This is a disease the rich
world eliminated decades ago and the poor world still buries kids over.

## The half-known solution

The fix is one of the oldest, cheapest drugs in medicine: intramuscular benzathine
penicillin G (BPG), one injection every 3–4 weeks. The 2021 NEJM
[GOAL trial in Uganda](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2102074) gave the first
randomized proof that it works in latent disease: among 818 children, progression occurred
in [0.8% on penicillin versus 8.3% with no treatment over two years](https://scienceblog.cincinnatichildrens.org/in-uganda-rheumatic-heart-disease-research-reaches-its-goal/)
— a tenfold reduction. A 1.2-million-unit dose costs on the order of cents. WHO put RHD on
the global agenda with [resolution WHA71.14 in 2018](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rheumatic-heart-disease)
and published [new prevention/diagnosis guidelines in 2024](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/rheumatic-heart-disease).
The science is settled; the molecule is ancient and off-patent.

## Why it's stuck

A dramatic inversion: the drug is stuck *because* it is cheap. BPG's manufacturing process
is [largely unchanged since the 1950s](https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2025/222/5/shortages-benzathine-benzylpenicillin-g-australia-highlight-need-new-sovereign),
margins are thin, and by 2016 [only three Chinese active-pharmaceutical-ingredient makers
remained supplying the world](https://academic.oup.com/inthealth/article/16/3/279/7288044).
The result is recurring stockouts — including a [US Bicillin L-A shortage running through
2025](https://vdh.virginia.gov/clinicians/update-on-bicillin-shortage). CHAI estimated
global demand at [74–100 million doses against a true need of ~200 million](https://www.clintonhealthaccess.org/blog/slowing-global-antimicrobial-resistance-to-syphilis-and-other-stis/).
On the demand side, coverage is dismal even where supply exists: in Australia's
well-resourced Northern Territory register, only [42% of patients received ≥80% of
scheduled doses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6009701/). A safety chill
compounds it — the AHA issued a [2022 presidential advisory on rare fatal injection
reactions in severe RHD](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.121.024517), which,
like the bisphosphonate scare, risks suppressing a vastly net-beneficial therapy. Scurvy-
shaped: a known cure, an unglamorous supply chain, and no one paid to close the loop.

## The AI-agent desk-research wedge

Pure desk synthesis over published data — the methane/fishing-attribution playbook applied
to a generic drug. An agent can, over hours: (1) build a **country-by-country RHD coverage-
and-stockout scorecard**, cross-referencing GBD burden estimates, national register reports
(Australia, New Zealand, Uganda, Fiji), and WHO/UNICEF essential-medicines stockout surveys
to quantify the gap per population; (2) assemble a **BPG supply-chain concentration map** —
naming the surviving API manufacturers, finished-dose suppliers, and known shortage events —
to expose the single-point-of-failure that no procurement agency has laid out publicly;
(3) compute the **deaths-and-dollars cost of inaction** per endemic country (the GOAL effect
size applied to GBD prevalence); and (4) draft the **benefit-harm dossier** that inverts the
injection-reaction scare with the GOAL arithmetic, the artifact a health ministry needs to
keep prophylaxis programs running. None of this requires a new trial.

## Who's closest today

[Reach (rheach.org)](https://www.rheach.org/news/tags/benzathine-penicillin-g) and the
[World Heart Federation](https://world-heart-federation.org/what-we-do/rheumatic-heart-disease/)
run RHD Action and the registries; [CHAI, with the Gates Foundation and WHO](https://www.clintonhealthaccess.org/blog/slowing-global-antimicrobial-resistance-to-syphilis-and-other-stis/),
owns the BPG supply-security work; the GOAL investigators (Cincinnati Children's, Children's
National, the Uganda Heart Institute) hold the clinical case. The cure is 70 years old, the
RCT is five years old, the WHO resolution is seven years old — and the supply-and-coverage
map that would let a funder or ministry act on a named gap still does not exist at the
specificity required. That map is the wedge.
