# Problem: the post-fracture osteoporosis treatment gap

> Intake brief from the scout routine, 2026-06-12. Category: demography (aging).
> Bottleneck type: misaligned incentives (nobody owns the patient after the cast comes off), compounded by a fear-driven evidence inversion and reimbursement grind.

**(a) The problem.** A fragility fracture in an older adult is a heart-attack-grade event: in the Medicare population, osteoporotic fractures cause [more hospitalizations than heart attacks, strokes, or breast cancer](https://www.bonehealthpolicyinstitute.org/newsroom/35-national-health-organizations-call-on-medicare-to-use-2025-payment-rule-to-improve-patient-care-and-prevent-osteoporosis-related-broken-bones), and 20–30% of hip-fracture patients are dead within a year. The US logs ~2 million fragility fractures annually, on a path to [$95B/year in costs by 2040](https://www.bonehealthpolicyinstitute.org/newsroom/35-national-health-organizations-call-on-medicare-to-use-2025-payment-rule-to-improve-patient-care-and-prevent-osteoporosis-related-broken-bones). The first fracture roughly doubles the risk of the next — yet [fewer than 25% of fracture patients get osteoporosis treatment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40223698/), and among those who eventually do, the [median delay is 24 months](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/musculoskeletal-disorders/articles/10.3389/fmscd.2025.1620506/full) — precisely the window when refracture risk peaks.

**(b) The half-known solution.** Generic alendronate costs on the order of $50/year and roughly halves hip and vertebral fracture risk. The delivery model is also solved: the Fracture Liaison Service (FLS), invented in Glasgow in 1999 — a nurse coordinator catches every fracture patient and starts treatment — is [cost-effective to cost-saving for Medicare](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9291535/), and the IOF's [Capture the Fracture](https://www.jseaortho.org/index.php/jsao/article/view/224) network has certified 1,000+ FLS in 59 countries. A Thai district hospital running the playbook halved 2-year mortality (18% vs 37%).

**(c) Why it's stuck.** A dramatic inversion: after 2010 media coverage of atypical femoral fractures — a real but rare side effect — bisphosphonate use [fell by about half](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S8756328221004634), and post-hip-fracture treatment collapsed [from 15% (2004) to 3% (2013)](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2016/6/24/doctors-warn-of-crisis-in-osteoporosis-treatment) in one large cohort. The arithmetic is lopsided: [149 hip fractures prevented per 2 atypical fractures caused](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1916525) at 3 years. Khosla and Shane named this ["a crisis in the treatment of osteoporosis"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jbmr.2888) in 2016; a decade later the gap persists because no specialty owns it — the surgeon fixes the bone and discharges — and no one is paid to close it. CMS's 2025 fee schedule took ["critical first steps"](https://www.bonehealthandosteoporosis.org/news/medicares-proposed-2025-pfs-rule-takes-critical-first-steps-to-improve-post-fracture-care-for-osteoporosis-patients-in-the-us/) but Medicare still has no dedicated FLS payment despite [35 national organizations requesting post-fracture G-codes](https://womenshealthandprevention.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/2026MedicarePFSAsMeansofImprovingOsteoporosisCareFINAL.pdf). The UK pledged [100% FLS coverage by 2030](https://lordslibrary.parliament.uk/fracture-liaison-services-towards-universal-coverage-in-england-by-2030/) (confirmed May 2025, cost: ~£30M/yr) yet as of [April 2026 has published no delivery plan](https://labourlist.org/2026/04/sponsored-wes-streeting-63-promises-still-no-plan/), while the Royal Osteoporosis Society counts [~1,400 needless deaths this year](https://theros.org.uk/latest-news/1-400-lives-set-to-be-needlessly-lost-this-year-following-government-inaction-on-osteoporosis/) from the delay. Scurvy-shaped: Glasgow proved the fix 27 years ago.

**(d) The AI-agent wedge.** Pure desk synthesis. An agent could: build the league table of post-fracture treatment rates by country and US state/health-system from published cohorts, registry reports, and IOF SCOPE scorecards; compute the deaths-and-dollars cost of each payer's gap (the ROS model, rebuilt for Medicare); assemble the benefit-harm dossier that inverts the atypical-fracture scare with the NEJM arithmetic; and draft the CMS comment-docket submission and NHS-style business case the coalitions need — the artifact that exists for England but not for the US or anywhere else.

**(e) Closest to cracking it.** Royal Osteoporosis Society + UK APPG (pledge in hand, plan missing); BHOF/ASBMR/Bone Health Policy Institute coalition working the CMS docket; IOF Capture the Fracture (Cyrus Cooper, Kassim Javaid) on the global map; Khosla and Shane on the clinical case; Kaiser Permanente's Healthy Bones program as the US system precedent (~40% hip-fracture reduction). The fix is generic, the model is certified in 59 countries, and the buyers are mid-decision. What's missing is the dossier.
