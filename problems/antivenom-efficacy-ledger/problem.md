# Antivenoms that don't work: the missing efficacy ledger for African snakebite

> Intake brief from the scout routine, 2026-06-28. Category: global health and disease (welfare lane).
> Shape: the investigation — name which marketed products fail against which snakes, and the flows that keep them on shelves. Any fix follows from the naming.

## (a) The problem, in plain language

Snakebite envenoming kills an estimated [81,000–138,000 people a year](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(23)01698-7/fulltext) and leaves roughly three times that number with amputations or permanent disability. Sub-Saharan Africa carries about [32,000 deaths and 100,000 disabilities annually](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-54070-y). The grim twist: a large share of the antivenom actually sold across Africa is of unknown or proven-poor efficacy against the snakes that bite there. A 2025 investigation documented products "[as useless as water](https://www.thebureauinvestigates.com/stories/2025-02-13/the-new-snake-oil-antivenoms-that-are-as-useless-as-water)," and in 2019–2020 Kenya [recalled India-made antivenoms after they failed in the field](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/africa/-ineffective-india-made-antivenoms-recalled-in-kenya-country-faces-snakebite-crisis-89125). Some products are manufactured with venoms from non-African snakes and are "[dangerously ineffective](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0008579)." A patient given a worthless vial dies as surely as one given none — but pays for it and loses the hours when a working product could have saved them.

## (b) State of the art

Antivenom is a proven 19th-century technology; the science of which immunoglobulin neutralizes which venom is well understood. The gap is not invention but verification and transparency. Independent preclinical (murine ED50) testing exists in scattered journal papers: VINS outperformed Premium Serums' PANAF in [three of four snakes tested](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0005969), while [no published preclinical efficacy data exist at all](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0008579) for ASNA-C (Bharat), Premium's Central Africa product, or VINS' Echis product. Only [three antivenoms in African use have robust clinical-trial data; seven have none](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0008579). WHO began a manufacturer-submitted [risk-benefit assessment (TAG-SAIL) in 2022](https://extranet.who.int/prequal/vaccines/antivenom-risk-benefit-assessment-procedure) and targets [halving snakebite deaths by 2030](https://haiweb.org/who-global-strategy/) — a target current trends [will miss](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12096475/).

## (c) Why it is stuck

The market is a lemons market: a procurement officer cannot tell a working vial from a useless one, so price wins, and cheap ineffective products from manufacturers using the wrong venoms undercut good ones — driving the makers of effective antivenom out (the original Fav-Afrique was discontinued for exactly this reason). WHO's assessment is voluntary and manufacturer-driven: a maker that never submits is never assessed, and the results are not published as a public, head-to-head, venom-by-venom league table. South Africa's NHLS [halted antivenom production entirely in 2025](https://www.meded.co.za/DocumentPart/?id=8515), deepening the vacuum that bad products fill. No actor owns the consolidated efficacy picture.

## (d) The AI-agent wedge

Pure desk synthesis. An agent can build the artifact that does not exist: a **public antivenom efficacy ledger** for sub-Saharan Africa. It would extract every published murine ED50 / preclinical and clinical efficacy result from the PLOS NTD, Toxins, and national-QC-lab literature; map each commercial product (VINS, Premium/PANAF, Bharat/ASNA, Inoserp, and others) to the specific medically important snake species it has and has not been shown to neutralize; flag products marketed in a country whose snakes were never in the immunizing mix; and cross-reference against the venoms named on each product's own label. Output: a product-by-species matrix with an evidence grade per cell ("effective / ineffective / untested"), plus the list of products in active African circulation with zero public efficacy evidence. That matrix is exactly what national procurement programs and WHO's TAG-SAIL need and what neither currently publishes.

## (e) Who is closest

The [Liverpool School of Tropical Medicine](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0005969) (Robert Harrison's and Nicholas Casewell's groups) and the [Kenya National Antivenom Quality Control Laboratory](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6651/18/2/106) generate the preclinical data; [WHO's snakebite programme and TAG-SAIL](https://www.who.int/activities/addressing-antivenoms-issues) hold the regulatory lever; Health Action International runs the [2030-strategy accountability campaign](https://haiweb.org/who-global-strategy/). The data exists in fragments; the consolidated, public, decision-ready ledger does not. The agent can build it; only WHO and national regulators can pull the bad products.
