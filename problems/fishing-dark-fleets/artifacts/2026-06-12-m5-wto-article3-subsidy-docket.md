## A. What this is

A docket of publicly documented links between government fisheries subsidies and operators behind IUU-determined vessels, built so that a WTO member delegation or the Secretariat's Committee on Fisheries Subsidies could act on them under Article 3 of the Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies (in force **15 September 2025**, per the [WTO legal-text page](https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/fish_e.htm), fetched today).

**Headline: 3 subsidy-to-operator links meet the full evidentiary bar** (≥2 independent subsidy-side sources + an official IUU determination or M3 entry on the operator side):

1. **Spain/EU → Vidal Armadores network** (M3 rank 5; 5 currently CCAMLR-listed hulls) — ≥€8.2M in subsidies and soft loans 1990s–2010, overlapping a 1999–2016 IUU record. *Confidence: confirmed.*
2. **China → Pingtan Marine Enterprise / Fuzhou Honglong** (M3 §D, FTC's #1 IUU-vessel owner) — state equity, policy-bank loans and disclosed subsidy income 2015–2021, continuing **after** Indonesia's 2016 and Ecuador's 2017 determinations. *Confidence: confirmed.*
3. **Spain/EU → Albacora S.A.** (M3 §D, FTC's #10) — vessel built with subsidies; €1.8M granted to fish in foreign waters **after** the largest IUU penalty NOAA had ever assessed. *Confidence: confirmed.*

Two further entries are documented but **excluded from the headline count**: China → CNFC (operator-to-vessel link is *reported-only*) and China → Shandong Lanyue (subsidy receipt is *probable by program-level inference* only — and is therefore the docket's primary Article 8 information-request target, because Lanyue is the largest owner of *currently listed* hulls in M3).

**The honest core finding:** every fully documented subsidy payment in this docket predates the Agreement's entry into force, so none is directly actionable as a past violation (the Agreement is not retroactive). What the docket establishes is (a) the named operators whose IUU determinations are **in force today** (CCAMLR listings re-verified in M2 on 2026-06-12; OFAC designations re-verified today), and (b) the documented subsidy channels that flowed to exactly these operators — so that the concrete, present-tense WTO question becomes: *are these subsidies being maintained after 15 September 2025?* Section D sets out the steps a member would take to force that answer onto the record.

## B. The legal hook — Article 3 verbatim, and who is bound

Quoted from the Agreement on Fisheries Subsidies, [WTO legal texts page](https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/fish_e.htm) (fetched 2026-06-12):

> **Article 3.1:** "No Member shall grant or maintain any subsidy to a vessel or operator engaged in illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing or fishing related activities in support of IUU fishing."

> **Article 3.2:** a vessel or operator is considered engaged in IUU fishing if an affirmative determination is made by "(a) a coastal Member, for activities in areas under its jurisdiction; or (b) a flag State Member, for activities by vessels flying its flag; or (c) a relevant Regional Fisheries Management Organization or Arrangement (RFMO/A)."

> **Article 3.3(a):** "An affirmative determination under Article 3.2 refers to the final finding by a Member and/or the final listing by an RFMO/A that a vessel or operator has engaged in IUU fishing."

> **Article 3.4:** members shall take into account "the nature, gravity, and repetition of IUU fishing committed by a vessel or operator when setting the duration of application of the prohibition in Article 3.1."

**Status and parties (verified today):** the Agreement entered into force 15 September 2025 and "applies only to the WTO Members that have accepted it" ([WTO legal-text page](https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/fish_e.htm); [WTO acceptances page](https://www.wto.org/english/tratop_e/rulesneg_e/fish_e/fish_acceptances_e.htm), both fetched today). **China deposited its acceptance on 27 June 2023** ([WTO news, 27 Jun 2023](https://www.wto.org/english/news_e/news23_e/fish_27jun23_e.htm)); by that date **all EU member states had already accepted** ([World Economic Forum, Jun 2023](https://www.weforum.org/press/2023/06/china-world-s-largest-seafood-producer-ratifies-wto-deal-on-fisheries-subsidies/)). So the two subsidizing jurisdictions in this docket — China and the EU/Spain — are both bound since entry into force.

The three RFMO/A listings and two coastal-state findings used below all satisfy the Article 3.2/3.3(a) "final listing / final finding" form; per-entry caveats are stated where the fit is arguable.

## D. How a member would raise these cases — Article 8 and the Committee, step by step

The transparency obligation, quoted from the [WTO legal-text page](https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/fish_e.htm) (fetched today):

> **Article 8.2:** "Each Member shall notify the Committee in writing on an annual basis of a list of vessels and operators that it has affirmatively determined as having been engaged in IUU fishing."

Article 8.1 additionally requires members' regular fisheries-subsidy notifications to state the "type or kind of fishing activity for which the subsidy is provided" plus fleet, vessel-identification and catch information; Article 8.3 requires members to notify their Article 3 implementation measures within one year of entry into force — i.e., **by 15 September 2026**, three months from this run date (same source).

Concrete sequence for a member (or coalition — e.g., Ecuador, the US and the EU each hold determinations cited above):

1. **Anchor the determinations.** The determining members notify their Article 3.2 findings under Article 8.2: Ecuador notifies the FU YUAN YU LENG 999/Honglong conviction; the US notifies the Albacora Uno finding; the EU and CCAMLR members rely on CCAMLR's standing IUU list (an Art. 3.2(c) listing needs no separate member determination — Art. 3.3(a)). The M2 ledger is, in substance, the consolidated Annex such notifications would contain.
2. **Interrogate the subsidizing members' notifications.** China's and the EU's Article 8.1 subsidy notifications and Article 8.3 implementation notifications are reviewable in the Committee on Fisheries Subsidies (established under the Agreement's institutional provisions). A member tables written questions: *Does China grant or maintain any subsidy — fuel, vessel construction, base construction, policy-bank concessional finance — to Shandong Lanyue Ocean Fishery, CNFC/COFC, or any entity on the OFAC-designated Pingtan/Honglong network? Does Spain or the EU maintain any payment, licence subsidy or EMFAF eligibility for Albacora S.A. or any successor entity of the Vidal network?* The cross-notification and counter-notification practice of the SCM Committee (ASCM Art. 25 procedures) supplies the precedent for putting unnotified subsidies on the record.
3. **Force the duration question.** Article 3.4 obliges the subsidizing member to set a prohibition duration reflecting "nature, gravity, and repetition." For operators with *repeat* determinations (Vidal: 40+ incidents and a €17.84M sanction; Pingtan/Honglong: determinations in three jurisdictions), a member can demand that China and the EU state on the record what duration they have set — a question with no compliant answer if no prohibition was ever applied.
4. **Escalate if maintained.** The Agreement provides for WTO dispute settlement (Agreement text, Article 10, same source); a documented post-September-2025 payment to a currently-listed operator would be the first justiciable Article 3 case. Short of litigation, the docket entries are usable now by the NGO carriers named in the spec (FTC, EJF, GFW JAC) as Committee-meeting submissions via sympathetic delegations.

## E. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Temporal mismatch is the docket's central weakness (required limitation).** Every documented payment above is pre-entry-into-force (latest: Pingtan's $19.0M, Jan–Sep 2021). Article 3 prohibits *granting or maintaining* subsidies from 15 September 2025 for accepting members only; nothing here is retroactively actionable, and a defending member will say exactly that. The docket's claim is narrower: the determinations are in force today, the subsidy channels are documented, and the burden of showing discontinuation now sits with notifying members under Article 8. Counter-evidence on the other side of the ledger: subsidies may genuinely have stopped — Pingtan's fleet has been OFAC-blocked since Dec 2022 and the company ceased SEC reporting, and Spain barred the Vidal network from aid after Operation Sparrow; absence of post-2025 payment evidence is partly an absence of disclosure, but it may also be absence of payment.
2. **Subsidy opacity is worst exactly where subsidies are largest (required limitation).** China stopped publicly reporting DWF fuel subsidies in 2012; the per-vessel subsidy base is undisclosed; 2019 DWF amounts are NGO *estimates* (CNY 953M) rather than published totals ([Oceana, Oct 2021, pp. 5–6](https://oceana.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/18/Final-China-Fisheries-Subsidies-October-2021.pdf)); the OECD FSE database was unreachable to this agent (M1 row 26, HTTP 403); and the EU's beneficiary registers (Kohesio/FTS, M1 rows 24–25) do not yet expose EMFAF fisheries lines in a way this agent could query. Result: the docket can document Chinese subsidies only where a recipient is listed on a stock exchange (CNFC, Pingtan via SEC) — a severe selection effect that explains why M3's rank-1 owner (Lanyue, private) has no entry-grade subsidy record.
3. **Beneficial-ownership layering between recipient and operator (required limitation).** The subsidy recipient and the IUU operator are rarely the same legal person: Pingtan (subsidy recipient) vs Fuzhou Honglong (vessel owner) are affiliated but distinct; Vidal subsidies went partly to Biomega Nutrición (a plant co-owned by a Vidal principal) while the listed vessels sat in Panamanian and other shells (M3 ranks 5/8); CNFC's subsidies flow to its listed arm COFC while the FTC attribution names the parent. Article 3.1's word "operator" gives some reach across these layers, but a tribunal could read it narrowly, and open sources cannot prove control chains that registries (OpenCorporates captcha-blocked, M1 row 15; Chinese SAIC records access-restricted per Pingtan's own 10-K) do not expose.
4. **What open sources cannot prove (required limitation).** This docket cannot show: (i) any post-September-2025 payment to any named operator; (ii) the internal eligibility decisions of MARA or Spanish paying agencies; (iii) that subsidy monies funded the specific IUU voyages (Article 3 does not require tracing, but advocacy audiences will ask); (iv) beneficiary-level Chinese DWF subsidy data, which is simply not published `[speculative that no provincial disclosure exists anywhere — Chinese-language municipal disclosure pages, e.g. Rongcheng's, were not searched exhaustively this run]`.
5. **Evidentiary-threshold contestability.** The five determinations vary in robustness: CCAMLR listings and the Ecuador criminal conviction fit Art. 3.3(a) cleanly; the NOAA outcome is a settlement (with admission) rather than a litigated finding; Indonesia's 2016 actions are court orders in a commercial-criminal context that Pingtan disputes in its own filings; and the OFAC designation, while verified live today, is a human-rights instrument, not an IUU determination, and is used here only as corroboration. Members raising Entry 3 should lead with the admission of 67 counts, not the settlement amount.
6. **Source-age and access caveats.** ICIJ (2011–12), Oceana press release (2010), Oceana subsidy report (Oct 2021), Pingtan 10-K (Oct 2021), FTC (Oct 2022) and the Treasury action (Dec 2022) are all >12 months old and document historical facts only; current-state claims rest exclusively on items fetched/downloaded 2026-06-12 (WTO treaty pages, SDN list, M2 list verifications).
