# M4 — DG MARE carding dossier: flag-state risk ranking

**Problem:** fishing-dark-fleets | **Milestone:** 4 | **Date:** 2026-06-12
**Builds on:** M2 consolidated ledger (214 currently IUU-listed vessels; flag counts recomputed from the ledger table today) and M3 beneficial-owner league table (107/214 vessels attributed; ownership jurisdictions). External sources below were fetched or search-verified on **2026-06-12** unless an earlier publication date is stated.

## A. What this is

An evidence dossier ranking the flag states most implicated in dark-fleet activity and forced-labor risk among seafood suppliers to the EU, structured as the evidence base for DG MARE's next carding round and for risk-flagging in **CATCH**, the EU's digital catch-certificate system that became mandatory on **10 January 2026** ([European Commission, 12 Jan 2026](https://oceans-and-fisheries.ec.europa.eu/news/new-digital-certification-system-tackle-illegal-fishing-2026-01-12_en); [IUU Watch, Jan 2026](https://www.iuuwatch.eu/2026/01/advancing-the-eus-fight-against-illegal-fishing-the-launch-of-the-it-catch-system/) — both fetched today). The ranking measures **unaddressed risk**: evidence of dark-fleet ties and forced labor, discounted by how much of the EU's carding lever is already deployed against that state. Red-carded states therefore sit low — not because they are clean, but because the EU's market lever is already fully exercised against them.

**Headline findings:**
1. **The two largest evidence stacks sit on states the EU has never carded or has delisted: China and Taiwan.** Both carry US forced-labor determinations from three separate agencies (CBP, DOL, NOAA), both dominate the M3 ownership table, and neither holds an EU card today.
2. **India flags 27 of the 214 currently IUU-listed vessels — the largest national bloc on the lists ([M2 ledger, recounted today]) — and has never been carded**, while exporting USD 1.13 billion of seafood to the EU in 2024–25 ([MPEDA via AgriTimes, Jun 2025](https://agritimes.co.in/aquaculture/indias-seafood-exports-us-7-45-billion-in-fy-2024-25-mpeda)).
3. **The carding record and the listing record do correlate at the bottom**: Cameroon, the only red-carded state, currently flags 3 listed vessels (M2 recount today) — the carding system caught the flag-of-convenience states; it has not yet touched the ownership jurisdictions.

## D. Yellow-card candidates — expanded justifications

Each candidate cites at least two **distinct evidence types** (M2 vessel/flag evidence; M3 ownership evidence; official forced-labor determinations; peer-reviewed dark-activity findings; trade exposure).

**D.1 China — candidate for pre-identification (yellow card).** Evidence types: (i) *M2/M3 vessel and ownership evidence* — 8 currently listed China-flagged vessels, and the two largest ownership networks behind currently listed vessels are Chinese (Shandong Lanyue, 12 vessels confirmed/probable; Shidao Group, 6 probable/contested — M3 §C), with the NPFC dark-squid cluster's identity-masking documented by [JAC Report 010 (Oct 2023)](https://imcsnet.org/sites/default/files/media_files/JAC%20Intel%20Report%20010%20-%20150%20vessels%20overview%20analysis%20-%20Final.pdf); (ii) *official forced-labor determinations* — fleet-wide [CBP WRO (2021)](https://www.cbp.gov/newsroom/national-media-release/cbp-issues-withhold-release-order-chinese-fishing-fleet), [DOL TVPRA listing (2020, retained 2024)](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ilab/reports/child-labor/list-of-goods), [NOAA forced-labor identification and negative certification (2023)](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2023-08/2023RTC-ImprovingIFManagement.pdf); (iii) *peer-reviewed dark-activity findings* — [Park et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abb1197) and [Welch et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abq2109). Article 31(4)–(6) of the IUU Regulation makes flag-state control failure and market access the relevant tests; both are met. The realistic obstacle is political-economic, not evidentiary (§E.1).

**D.2 Taiwan — candidate for re-carding.** Evidence types: (i) *M3 ownership evidence* — RFMO records name Kaohsiung/Pingtung owners with street addresses for 7 currently listed vessels (Huang Jia Yi ×5, Chang Lin Pao-Chun ×2 — official IOTC/WCPFC owner fields, M3 ranks 3 and 14), with a further ~31 stateless listed vessels carrying Taiwanese-style fleet names (probable, M3 §E); (ii) *official forced-labor determinations* — third consecutive [DOL TVPRA listing (Sep 2024)](https://www.greenpeace.org/usa/taiwan-caught-fish-once-again-on-department-of-labors-dol-list-of-goods-produced-by-child-labor-or-forced-labor/), [NOAA 2023 identification](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2023-08/2023RTC-ImprovingIFManagement.pdf), CBP vessel WROs; (iii) *dark-activity* — 3rd-highest AIS-disabling fraction among flags studied ([Welch et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abq2109)). The 2019 delisting rewarded legal reform; the 2020–2024 forced-labor record post-dates it. Ghana's June 2021 second card established that delisting is reversible ([EJF](https://ejfoundation.org/news-media/ghana-given-second-yellow-card-warning-from-eu-over-illegal-fishing)).

**D.3 India — candidate for pre-identification dialogue.** Evidence types: (i) *M2 vessel count* — 27 currently listed vessels, the largest national bloc, accumulated through new IOTC batch listings in each of 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024 and 2025 (M2 ledger rows and dates), demonstrating that the flag state has not stopped the violation pattern in five years; (ii) *trade exposure* — USD 1.126bn EU exports, the EU's leverage being precisely the IUU Regulation's design ([MPEDA](https://agritimes.co.in/aquaculture/indias-seafood-exports-us-7-45-billion-in-fy-2024-25-mpeda)). **Honest caveat:** these are small multiday gillnetters caught fishing in the BIOT/Chagos zone, not industrial dark vessels; M2 §F.6 warns raw counts overweight exactly this fleet. The recommendation is therefore the *dialogue* stage — the Commission's standard first step — not an assertion that India's situation equals China's. If weighted by tonnage rather than hull count, India would fall several ranks (Limitations §F.6).

**D.4 Vanuatu — candidate for renewed pre-identification.** Evidence types: (i) *official forced-labor determinations* — CBP WROs on two Vanuatu-flagged longliners (Tunago No. 61, 2019; Da Wang, 2020 — [CBP](https://www.cbp.gov/newsroom/national-media-release/cbp-issues-withhold-release-order-chinese-fishing-fleet)); (ii) *flag-state identification by a third party* — [NOAA 2023 IUU and shark-catch identification](https://www.fisheries.noaa.gov/s3/2023-08/2023RTC-ImprovingIFManagement.pdf); (iii) *M2/M3* — one currently listed Vanuatu-flagged vessel with a named owner (M3). The EU lifted Vanuatu's yellow card in 2014; every adverse finding above post-dates that decision.

## E. Methodology — ranking construction and weights

**Pillars (scored 0–3 each, ordinal, from the table's cited cells):**
- **A. Listed-fleet ties** (M2 current-flag count, weighted down for artisanal-scale fleets per M2 §F.6; former-flag rows at half weight; M3 ownership-jurisdiction concentration at full weight, since owners outlast flags).
- **B. Dark-activity evidence** (peer-reviewed or JAC per-flag findings = 2–3; regional-only = 1; none = 0).
- **C. Forced-labor evidence** (official determinations — CBP WRO, DOL TVPRA, NOAA identification — score 1 each, cap 3; model-only risk = 1).
- **D. EU leverage headroom** (never carded or delisted = ×1; active yellow = ×0.6; active red = ×0.2) — this is the discount that pushes red-carded states down: the ranking orders *what DG MARE can still act on*.

Rank ≈ (A + B + C) × D, with EU import exposure reported as context (leverage), **not** added to risk — a state is not riskier because it sells more to the EU, but action against it is more consequential. Scores: China (3+3+3)×1=9; Taiwan (2+2+3)×1=7; India (3+1+0)×1=4 (A already discounted for artisanal scale; undiscounted it would be 5); Panama (2+0+0... +shell evidence 1)×0.6≈1.8 ranked above Cameroon (3+1+0)×0.2=0.8; Vanuatu (1+0+3)×1=4 but ranked below India on fleet size; ties broken by exposure scale (Ecuador above Vietnam) and recency of adverse findings (Senegal, Ghana above Sri Lanka). The scoring is **judgment made explicit, not measurement** — changing the artisanal-fleet discount or the headroom multipliers reorders the middle of the table (sensitivity discussed in §F.6).

**Why ownership jurisdictions count against a flag state's risk picture even when vessels fly other flags:** the IUU Regulation's carding test is whether a state "discharges its duties under international law as flag, port, coastal or market State" — China and Taiwan enter mainly as states whose *nationals and companies* operate the listed and dark fleets (M3 ranks 1–3, 14), a responsibility the Regulation's Article 39 (nationals) covers explicitly.

## F. Agreement and divergence with the EU's carding record

**Where this ranking agrees with Brussels:**
- **Cameroon (red 2023):** the only red-carded state still flagging currently listed vessels (3, M2 recount) — the Commission's flag-of-convenience finding ([IP/22/7890](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/detail/en/ip_22_7890)) is corroborated by the ledger.
- **Senegal (yellow 2024):** the pre-identification's stated grounds (ICCAT-listed vessels under the flag, export/quota mismatch) are exactly what M2/M3-type evidence shows; the card is well-aimed ([CFFA](https://www.cffacape.org/publications-blog/yellow-card-senegal-the-fight-against-iuu-fishing-must-also-be-waged-in-the-coastal-zone)).
- **Ghana, Ecuador, Vietnam (active yellows):** all three retain unresolved control deficiencies; none should be greened on schedule pressure.
- **The 2014–2017 reds (Cambodia, Comoros, SVG):** their registries' near-absence from the *current* lists (0 current flags each, M2 recount) is consistent with carding having pushed dirty tonnage off those registries — the instrument works on registries.

**Where this ranking diverges — and why:**
1. **China (this dossier: #1; EU: never carded).** The largest divergence in the file. Every carded state's evidence base is thinner than China's stack of two M3-top-ownership networks, a fleet-wide CBP WRO, DOL and NOAA determinations, and two peer-reviewed dark-fleet findings. The Commission's own instrument does not exempt large partners, but its 14-year practice has carded only small and mid-sized states — an asymmetry critiqued in the trade-measure literature (e.g., [the UC study of Panama's carding](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/18m2394g)) and visible in the record itself: the largest subsidizer and largest DWF state has never received the first, purely diplomatic, step of pre-identification. The divergence is explainable by trade and diplomatic weight — China is the EU's 4th seafood supplier ([EUMOFA 2025](https://marketac.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/EUMOFA-Presentation-The-EU-Fish-Market-2025.pdf)) — but not by the evidence. *(Analysis, not a sourced motive claim.)*
2. **Vanuatu (this dossier: YC candidate; EU: green since Oct 2014).** Two CBP forced-labor vessel actions and a NOAA IUU/shark identification all post-date the EU's lifting decision; the EU record has not been revisited since.
3. **Taiwan (this dossier: #2 and YC candidate; EU: delisted Jun 2019).** The EU judged the legal framework fixed in 2019; three subsequent US determinations concern labor conditions on the same DWF the card was about. Either the EU considers forced labor outside the IUU Regulation's scope — a reading EJF and the IUU Watch coalition contest — or the file is due for reopening.
4. **India and Sri Lanka (27 and 9 current listed vessels; no card and delisted-2016 respectively).** The two largest national blocs on the current lists hold no card. The benign explanation — artisanal vessels, already sanctioned domestically, low EU-supply-chain relevance of the specific hulls — is plausible, but the Commission has carded states (St Kitts, Dec 2014, yellow still in force per COM(2024) 171 record) with zero current listed vessels. The carding record tracks registry behavior and port control far better than it tracks the current IUU lists.
5. **A structural divergence the table cannot show:** the highest AIS-disabling fraction in [Welch et al. 2022](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abq2109) belonged to **Spain (up to 14%)** — an EU member state, constitutionally outside the carding instrument, and home jurisdiction of the M3 Vidal network. Any flag-state ranking built for DG MARE inherits this blind spot by design.

## G. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **EU import-data gaps (required).** EUMOFA reports country-of-consignment, not flag-of-catch: catch by a Chinese- or Taiwanese-flagged vessel typically enters the EU as a product of the processing state (Ecuador, Thailand, Spain), so the "EU import exposure" column systematically understates DWF states' true market access and overstates processing states'. No public dataset links EU catch certificates to vessel flags — CATCH would hold exactly this linkage but its records are not public ([IUU Watch, Jan 2026](https://www.iuuwatch.eu/2026/01/advancing-the-eus-fight-against-illegal-fishing-the-launch-of-the-it-catch-system/)). Also, EUMOFA's primary report URL broke mid-run (site migrating to fishery-aquaculture-market-observatory.ec.europa.eu; observed today) — exposure figures rest on the extracted presentation deck (2024 data).
2. **Reliability of forced-labor risk models (required).** The McDonald et al. model was directly challenged in PNAS: [Swartz et al. 2021](https://www.pnas.org/content/118/19/e2100341118) argue AIS-based profiling "falls short as a proof of concept" — trained on few confirmed cases, with behaviors (long trips, transshipment) that also describe lawful economics. This dossier therefore weights *official determinations* (CBP WROs, DOL listings, NOAA identifications) above model output — but those are US legal-administrative instruments with their own evidentiary thresholds and politics, all three from one government (correlated, not independent), and China and Taiwan contest them. NOAA's next biennial report was due 2025; whether it appeared on schedule post-EO 14276 was not verified this run.
3. **Flag-hopping (required).** Flags are the most rotated identifier in the M2 ledger (Panama 14, Belize 12, Djibouti 5 former-flag rows; 69.6% of listed vessels now stateless). A flag-state ranking is therefore a snapshot of where dirty tonnage *last paused*: carding Cambodia and Belize pushed registrations toward Cameroon (carded 2023) and onward; deflagging makes responsive states (Oman) look clean and creates the stateless bloc that no card can reach. Pew (Apr 2026) documents the EU-side mirror: at least 105 large-scale vessels owned by EU interests under non-EU flags of noncompliance ([Pew, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.pew.org/en/research-and-analysis/articles/2026/04/13/the-european-union-can-curb-illegal-fishing-by-closing-vessel-registration-loopholes)). Carding treats the symptom (registries), not the network (owners) — which is why §E imports M3 ownership jurisdictions into the score.
4. **Source ages.** The per-flag dark-activity studies are 2020–2024 publications analyzing 2017–2021 data (Park, Welch, McDonald, Paolo, JAC) — cited as evidence of patterns, not as current-state measurements; no per-flag SAR/AIS statistic newer than Paolo et al. (Jan 2024) was located in public sources this run. The Ghana trade figure (Dialogue Earth) is from 2021.
5. **Card-status verification limits.** EUR-Lex blocked automated access (M1 row 18, reconfirmed); statuses rest on COM(2024) 171, press releases, and trade press, with **no single machine-readable Commission registry to diff against**. Pre-2020 decision months (Sierra Leone Apr 2016, Liberia May 2017, St Kitts Dec 2014) are taken from the secondary record and could be off by weeks. A 2025–26 decision missed by today's searches is possible; the strongest currency check found is the March 2026 Vietnam inspection cycle reported as still-yellow ([VietnamPlus](https://www.vietnam.vn/en/doan-thanh-tra-cua-ec-sap-sang-viet-nam-quyet-tam-go-the-vang-iuu)).
6. **Ranking sensitivity / counter-evidence to the headline.** India at #3 is the most contestable call: its 27 vessels are gillnetters, while China's 8 flagged + ~45 attributed identities are industrial squid vessels — by tonnage or catch value the gap between #1 and #3 is far larger than the ranks suggest, and a tonnage-weighted table would drop India behind Panama and Senegal. Conversely, a pure hull-count table would put India #1 — both extremes are wrong, which is why the weights are stated. The Shidao attribution underpinning part of China's score is *contested by China's own ministry* (M3 §F.2); removing it does not change China's rank (the CBP/DOL/NOAA stack alone exceeds every carded state's file).
7. **What a card can and cannot do.** Counter-evidence on effectiveness: Cameroon's exports were already shifting to non-EU markets before its red card ([SeafoodSource](https://www.seafoodsource.com/news/premium/supply-trade/cameroon-s-eu-red-card-could-accelerate-its-export-shift-to-other-markets)), and the M2 stateless bloc (69.6%) is beyond any flag-state instrument. Carding China would be the system's first test against a state that could retaliate; the recommendation stands on the evidence while acknowledging the Regulation has never been used that way.
