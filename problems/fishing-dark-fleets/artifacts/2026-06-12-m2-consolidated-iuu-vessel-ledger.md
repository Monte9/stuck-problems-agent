## A. What this ledger is

A single deduplicated ledger of **214 distinct vessels currently listed** on RFMO IUU vessel lists and/or the TMT/IMCS Combined IUU Vessel List, built by machine-parsing eight primary lists end-to-end and merging them on hard identifiers and name-variant keys. "Currently listed" means: present in the Combined IUU Vessel List default (current) search, the NAFO consolidated IUU file of 1 June 2026, ICCAT's active IUU records, the CCAMLR/NEAFC/WCPFC/SPRFMO current lists, or the IOTC IUU list of 15 May 2026, as retrieved today. Delisted vessels are excluded by design.

**Headline numbers (computed from the table below):**
- **214 distinct vessels** from 608 source records (607 parsed records + 1 vessel added from the IOTC PDF found in no other source).
- **66 vessels (30.8%) carry an IMO number**; a further **30 carry another hard identifier** (MMSI, national registration number, or IRCS) without an IMO; the remaining **118 are identified by name + flag-as-recorded + listing body** (mostly small Indian/Sri Lankan and Chinese squid vessels for which the lists themselves publish no identifier).
- Listing-body presence (a vessel can be listed by several bodies): IOTC 204, NPFC 45, ICCAT 38, NAFO 28, NEAFC 26, SIOFA 26, CCSBT 20, CCAMLR 18, GFCM 14, IATTC 13, SEAFO 9, WCPFC 4, SPRFMO 1.
- Flag as recorded by the listing bodies: **Unknown/stateless 149 (69.6%)**, India 27, Sri Lanka 9, China 8, all others ≤3. Statelessness is the dominant recorded condition of the listed fleet.
- 100% of rows cite at least one list verified live in M1 (source key below maps to M1 inventory rows 1–8).

## D. Methodology

**Retrieval dates and versions.** All eight sources were fetched on 2026-06-12: NAFO file dated 2026-06-01 (downloaded, 23,470 bytes, valid xlsx); ICCAT `IUU.xlsx` (most recent record dated 2025-11-22); CCAMLR NCP page (site states last modified 2025-11-12; CP list empty); IOTC PDF dated 2026-05-15 (1,292,646 bytes, 14 pages); NEAFC B list page; WCPFC 2025 list PDF (254,728 bytes); SPRFMO 2026 list PDF; TMT Combined List current-search page (69,986 bytes; 213 records) plus 28 vessel record pages.

**Date repair.** The NAFO Excel stores some "Original Date listed" values as bare years or Excel serials that render as corrupt dates (e.g., `1905-07-08` is the integer 2016 displayed as a date; `42976` is the serial for 2017-08-29). Both forms were converted via the Excel epoch (1899-12-30). Validation example: NAFO's corrupt `1905-07-08` for ANTONY converts to 2016, matching CCAMLR's independently parsed "Year Listed: 2016" for the same vessel.

**Dedup keys, in order:**
1. **IMO number** (7-digit), exact match — after merging, **zero clusters contained conflicting IMOs**.
2. **Normalized name variants**: uppercase, punctuation stripped, "NO." dropped, plus variants split on comma/semicolon/slash/ampersand, plus all former names carried by each record. Tokens like "Unknown"/"Not known" were blocklisted (an early run showed a stray "Previous Names" token would otherwise chain-merge 16 CCAMLR vessels — caught by an IMO-conflict check and fixed).
3. **Spaceless containment** for residual singletons (e.g., TMT "MANJUMATHA" = NAFO "MANJU MATHA"; TMT "HAMBAN TOTA EXPRESS" = NAFO "HAMBANTOTA EXPRESS") — 4 merges, each logged and manually reviewed.
4. **Rename joins via TMT record pages** (14 manual unions): TMT's Combined List shows vessels under their *current* identity while RFMO lists keep the *listed* identity; the fetched TMT pages carry an "RFMO Vessel Name" field that bridges them. Example unions: NAJEM AL SHAMAL = ORIENTE NO. 7 (IMO 8430586); VEDRA = ACROS NO. 3 (IMO 7717913); CEVAHIR = GALA I (IMO 8133839); PHOENIX = NO. 2 CHOYU (IMO 8429991); DONG WON NO. 629 = MELILLA NO. 103 (IMO 7809986); VIRILE = MELILLA NO. 101 (IMO 7920182); SHUN CHANG NO. 3 = NO. 3 CHOYU (IMO 8430550); SEYARA = IMUL-A-0168-PTM; NINKI 1 = IMUL-A-0833-KLT; SUBA PATHUM 03 = IMUL-A-0892-KLT (IMO 8444264). These unions recover IMO numbers for 11 vessels that carry none on the RFMO lists themselves.

**Identity decisions (where lists disagree):**
- **LU RONG YUAN YU 101/102/103/105/106/109 vs 810/811/813/815/817:** TMT keeps separate records for the NPFC-listed 10x identities and the IMO-numbered hulls now named 81x, while noting (quoted from the fetched 810 record): *"Two different vessels using the name LU RONG YUAN YU 101 were sighted in the NPFC area. NPFC considers any vessel using that name to be IUU. The trawler with this name that was IUU listed by NPFC is a match on name and MMSI to the previous identity of IMO 8798093…"* This ledger follows TMT and keeps both row sets (rows for 10x and 81x), rather than asserting hull identity the listing bodies have not asserted.
- **MANJU MATHA vs MANJUMATHA:** kept as two rows — the TMT records show different Indian registration numbers (IND-TN-15-MM-5344 vs IND-TN-15-MM-4683) despite identical owner names.
- **ANEKA 228 vs KM. ANEKA 228** and the two **FU HSIANG FA NO. 21** entries (IOTC Nos. 2013001 and 2014008): kept as separate rows because IOTC, ICCAT, NAFO and TMT all maintain separate entries; IOTC states it has no information on whether the two FU HSIANG FA NO. 21 are the same vessel.
- **PESCACISNE 1, PESCACISNE 2** is one CCAMLR entry that may denote two hulls; kept as one row as listed.
- **ZHOU YU 808/809:** NPFC lists them as unknown vessels displaying those names; IOTC cross-lists them as "NPFC 29/30 Unknown" with MMSIs 412671880 and 412401260 (IOTC Nos. 2021009/2021010) — merged on that correspondence.

**Field conflict resolution.** Name shown = current identity (TMT) with listed/former names in parentheses. Flag = most specific non-"Unknown" value across sources, preferring the fetched TMT record (which reflects registry data) over list fields; former flags = union. "First listed" priority: NAFO "Original Date listed" (originating-RFMO date) → CCAMLR "Year listed" → WCPFC/SPRFMO list dates → IOTC PDF entry date or IOTC-number year → TMT original-listing date → ICCAT `DateFrom` last (ICCAT's date field reflects ICCAT (re)publication for cross-listed vessels, e.g. its 2015-10-02 batch date for vessels IOTC first listed in May 2017, so it is used only as a fallback).

**Reconciliation.** 608 source records (NAFO 203 + ICCAT-active 162 + CCAMLR 18 + NEAFC 6 + WCPFC 4 + SPRFMO 1 + TMT 213 + IOTC addition 1) → 214 rows. TMT's 213 current records map onto 211 rows (TMT itself holds two records under the name LU RONG YUAN YU 108, and near-duplicate name-variant records merged); coverage check against the IOTC PDF's 201 entries found every IOTC entry already represented except KEBA (No. 2026001), which was added.

## F. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **"Flag: Unknown" rows and the name-only criterion.** 149 rows (69.6%) record flag "Unknown" because that is what the listing bodies themselves publish — most listed vessels are stateless or of unverifiable registration. For the 118 rows with no IMO/MMSI/registration/IRCS, identification therefore rests on name + flag-as-recorded + listing body + listing date. Where two such vessels share a name (FU HSIANG FA NO. 21), even the listing RFMO cannot say whether they are one hull or two; this ledger inherits that ambiguity and says so per row.
2. **Six RFMO lists were not fetched from their own registries.** CCSBT, GFCM, SEAFO, SIOFA, IATTC and NPFC listings are shown only as carried by NAFO's consolidated file, ICCAT's Excel, IOTC's cross-listings, and TMT record pages — all retrieved today, but secondary for those bodies. A vessel listed *only* by one of those six and missed by all four carriers would be absent. The Combined List's claim to aggregate all RFMO lists (M1 row 1) caps the plausible miss count at roughly zero-to-few.
3. **IOTC PDF parsing is partial.** The bilingual, image-bearing PDF defeats clean field extraction; reasons/dates for IOTC-only rows were regex-extracted and abridged. Two TMT-current vessels (EMMANUEL, IOTC-origin listing 2023-05-10; AVE MARIYA, IOTC-origin listing 2022-05-30, both per their fetched TMT records) could **not** be found in the 2026-05-15 IOTC PDF text — either extraction misses or quiet delistings; their rows rest on the TMT record pages alone and are flagged by the absence of "IO" in their Sources cell.
4. **"Currently listed" is a legal status, not a complete census of the dark fleet.** Roughly 75% of industrial fishing activity detected by SAR is not publicly tracked (Paolo et al. 2024, *Nature*; see M1 gaps §2), and almost none of those dark vessels are on any IUU list. This ledger is the *named, listable* tip; treating it as the dark fleet's full extent would be a category error. Conversely, listing is not adjudicated guilt — several entries rest on single sightings, and flag states have contested listings.
5. **List hygiene cuts against operational use.** At least three listed identities are reported by registry data (via TMT records) as physically gone — DONG WON NO. 629 (sunk 2000), VEDRA ex ACROS NO. 3 (scuttled 2001), SHUN CHANG NO. 3 (broken up 2020) — yet remain listed; the lists partly memorialize identities rather than track hulls. M3 ownership attribution must therefore target the *operator at time of offence* as well as current registry owners.
6. **Composition skew.** IOTC's mass listings of small Indian and Sri Lankan gillnetters (2019 and 2022–2025 batches) contribute ~120 rows — more than half the ledger — while a single industrial Chinese squid fleet (the LU RONG/ZHOU YU/NPFC cluster, 45 NPFC rows) is the largest industrial bloc. Raw vessel counts per flag or per RFMO are therefore poor proxies for catch volume or economic harm; M4 must weight, not count.
7. **Dates are heterogeneous.** "First listed" mixes day-precision dates, month-years, bare years, and IOTC-number year prefixes; ICCAT's date field was demonstrated unreliable for cross-listings (methodology §D). Rows sourced from corrupted NAFO serials were repaired arithmetically and one case was cross-validated, but the repair rule itself ([Excel epoch] arithmetic) was not independently verified for every row.
8. **Snapshot risk.** WCPFC's "2025" list is the most recent *adopted* list per its public page (a 2026 revision sits in meeting records, M1 row 5); SPRFMO/IOTC/NAFO versions are 2026-dated. The ledger is internally consistent as of 2026-06-12 but RFMO compliance meetings in mid-2026 will move it.
9. **XING FA identifier conflict, resolved externally.** The SPRFMO 2026 PDF prints "8550740" as an *IMO company number* of a prior owner, while TMT lists it as the vessel's IMO; two independent ship trackers confirm 8550740 is the hull's IMO under both XING FA and its prior identity XIANG HAI ([vesseltracker](https://www.vesseltracker.com/en/Ships/Xing-Fa-8550740.html), [vesselfinder](https://www.vesselfinder.com/vessels/details/8550740), both checked 2026-06-12). One number serving as both in the SPRFMO document is likely a PDF-extraction or drafting artifact; the ledger records 8550740 as the vessel IMO.
10. **TMT record-page links** (28 fetched, the rest GUID links harvested from today's live search page) are deep links into a third-party database that may rotate identifiers; the eight primary list URLs are the durable citations.
