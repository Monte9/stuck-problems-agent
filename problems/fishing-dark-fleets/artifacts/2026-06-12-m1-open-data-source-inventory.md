## B. Gaps — data referenced in the literature but not (fully) publicly reachable

1. **Beneficial-ownership coverage is the binding constraint, not detection.** Shareholder information exists for only ~16% of vessels implicated in IUU fishing, and almost no country requires beneficial-ownership disclosure at vessel registration or licensing — so even perfect list-joining will leave most M2 vessels ownerless at the *beneficial* (vs. registered) level. Source: [FTC, "Fishy Networks," Oct 2022](https://financialtransparency.org/fishy-networks-challenges-uncovering-beneficial-owners-behind-illegal-fishing-global-south-countries/) (fetched landing page this run).
2. **Most industrial fishing activity is absent from public AIS tracking.** GFW's Sentinel-1 analysis found ~75% of industrial fishing vessels are not publicly tracked, meaning AIS-based enrichment in M2/M4 systematically under-observes exactly the fleet segment of interest. Sources: [ESA summary of Paolo et al. 2024, *Nature*](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-1/Sentinel-1_and_AI_reveal_75_of_fishing_vessels_not_tracked); [GFW dataset announcement, 2024-05-31](https://globalfishingwatch.org/platform-update/2024-may-data-download-portal-new-dataset-released-featuring-vessel-detections-from-sentinel-1-sar/) (fetched this run).
3. **No machine-readable consolidated EU carding registry exists, and EUR-Lex blocks automated retrieval.** Card statuses live in scattered OJ decisions and press releases; EUR-Lex returned HTTP 202/empty to every automated fetch attempted this run, and the NGO tracker's (IUU Watch) textual snapshot still reflects August 2021 aggregates (verified this run, rows 18–19). M4 will need to reconstruct the carding record decision-by-decision from press releases and secondary trackers.
4. **Corporate-registry access is being enclosed.** OpenCorporates served an hCaptcha challenge to every automated request this run (row 15), and its API requires a key; many national registries (e.g., China's) require local-language navigation or payment. This directly threatens M3 throughput and corroborates FTC's finding of registry fragmentation ([FTC, Oct 2022](https://financialtransparency.org/reports/fishy-networks-uncovering-companies-individuals-behind-illegal-fishing-globally/)).
5. **Subsidy disclosure is weakest where subsidies are largest.** OECD FSE was unreachable to this agent (403, row 26); China — estimated the world's largest fisheries subsidizer at ~$7.2B/yr, mostly capacity-enhancing — publishes no line-item beneficiary-level disclosure usable for M5 joins. Scale source (historical, 2019, not current-state): Sumaila et al., "Updated estimates and analysis of global fisheries subsidies," *Marine Policy* 109 (2019) 103695, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpol.2019.103695. Current beneficiary-level Chinese data: not publicly reachable `[speculative that no mirror exists — to be re-tested in M5]`.
6. **Licensing limits on republication.** Tokyo MOU's APCIS disclaimer prohibits use of its data for external publication without prior permission (fetched this run, row 12) — M2/M4 must cite rather than reproduce APCIS records. GFW downloads sit behind registration and (per its data terms) typically a CC BY-NC license `[license terms not re-verified this run — confirm at M2 download time]`.
7. **Hard identifiers are missing from the lists themselves.** Multiple vessels on the Combined List carry no IMO number (e.g., LU RONG YUAN YU 197, observed this run), and ICCAT's table marks several IMOs "Unknown" — so M2's dedup cannot rely on IMO alone and must fall back to name+flag+listing-body keys.

## C. Limitations & counter-evidence

- **Access verdicts are tooling-relative.** "No (automated)" for OpenCorporates, OECD, IOMOU, and EUR-Lex means this agent's fetch stack was blocked on 2026-06-12; a human browser, an API key, or an institutional mirror may succeed. The inverse also holds: "yes" today is no guarantee for M2 — bot defenses changed within this single run's observations (CBP 403 to one client, 200 to another).
- **Rendering-fetch summaries can err.** One supporting link checked this run (an IUU Watch "carding process" PDF) resolved to a mislabeled, unrelated photo PDF — direct evidence of link rot in this domain. Load-bearing claims (OFAC CSV, NAFO xlsx, WCPFC PDF) were therefore verified by raw download (byte counts and file-type checks recorded in the table); page-level details that rest only on the rendered summary (e.g., exact field names on Paris MOU) should be re-confirmed at point of use.
- **"Last-updated" dates are self-reported by the pages**, not independently verified; the future-leaning dates (IOTC 2026-05-20, NEAFC 2026-05-27, NAFO 2026-06-01) are consistent with the 2026-06-12 run date but were not cross-checked against archived copies.
- **Record counts in the table are as-displayed, not validated.** ICCAT's "162 records" plainly includes historical/delisted entries (the active ICCAT IUU list has historically been far shorter); the Combined List's "~350+" is a page-level observation. M2 must derive active-vessel counts from the downloaded files, not from this inventory.
- **Demonstrated pulls were samples, not full ingests.** Only OFAC (full CSV) and NAFO (full xlsx) were downloaded end-to-end. GFW datasets, Equasis, and both PSC databases were verified live but their record-pull paths (free registration, captcha-gated search) were not exercised; M2 may discover additional friction (rate limits, export caps) behind those gates.
- **Counter-evidence to the framing:** the inventory confirms detection data and listing data are abundant — but two of the three highest-leverage joins (beneficial ownership, subsidy beneficiaries) are exactly where automated access failed this run. If OpenCorporates and OECD/Chinese subsidy data stay closed, M3 will lean more heavily on NGO investigations (FTC, EJF) and Equasis registered-owner records than on primary registries, which lowers the achievable confidence labels defined in the M3 spec.
- **Potential staleness risk:** the FTC "Fishy Networks" analysis (Oct 2022) and NOAA's 2023 report are over 12 months old; they are cited here as inputs to replicate or triangulate, not as current state. Whether NOAA's next biennial report appears on schedule under the post-EO-14276 policy environment is unknown.
