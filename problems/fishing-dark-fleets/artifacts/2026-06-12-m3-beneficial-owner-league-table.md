# M3 — Beneficial-owner league table

**Problem:** fishing-dark-fleets | **Milestone:** 3 | **Date:** 2026-06-12
**Builds on:** M2 consolidated ledger (`artifacts/2026-06-12-m2-consolidated-iuu-vessel-ledger.md`, 214 vessels; "M2 row N" below refers to its table) and the M1 source inventory. No matching skill exists in `.claude/skills/` (spec marks M3 freeform).

## A. What this is

A ranked league table of the owners, operators and ownership networks behind the 214 currently IUU-listed vessels in the M2 ledger, built from owner fields machine-extracted today from five RFMO list publications, CCAMLR's per-vessel ownership histories, TMT Combined-List record pages, the FTC "Fishy Networks" dataset, and investigative/court reporting — replicating and extending FTC's "ten companies own a quarter of IUU vessels" analysis against the *currently listed* fleet.

**Headline numbers (computed from sections C and F):**
- **16 owners/networks ranked**, each tied to specific M2 vessels by name and (where the lists publish one) IMO.
- **107 of 214 M2 vessels (50.0%) now carry at least one named owner or operator** (88 confirmed, 18 probable, 1 reported-only); **107 (50.0%) remain ownerless** — almost all of them vessels the listing bodies themselves record as stateless with owner "unknown."
- **The top 10 networks account for 45 distinct vessels = 21.0% of the entire ledger** — strikingly close to FTC's October 2022 finding that the top 10 companies owned 23.7% of reported IUU vessels ([FTC, "Fishy Networks," Oct 2022, p.26](https://financialtransparency.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/FTC-fishy-Network-OCT-2022-Final.pdf)) — but the two top-10 sets share only **one** vessel-owning company (CNFC), see §E.
- The single largest identified owner of currently RFMO-listed hulls is **Shandong Lanyue Ocean Fishery Co Ltd** (Rongcheng, China) — not any company in FTC's top 10.

**Attribution rule.** A vessel is "attributed" if at least one named owner or operator (current or former, at any confidence) is linked to it by the sources above. Former owners count — M2 §F.5 showed the lists partly memorialize identities, so the operator at time of offence is exactly who accountability mechanisms need.

**Confidence labels (used per attribution, not per row):**
- **confirmed** — named in an official RFMO/registry record, and either corroborated by an organizationally independent source (court, NGO investigation, second registry) or stated identically across separately maintained official records.
- **probable** — official or registry naming exists but the link is name/identity-based, single-provenance, or contested by a flag state.
- **reported-only** — rests on NGO/press reporting with no official registry confirmation. Rows whose *ranking* rests on reported-only evidence are marked **[REPORTED-ONLY]** in bold at the start of the owner cell and italicized.

**Independence caveat, stated once and flagged per row:** IOTC, ICCAT, NAFO and TMT cross-list each other, so an owner name appearing in all four may descend from one originating circular. Rows whose evidence is RFMO-records-only say so in the Evidence column ("RFMO records (shared provenance)"); they still satisfy the two-citation rule formally (two separately maintained official publications) but are weaker than rows with genuinely independent corroboration.

## E. The attribution gap — exact count

**107 of 214 M2 vessels (50.0%) have at least one named owner or operator after this run; 107 of 214 (50.0%) have none.**

Of the 107 attributed: **88 confirmed** (41.1% of the ledger), **18 probable** (8.4%), **1 reported-only** (0.5%). Of the 107 unattributed, the listing bodies themselves publish owner "unknown" — the gap is in the official records, not merely in this run's joins.

Where the ownerless 107 concentrate, and why they resist attribution:
- **The Taiwan-linked stateless tuna fleets of 2013–2017** (~31 vessels): FU HSIANG FA series (15 rows, M2 rows 44–58), SRI FU FA series (7 rows, 169–175), SIN SHUN FA series (4 rows, 164–167), KUANG HSING (2), MAAN YIH HSING, SHUEN SIANG, TIAN LUNG NO. 12 — IOTC listed them with no identifier and owner unknown; no IMO means no GISIS/Equasis join is possible. Their Taiwanese-style names suggest origin but Taiwan's per-vessel shell structure defeats tracing (each vessel "owned" by a separate company — [SIF Investigation 2](https://stopillegalfishing.com/publications/fake-licensing-operation-uncovered-in-tanzania/)).
- **The Zhoushan/Chinese coastal clusters sighted by Japan** (~22 vessels): ZHOU YU 651–661 + 808/809 (11 rows), LIAO YUAN YU 071/072/9 (3), ZHE LING YU LENG (2), ZHEXIANG YU 23029, YUANDA 6/8, HAI DA 705, LU RONG YU 612, LU RONG YUAN YU YUN 958 — NPFC's own owner column reads "Not known" for these ([NPFC IUU list 2025](https://www.npfc.int/system/files/2025-04/2025%20NPFC%20IUU%20Vessel%20List_20250327.pdf)); the sighted hulls hid names, ports, or transmitted falsified MMSIs.
- **Pre-2012 ICCAT/IATTC legacy listings** (~25 vessels): BIGEYE, BRAVO, CAMELOT, OCEAN DIAMOND, ORCA, REYMAR 6, TA FU NO. 1, WEN TENG NO. 688, LILA NO. 10, MARIA, etc. — listed before owner fields were systematically populated; several hulls likely no longer exist (M2 §F.5: DONG WON NO. 629 reported sunk in 2000, VEDRA scuttled 2001 — both also ownerless here, though DONG WON NO. 629's name implies a Korean corporate history this run could not document `[speculative]`).
- **Indian/Sri Lankan gillnetters without owner fields** (~12 of the ~36 such vessels): ARARAT/RESHMITHA, AVE MARIYA, DEWLI FISHING KUDAWELLA, EMMANUEL, GOD BLESS, LITTLESHA, MARIYAL, NOVA, YONA, etc. — the IOTC/ICCAT records carry registration numbers but no owner names for these specific hulls (most of their sister vessels *are* attributed to named individuals).
- **Access friction documented in M1 compounds all of the above**: OpenCorporates served captchas to automated access and Equasis sits behind registration (M1 rows 14–15), so corporate-registry joins that might convert "probable" to "confirmed" — or crack the shell companies — were not executable this run.

FTC found shareholder information existed for only ~16% of vessels implicated in IUU fishing ([FTC, Oct 2022](https://financialtransparency.org/fishy-networks-challenges-uncovering-beneficial-owners-behind-illegal-fishing-global-south-countries/)). This run's 50.0% owner-name coverage is *not* a refutation: most of the 88 "confirmed" names are registered owners — frequently single-purpose shells in Panama, Belize, Djibouti, Oman or Hong Kong — and genuine *beneficial* ownership (a natural person) is documented for only a handful of networks: Vidal family (court record), Yin Yuanhua/Shidao (Chinese corporate registry via JAC), Huang Jia Yi and Chang Lin Pao-Chun (individuals named directly in RFMO records), the Sangsukiam family (investigations), the Indian/Sri Lankan individual owners (~33 vessels), and Zhang Suiming/Zhang Yongren-type registry findings in JAC for non-listed fleet segments. Counting those, **natural-person beneficial owners are identifiable for roughly 50–55 of 214 vessels (~24–26%)** — better than FTC's 16% baseline but on a much smaller, list-defined universe.

## F. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Cross-listing inflates apparent corroboration.** IOTC, ICCAT, NAFO and TMT republish each other's records; for ranks 3, 6, 10, 11, 12 and the single-vessel owners, the "two official citations" may descend from one originating circular. These rows are explicitly labeled "RFMO records (shared provenance)" — they are official attributions, but a single clerical error at origin would propagate. Counter-evidence of exactly this risk: ICCAT spells KIKI's owner field "Unkown" and hedges LUCAS's as "'Unknown' or 'HSIN FEI…'".
2. **The biggest row rests on one research organization for the vessel-link.** Shandong Lanyue's ownership of the 81x hulls comes from TMT's registry research (which cites Chinese registration numbers); the company's existence and group affiliation are corroborated by its parent conglomerate's own site, but no second organization independently ties hulls to company. If TMT's registry read is wrong, rank 1 collapses to "probable" throughout. The same single-organization dependency applies in mirror image to rank 2, which relies on JAC (a TMT/GFW/IMCS joint product) — and JAC itself stresses China's Ministry of Agriculture *disputes* that the sighted hulls were Shidao's vessels. Rank 2 may name a victim of identity theft rather than an owner of offending hulls; the listing bodies deliberately left this open by listing the hulls as stateless.
3. **"Owner" rarely means beneficial owner.** Most confirmed names are registered owners; for High Mountain Overseas, Eastern Holdings, Advantage Company, Belfast Global, Gold Charm et al., the natural persons behind them are undocumented in every retrieved source. Ranking by registered owner can *understate* concentration (one hidden principal may sit behind several shells — investigators allege exactly this for the toothfish fleet) or overstate it (a nominee company may serve unrelated clients).
4. **Listing ≠ adjudicated guilt; attribution ≠ culpability.** Several listings rest on single sightings; flag states have contested listings (M2 §F.4). Naming a registered owner of a listed vessel asserts a documented relationship, not a legal finding against that owner — only the Vidal network (Spanish administrative fines of €17.84M, [COLTO, Dec 2015](https://www.colto.org/2015/12/17/operation-sparrow-investigation-complete-e17-84-million-in-fines/)) and the STS-50/NIKA cases (Indonesian court rulings) carry enforcement outcomes among the ranked rows.
5. **Temporal smear.** The league table mixes owners-at-listing (2004–2026), current registered owners, and former owners. The Vidal row is explicitly historical: the syndicate's principals were fined in 2016 and the vessels have since changed hands; treating rank 5 as a statement about 2026 ownership would be wrong. M5 (subsidy linkage) must use the owner contemporaneous with the subsidy, which this table supports via the per-row date context but does not fully resolve.
6. **Source ages.** FTC (Oct 2022) and JAC (Oct 2023) are both >12 months old and are used for baseline comparison and identity analysis respectively, not as current state; the Operation Sparrow and STS-50/NIKA materials (2015–2019) document historical facts. All RFMO owner fields, CCAMLR ownership histories, and TMT records cited as current were fetched 2026-06-12.
7. **Selection effect cuts against the headline.** §D's "the two channels watch different fleets" finding is partly an artifact of what gets listed: RFMO lists skew toward stateless vessels and small South-Asian gillnetters (M2 §F.6), so *of course* large licensed Chinese corporates are scarce on them. The correct inference is not "Pingtan-type firms stopped offending" but "RFMO listing is structurally blind to flagged corporate fleets" — an inference about the instrument, not the fleet.
8. **Counting judgment calls.** The 50.0%/50.0% split depends on rules stated in §B: counting former owners, counting probable identity-links (Lanyue 10x rows, Hoom Xiang name-pattern rows), and not counting JAC's discounted SHUN HANG/Zhoushan Shunxing match for HAN (M2 row 70). Under the strictest rule (confirmed current owners only), attribution falls to ~41%; under the loosest (any name ever associated), it would rise a few points. The exact-count requirement is met for the stated rule.
9. **What was not done.** No OpenCorporates/Equasis/GISIS direct queries (access barriers per M1), no Chinese national registry pulls beyond what JAC/TMT republish, no outreach to named owners (FTC reported none of its top-10 replied to its requests for comment), and no verification that the ~30 Indian/Sri Lankan individual names are correctly transliterated — RFMO records visibly garble them ("Rajesn Kumarc", "NITHIN S/O SESADIMAI,").
