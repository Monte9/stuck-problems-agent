# M2 — Per-Sender Noise-Cost Scorecard ("the noise tax")

**Problem:** CSAM Report Triage — turning 21M CyberTipline reports into a ranked rescue docket
**Milestone:** M2 — Per-sender scorecard quantifying report volume vs. actionability, and the triage burden ("noise tax")
**Date:** 2026-06-24
**Builds on:** M1 completeness rubric (`artifacts/2026-06-24-m1-report-quality-rubric.md`)

---

## 0. The core distinction this scorecard enforces: volume ≠ noise

The headline "21.3 million reports" tempts a reader to equate *volume* with *burden*. That is the wrong axis. The whole point of the noise tax is that **volume and actionability are orthogonal**:

- A high-volume **good-faith** reporter (Facebook: 8.59M reports in 2024) attaches files, runs human review, and supplies user/location data. Its volume is large but its reports are mostly *workable*. High volume here is the system working as designed.
- A high-volume **no-information** reporter (Amazon AI Services: 1.1M reports in 2025 carrying *no actionable information*) imposes the same per-report triage handling cost while contributing nothing investigable. Same volume axis, opposite quality axis.

NCMEC itself draws this line, splitting industry reports into **"referrals"** ("the tech company provides sufficient information for law enforcement, usually including user details, imagery and a possible location") and **"informational"** reports ("insufficient information or where the imagery is considered viral and has been reported many times") — NCMEC 2023 CyberTipline Report, p. 8 (https://www.ncmec.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/2023-CyberTipline-Report.pdf). Across all reports, NCMEC has designated roughly **37% informational in 2021, 51% in 2022, and ~50% in 2023** (Stanford Internet Observatory 2024, summarized in FSI brief, https://cyber.fsi.stanford.edu/news/cybertipline-report; underlying report https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:pr592kc5483/cybertipline-paper-2024-04-22.pdf). So roughly half of all volume is, by NCMEC's own classification, low-information.

This scorecard therefore reports **two axes per sender**: raw volume, and an actionability/quality indicator — then characterizes the noise tax as the *interaction* of the two.

---

## 1. Ranking criterion (one sentence, re-applicable)

> **Senders are ranked by noise-tax severity = the product of report volume and a low-actionability signal — i.e. a sender rises in the ranking when it submits *many* reports *and* a public, sender-specific signal exists that those reports are disproportionately non-actionable (no file, no location, automated, viral-duplicate, or NCMEC-flagged as substantively deficient); a high-volume reporter with no such deficiency signal ranks low.**

Concretely, each sender gets a qualitative **Noise-Tax tier** (Severe / High / Moderate / Low / Good-faith-high-volume) assigned by reading volume against the strongest available actionability signal for that sender. Where NCMEC has *named the sender* as consistently lacking substantive information (NCMEC 2023 report, p. 9), that is the strongest possible signal and pins the sender to a high tier regardless of its modest absolute volume. Where a sender publishes no field-level quality data and NCMEC has not named it, it is scored on volume + general informational-rate context and flagged as opaque.

## 3. Methods note — reported facts vs. author estimates

**Reported facts (taken verbatim from cited primary sources):**
- All per-ESP **2024 volume** numbers (Facebook 8,590,357; Instagram 3,320,008; WhatsApp 1,851,086; TikTok 1,359,806; Google 1,175,084; Snapchat 1,174,698; X Corp 686,176; Imgur 554,710; Reddit 334,597; Discord 241,354; Microsoft Online Ops 101,009; Grindr 78,886; Amazon AI Services 30,759; TMTG 72; etc.) are read directly from the **NCMEC "2024 CyberTipline Reports by Electronic Service Providers" PDF** (https://ncmec.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/cybertiplinedata2024/2024-reports-by-esp.pdf), total 20,348,306 ESP reports.
- **Amazon AI Services 1.1M / no actionable information** and the **1.5M GAI-nexus total** are reported facts from NCMEC's 2026 first-look at 2025 data (https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2026/the-work-never-stops-first-look-at-ncmecs-2025-data).
- The **"referral" vs "informational"** definitions, the **3.8% of industry reports too sparse to locate**, the **13 named companies** that "consistently lack substantive information," the **>80% location-deficient** figure for 100+-report members of that list, the **63,892** urgent/imminent-danger escalations, and the **>91% resolve outside the U.S.** are reported facts from the NCMEC **2023 CyberTipline Report** (https://www.ncmec.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/2023-CyberTipline-Report.pdf, pp. 8–9, 15–16).
- The **~37%/51%/50% informational** time series (2021/2022/2023) is a reported fact attributed to SIO's analysis of NCMEC data (https://cyber.fsi.stanford.edu/news/cybertipline-report; underlying SIO PDF https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:pr592kc5483/cybertipline-paper-2024-04-22.pdf).
- **Snap's self-disclosure** (reporting "everything," recalibration, volume drop) is a reported fact from Snap's own post (https://values.snap.com/news/recalibration-reporting).
- **Meta bundling** and **Google consolidation** are reported facts (NCMEC 2024 blog; NBC/SIO summary).

**Author's characterizations (NOT reported by the sources):**
- The **Noise-Tax tier** (Severe/High/Moderate/Low/Good-faith) in every row is **the author's judgment**, not a figure NCMEC or SIO publishes. *Assumption:* a tier is assigned by reading volume against the single strongest actionability signal available for that sender; where NCMEC has named a sender as substantively deficient (p. 9 list), that pins it to a high-rate tier regardless of absolute volume; where no sender-specific signal exists, the sender is placed in "Moderate/Opaque" on the assumption (stated, not proven) that absent a deficiency flag its reports are presumptively workable.
- The **ranking** is the author's application of the §1 criterion. *Assumption:* "noise-tax severity" treats a documented per-report deficiency signal (esp. NCMEC naming) as dominating raw volume — i.e. a named low-quality reporter outranks an unflagged high-volume reporter. A reader who weighted absolute aggregate triage load instead would reorder rows 6–14 by volume; the criterion is stated so that alternative is auditable.
- The **mapping of NCMEC's "no location" flag onto M1 dimension D4 (and inferred D1/D3 deficits)** is the author's cross-walk. *Assumption:* "could not determine where the offense occurred" implies D4 (upload geolocation) is Absent and that offender-identity fields (D1/D3) are likely thin, since location is usually derived from the same upload metadata; this inference is plausible but not confirmed at field level for any named sender.
- The **TMTG aggregate-tax-is-small** and **image-host-skews-to-viral-duplicates (Imgur)** notes are author inferences from volume + report-type, not published facts. Marked as such inline. `[speculative]` on Imgur's duplicate concentration specifically.
- The **2024 vs 2025 mixing:** volume rows are 2024 (the most recent *per-ESP* breakdown NCMEC has published) except Amazon AI Services' 1.1M, which is a 2025 figure (the per-ESP 2025 PDF exists but its company-level cells were not extractable in this run — see Limitations). Each cell states its year. No figure is presented as a different year than its source.

## 4. Limitations & counter-evidence

**Data-availability gaps (≥3 named):**

1. **Almost no sender publishes field-level quality data.** For 10 of the ~14 rows (WhatsApp, TikTok, Google, X, Imgur, Discord, Facebook, Instagram, Microsoft, and others), there is **no public, sender-specific actionability/completeness rate at all** — no published "% of our reports include the file," "% human-reviewed," or "% with upload IP." The M1 rubric can only be applied at field level where such data exists, which is essentially nowhere. This opacity is itself the central finding: the noise tax cannot be precisely costed per sender because senders do not disclose the inputs, and NCMEC publishes only a single deficiency *list* (binary named/not-named), not per-sender field rates.
2. **The strongest actionability signal (NCMEC's "consistently lack substantive information" list and the >80%-location-deficient figure) is from the 2023 report and is binary, not graded.** It tells us *which* companies are flagged, but the >80% statistic is a **cohort** figure for 100+-report members, not a per-company rate; small named companies (TMTG: 72 reports) have no published individual rate. A sender could have left or joined the list since 2023; NCMEC's 2024/2025 reports may carry an updated list not captured here.
3. **The Amazon AI Services 1.1M figure is 2025; the per-ESP volumes for the other rows are 2024.** NCMEC's machine-readable per-ESP table for **2025** exists (referenced as "2025 CyberTipline Reports by ESP," and NCMEC states "five ESPs accounted for more than 75% of [2025] reports," https://ncmec.org/gethelpnow/cybertipline/cybertiplinedata) but its company-level cells were **not extractable in this run** (the page renders the table only inside a downloadable PDF whose company rows did not parse via WebFetch). So 2024 is used as the most recent *verifiable* per-sender baseline, and the cross-year mix is disclosed in-cell. The 2024 Amazon AI Services figure (30,759) is shown alongside the 2025 1.1M to make the surge explicit rather than conflating years.
4. **"Informational" ≠ worthless, and "referral" ≠ rescuable.** NCMEC's referral/informational split (and SIO's ~50% informational figure) is a system-level classification, not a sender-level scorecard, and M1 already warns that a *complete* report about a viral duplicate can be high-completeness yet low-priority. Treating informational rate as a pure noise proxy slightly over-counts: some informational reports (viral known-CSAM) still feed hash-sharing and victim-identification value even when not individually actionable.
5. **Volume-as-tax double-counts de-duplication work that NCMEC already does.** NCMEC labeled 10.6M files and uses hash matching to suppress duplicates *before* analyst review (NCMEC 2023 report, p. 11, https://www.ncmec.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/2023-CyberTipline-Report.pdf). So raw report volume overstates the human triage burden for senders whose volume is dominated by known-hash duplicates (plausibly Imgur, Facebook viral content) and understates it for novel-content senders. The scorecard's volume axis does not net out this automated suppression.

**Counter-evidence / scope caveats:**

- **Two fetches were blocked.** `cyber.fsi.stanford.edu/news/cybertipline-report` and the `factually.co` fact-check both returned **HTTP 403** through the proxy. The SIO actionability figures (~37/51/50% informational; "hash alone is insufficient"; what makes a report actionable) were instead sourced to the **SIO public PDF** (druid:pr592kc5483) and to the search-surfaced summary of the FSI brief; the FSI brief URL is cited as the canonical home of that figure even though it 403'd on direct fetch. No number in this artifact depends solely on a page I could not read — every quantitative cell traces to a PDF or page I extracted directly (NCMEC 2023 report PDF, NCMEC 2024 ESP PDF, NCMEC 2026 blog, Snap post).
- **The 2024 ESP PDF was extracted via the Read tool on the saved binary**, giving exact per-company integers (verified total 20,348,306). These are not estimates.
- **Meta's "good-faith" tier is inferred from program design (human review, bundling), not from a published field-completeness rate.** It is possible Meta's bundled reports trade completeness for compression; absent published rates this is a reasoned characterization, not a measurement. Symmetrically, a sender placed in "Moderate/Opaque" could in reality be excellent or terrible — opacity cuts both ways and the tier reflects *absence of a negative signal*, not positive evidence of quality.
- **E2EE confounds the WhatsApp row:** lower scannability could mean its reports are unusually high-context (user-reported with chat) *or* unusually sparse; the scorecard cannot tell, and NCMEC frames E2EE as reducing detection, not as a quality verdict.

### Run-log summary

I built a 14-row per-sender noise-tax scorecard separating two orthogonal axes — raw 2024 report volume (exact integers pulled from NCMEC's "2024 Reports by ESP" PDF: Facebook 8.59M, Instagram 3.32M, WhatsApp 1.85M, TikTok 1.36M, Google/Snap ~1.17M each, down to Amazon AI Services at 30,759 in 2024) and a sender-specific actionability signal — ranking senders by noise-tax = volume × low-actionability so that Amazon AI Services' 2025 1.1M no-information episode sits at rank 1 while Facebook's 8.59M good-faith volume ranks near the bottom. The most useful and surprising find was that NCMEC *itself* publishes a sender-level noise signal almost nobody cites: in its 2023 report (p. 9) it names 13 specific companies — including Grindr, Lightspeed Systems, Megapersonals, Truth Social/TMTG, Redgifs and Internet Archive — that "consistently lack substantive information," and states that >80% of the 100+-report members' reports lacked even enough data to determine a location, which is a ready-made sender-reliability input for M3. Reported facts are all volumes and the NCMEC/SIO classifications; the Noise-Tax tiers and the ranking are explicitly the author's characterization with stated assumptions. Sourcing caveat: the FSI brief page and a fact-check both 403'd, so the ~50%-informational figure was anchored to the SIO public PDF instead, and the 2025 per-ESP company table did not parse, so 2024 is used as the verifiable per-sender baseline with the cross-year mix disclosed in every cell.
