## Executive summary (for policy staff and funders)

The CyberTipline received ~21.3M reports in 2025, roughly half of which NCMEC's own classification treats as low-information, and a single sender (Amazon AI Services) filed 1.1M reports "containing no actionable information" (NCMEC 2026 first-look, https://www.missingkids.org/blog/2026/the-work-never-stops-first-look-at-ncmecs-2025-data; M2 §2 row 1). The four prior milestones surfaced a concrete menu of fixes. This map sorts each by **who must move** — and the central, fundable finding is that the two highest-leverage fixes live on opposite sides of the line.

**Highest-leverage fix implementable today (no statute needed): a shared rescue-priority triage layer + sender-reliability prior** (Fixes 7 + 9). NCMEC and platforms already hold every authority and data input M3 needs to rank reports by imminence × novelty × jurisdiction × sender reliability, and M4 confirms the schema can be built today *if* it is designed to score from hash/field metadata without opening never-viewed files (M4 §3 agnostic-design rules). This converts the existing 63,892-per-year manual "urgent" escalation (NCMEC 2023 report, p. 9) into a documented, auditable docket — the core deliverable — with zero legislative dependency. It is the single fix a funder can resource immediately and an engineering team can ship.

**Highest-leverage fix needing statutory change: make the quality fields mandatory, not discretionary** (Fix 1). The root cause M1 isolates is the §2258A (a)-mandatory / (b)-discretionary split: the law compels the *volume* of reporting but makes every quality field — the file itself, identity, geolocation, timestamp, the full communication — optional "at the sole discretion of the provider" (18 U.S.C. 2258A(b), https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2258A; M1 §1). No platform best-practice and no NCMEC guidance can close this, because NCMEC is structurally barred from issuing binding quality standards itself: doing so risks the "platforms as government agents" argument that could make millions of reports inadmissible under the private-search doctrine (M1 §4; M4 C2 *Ackerman*). Only Congress can require the fields without creating that Fourth-Amendment exposure. This is the appropriations-and-authorization ask.

**The map is not one-sided.** Of 13 fixes, 5 are implementable today, 5 need statutory/regulatory change, and 3 are genuinely mixed (an actionable core plus a statutory ceiling). The practical message for funders: fund the triage layer now; the legislative asks are real but slower, and several "today" fixes (deduplication, sender feedback loops, agnostic schema design) deliver value while the statutory ones are pending.

## Limitations & counter-evidence

### Fixes whose actor-attribution is genuinely ambiguous (≥2 required)

1. **Fix 8 (jurisdiction filter / foreign-referral routing) — ambiguous because the *act* is TODAY but the *channels* are statutory.** NCMEC can build and apply a jurisdiction filter and already refers >91% of reports abroad via Interpol I-24/7 (M2 diagnostic row; NCMEC 2023 report pp. 15–16) — so the triage/filtering action is implementable today. But *who NCMEC may share a prioritized queue with* is fixed by 18 U.S.C. 2258A(g)'s permitted-disclosure list (M4 C7). If routing requires sharing with a recipient outside that list, the fix needs a statutory change; if it stays within enumerated recipients, it does not. The attribution therefore depends on the routing target, which the public record does not fully specify — so I cannot cleanly assign one actor.

2. **Fix 10 (sender-quality feedback loop) — ambiguous because the loop is TODAY but its content is statutorily capped.** NCMEC already notifies deficient companies (M2 §2), so the feedback mechanism exists and can be expanded by NCMEC unilaterally. But 2258A(g) bars returning visual depictions to providers (M4 C7), so the *substance* a feedback loop can carry is statutorily limited to metadata. Whether the metadata-only version is "enough" to actually change platform behavior — versus needing a statutory carve-out to share more — is unknown, so the actor is split between NCMEC (today, metadata) and Congress (if more is needed). `[speculative]` on whether metadata-only feedback is sufficient to move sender behavior.

3. **Fix 7 (the triage layer itself) — ambiguous because it is "TODAY" only conditional on two other fixes.** M3 is buildable today (Fix 7 = TODAY), but M4 §3 shows it is *lawfully* buildable today only if it adopts the agnostic design discipline (Fix 11), and its evidentiary durability is undercut until the circuit split is resolved (Fix 13, STATUTE). So Fix 7's clean "TODAY" classification borrows stability from a fix that is "STATUTE." I classified it TODAY (the build does not require a statute), but a reader could fairly call it MIXED because its full value depends on a statutory fix.

### Counter-evidence and scope caveats

- **The TODAY/STATUTE line is partly a matter of risk appetite, not pure law.** Several "TODAY" fixes (2, 9, 11) are implementable *because* actors choose the conservative, voluntariness-preserving path. A more aggressive reading — e.g. NCMEC itself issuing binding quality standards — might "work" operationally but would manufacture the agent-of-government exposure (M4 C2 *Ackerman*) that the conservative classification exists to avoid. So "implementable today" sometimes means "implementable today *if you accept a 4A risk*," and I have classified to the suppression-avoiding posture M4 recommends.

- **Voluntariness cuts against some statutory fixes.** Fix 1 (mandate quality fields) is the highest-leverage statutory ask, but M1 §4 limitation 5 and M4 warn that courts treat platform reporting as *voluntary* precisely to keep evidence admissible. A poorly-drafted mandate could convert platforms into government agents and *reduce* admissibility — so the statutory fix carries a 4A risk of its own, and "needs statutory change" does not guarantee the statute would be net-positive. `[speculative]` on whether a mandate could be drafted to compel fields without triggering agency status.

- **Weights and tier assignments are unvalidated.** Fixes 7 and 9 inherit M3's `[speculative]` weights and M2's author-assigned tiers; the triage layer is buildable today but cannot be *empirically* tuned until Fix 12 (outcome data, STATUTE) exists. So the "today" fix and the "statute" fix are coupled: the actionable tool is calibration-blind until the legislative one lands.

- **Recency.** Volume context (21.3M; Amazon 1.1M) is from NCMEC's 2026 first-look at 2025 data (current). The 63,892 urgent escalations, >91%-foreign, named-deficiency list, and referral/informational split are from the **NCMEC 2023 CyberTipline Report** (https://www.ncmec.org/content/dam/missingkids/pdfs/2023-CyberTipline-Report.pdf) and the **SIO 2024 blueprint** (April 2024) — both older than 12 months and cited as *design basis*, not current-state statistics. The §2258A(h) preservation period reflects the 1-year figure after the 2024 REPORT Act (M4 C6); older commentary citing 90 days is stale. Statutory text is from Cornell LII (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2258A) as of 2026-06.

- **No new sources introduced.** Every attribution here cross-references a prior milestone artifact and the source that milestone already verified; M5 adds synthesis, not new primary research, per the spec's "consume, don't redo" instruction. The one place I assert beyond the prior artifacts — that a *third-party NGO* (e.g. Tech Coalition) is the right actor for Fix 2 — names a plausible body but the *specific* organization is `[speculative]`; what is sourced (M1 §4, SIO) is only that the actor must be *non-NCMEC*.

### Run-log summary

I built a 13-fix attribution map synthesizing every fix surfaced across M1–M4 and sorting each into "implementable today by NCMEC/platforms" (6 clean: voluntary quality standard, accurate review-flag discipline, attach-the-file, deduplication/bundling, over-reporting recalibration, the rescue-priority triage layer, the sender-reliability prior, and 4A-agnostic schema design) versus "needs statutory/regulatory change" (4 clean: mandate the quality fields, mandate ICAC outcome reporting, codify NCMEC's status/safe-harbor, plus the disclosure-channel ceilings), with three genuinely mixed rows flagged. The thing that crystallized in synthesis was how *coupled* the two columns are: the highest-leverage today-fix (the triage layer) is calibration-blind until the highest-leverage statute-fix's sibling (ICAC outcome data) exists, and the today-buildable schema only stays lawful because it is deliberately designed around a statutory gap (the unresolved Ackerman/Wilson circuit split) that only Congress or the Supreme Court can close — so the cleanest "NCMEC can do this today" fixes are precisely the ones whose value is capped by the statutory ones, which is exactly the tension a funder needs to see.
