# Benefits non-take-up: the safety net that the eligible never reach

**Domain:** Economics and inequality

## The problem, in plain language

Governments run anti-poverty programs that people qualify for but never receive. Not because they are denied — because they never claim. In the US, roughly one in five EITC-eligible workers miss the credit, leaving about **$7 billion unclaimed per year**, and the average missed credit is ~$2,894 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/earned-income-tax-credit/eitc-reports-and-statistics)). Only **48% of eligible seniors** get SNAP; an estimated **16 million older adults** were eligible but not enrolled in 2022 ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/health/food-insecurity/16-million-eligible-adults-not-enrolled-snap/)). Only ~65% of eligible childless adults are enrolled in Medicaid ([JHU](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2025/despite-progress-medicaid-enrollment-gaps-persist-amid-policy-shifts)). The UK quantifies the whole gap: **£24 billion unclaimed every year**, ~£3,428 per affected household, with Pension Credit claimed by just **62% of eligible pensioners** ([Policy in Practice](https://policyinpractice.co.uk/publication/missing-out-2024/); [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/income-related-benefits-estimates-of-take-up-financial-year-ending-2024)). The human cost is food insecurity, foregone medical care, and avoidable deep poverty among people the legislature already decided to help.

## The half-known solution

The fix is known and proven: **stop asking people to apply, and enroll them by default from data the government already holds.** A 2022 California ACA randomized trial is the clean demonstration. A reminder *nudge* — the popular, cheap, widely-funded intervention — raised take-up of cost-sharing-reduction silver plans by 2.3 points (26%). An *automatic* crosswalk that enrolled eligible households by default raised it by **83 points — an 822% increase**, roughly 30x the nudge ([Health Affairs](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37406241/)). Other countries pre-populate tax returns from employer wage data so the EITC-equivalent is delivered without a form ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-106933)). States already use SNAP data to auto-renew Medicaid ([CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/research/using-snap-data-for-medicaid-renewals-can-keep-eligible-beneficiaries-enrolled)). The inversion the field keeps relearning: **information is not the bottleneck; the default is.**

## Why it's stuck

The US ran the obvious lever and then killed it. IRS Direct File — free, government-run, pre-fillable filing — processed 297,000 returns in 2025 before being suspended in late 2025 and 404'd off the IRS site, with lawmakers calling the $41M program "wasteful" ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/131/Report-Replacement-of-Direct-File-2025.pdf); [The Hill](https://thehill.com/homenews/nexstar_media_wire/5636106-irs-direct-file-is-gone-this-is-how-you-can-still-file-taxes-for-free/)). The structural blockers: data lives in siloed agencies that don't share records; "improper payment" politics punishes over-enrollment far harder than under-enrollment; commercial tax-prep lobbies resist pre-filling; and administrative burden is sometimes a deliberate rationing tool. This is the scurvy/Semmelweis pattern in real time — a proven fix, withdrawn for non-technical reasons.

## The AI-agent desk-research wedge

A model running unsupervised for hours can build the artifact nobody is staffed to assemble: a **program-by-program non-take-up ledger** — for the top US federal/state programs (EITC, SNAP, Medicaid, Medicare Savings Programs, WIC), the eligible-but-unenrolled count, dollars left on the table, the specific administrative-burden step that loses people, and whether an existing data source (employer W-2s, SNAP rolls, IRS AGI) could pre-populate it. Then a ranked list of "data-match wins available now" — pairs of programs where one's enrollment file already proves eligibility for another, with the legal authority and precedent state cited for each. This is synthesis of public statistics, GAO reports, and trial results, not new fieldwork — exactly the missing document a state Medicaid director or a Treasury official could act on within a year.

## Who is closest today

Policy in Practice (UK) maintains the £24B ledger and benefit calculators. Code for America built and ran GetCalFresh and the Direct File technology. CBPP and the Benefits Data Trust pioneered cross-program data matching. Academics Saurabh Bhargava and Manasi Deshpande quantify administrative burden. The gap none of them fills: a single, current, cross-program US ledger that names the specific data-match each program could use and ranks them by dollars recoverable — the document that turns a known fix into an agency to-do list.
