# Problem: uncontrolled rocket-body reentries (risk the launchers export and nobody is billed for)

> Intake brief from the scout routine, 2026-06-26. Category: frontier expansion (space side).
> Bottleneck type: misaligned incentives — controlled deorbit costs the operator payload mass and money, while the casualty risk lands on people under the flight path who never consented.

**(a) The problem.** When a rocket lifts a satellite, the spent upper stage or core stage is often left in orbit to fall back uncontrolled, weeks to years later, scattering surviving metal across a random ground track. The industry's own safety standard — adopted by the US, France, Japan and ESA — is that any reentry with a casualty risk above [1-in-10,000 should be done as a controlled deorbit](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468896724000077). Yet [84% of uncontrolled rocket-body reentries from 2010-2022 exceeded that 1-in-10,000 threshold](https://www.cnr.it/en/focus/074-60/casualty-risk-from-the-uncontrolled-reentry-of-rocket-bodies-and-satellites). The aggregate stakes: [a ~10% chance of one or more casualties over the next decade](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-022-01718-8) from current reentry rates, plus a rising aviation hazard — The Aerospace Corporation projects the probability of a fatal aircraft collision with reentry debris rising from ~1-in-100,000/year toward [7-in-10,000/year by 2035 once megaconstellations are included](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094576524002807), and busy airspace such as the northeastern US has a [26% annual chance of being affected by an uncontrolled reentry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84001-2). The dramatic inversion: the risk is not borne by the launchers. The latitudes of [Jakarta, Dhaka, Lagos, Bogota and Mexico City are at least three times as likely to have a rocket body reenter over them as Washington, New York, Beijing or Moscow](https://news.ubc.ca/2022/07/space-rocket-junk-deadly/) — major launching states export the hazard to the Global South. In 2023 alone, [128 rocket bodies were abandoned in orbit to reenter uncontrolled](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84001-2).

**(b) The half-known solution.** Controlled deorbit. The technology is mature and already routine for the operators who choose it: propulsive deorbit burns, retained fuel reserves, and design-for-demise. In 2019-2023, [controlled reentries accounted for ~62% of returned mass, including 31% from Falcon 9 second stages alone](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468896724000077). The fix is not invention; it is making the rest of the fleet do what SpaceX's Falcon 9 already does. The risk is also highly concentrated, which makes the fix tractable: [Chinese upper stages account for ~62% of total casualty risk and Russian/Soviet stages ~18%](https://www.cnr.it/en/focus/074-60/casualty-risk-from-the-uncontrolled-reentry-of-rocket-bodies-and-satellites). A short list of vehicle families drives most of the hazard.

**(c) Why it's stuck.** Controlled deorbit costs payload mass and money, and the casualty risk is an externality dumped on uninvolved populations and aircraft. The [FAA proposed in September 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468896724000077) to require US launch operators to dispose of upper stages within 30 days — but the rule covers only US-licensed launches, the worst actors are foreign, and the international standard is a non-binding recommendation no body enforces. Under the 1967 Outer Space Treaty a launching state retains liability in perpetuity, so there is a liability regime but no enforcement and no price signal. This is Semmelweis-shaped: the safety threshold is published, the compliant technique is in routine use, and the gap is that the people setting launch budgets are not the people standing under the debris.

**(d) The AI-agent wedge.** Pure desk synthesis on open catalogs. An agent could build the artifact that does not exist: a public, per-launch-vehicle reentry-compliance ledger. Using the [public satellite catalog / reentry records](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-84001-2), it could classify every abandoned rocket body of the past decade as controlled vs. uncontrolled, estimate per-reentry casualty risk from published mass/demise models, attribute aggregate risk by operator, vehicle family and launching state, and overlay ground-track probability against gridded population and major-airport airspace to quantify exported risk by country. The output is a ranked accountability docket — which specific vehicle families to target, how much risk each clean-up would retire, and which states bear risk they did not create — the exact evidence base the FAA, ICAO, and UN COPUOS lack to move the standard from recommendation to rule. The agent can produce this; it cannot pass the rule.

**(e) Closest to cracking it.** Michael Byers and Aaron Boley's [Outer Space Institute](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-022-01718-8) (the casualty-inequity analysis and an open-letter campaign for a multilateral controlled-reentry treaty); The Aerospace Corporation (aviation-collision modeling); the [FAA's stalled upper-stage disposal NPRM](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468896724000077); ESA's Space Safety Programme and its zero-debris charter; and UN COPUOS, which set up a new SSA expert group in 2025. The science is settled and the fix is proven; the missing piece is the attribution document that names the noncompliant vehicles and prices the exported risk.
