# Problem: fabricated "zombie trials" inside the medical evidence base

> Intake brief from the scout routine, 2026-06-10. Domain: scientific literature synthesis.
> Bottleneck type: sheer grind nobody is staffed to do, compounded by misaligned incentives (journals, publishers, universities).

**(a) The problem.** Systematic reviews and meta-analyses are the top of the evidence pyramid; clinical guidelines worldwide are built on them. A meaningful fraction of the randomized trials feeding them appear to be fabricated or fatally flawed. When *Anaesthesia* editor John Carlisle checked individual patient data for trials submitted to his journal, [44% contained false data and 26% were outright "zombies"](https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/anae.15263); Ioannidis estimates [hundreds of thousands of zombie trials circulate](https://associationofanaesthetists-publications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/anae.15297). Ben Mol's audits of women's health RCTs put the untrustworthy share at [20–30%, with 200+ retractions to his name](https://trustworthy-research.com/about-ben-w-mol/). The harm is concrete: Joachim Boldt's ~184 retracted papers propped up hydroxyethyl starch for fluid resuscitation; [meta-analysis excluding Boldt showed HES increases mortality and renal failure](https://retractionwatch.com/2013/02/19/does-scientific-misconduct-cause-patient-harm-the-case-of-joachim-boldt/) — it had been infused into millions of patients. The ivermectin-for-COVID debacle was [driven by meta-analyses containing fraudulent trials](https://restores.univ-rennes.fr/zombie-trials).

**(b) The half-known solution.** Detection methods exist and work: Carlisle's baseline-balance test, Heathers/Brown's GRIM/SPRITE, Mol's TRACT checklist, registry-vs-publication cross-checks. In September 2025 a 150-expert consortium led by Manchester's Jack Wilkinson published [INSPECT-SR](https://www.cochrane.org/about-us/news/new-tool-detects-problematic-trials-they-distort-evidence), a validated tool for screening trial trustworthiness during systematic reviews, now [submitted for Cochrane endorsement](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-09-tool-unreliable-trials.html). Cochrane's pregnancy-and-childbirth pilot showed that screening changes review conclusions. The fix is known: screen every trial entering a review, and re-run existing influential meta-analyses with suspect trials excluded.

**(c) Why it's stuck.** Scale and incentives. Forensic assessment takes expert-hours per trial; ~30,000+ RCTs publish yearly, and the back catalog of guideline-anchoring meta-analyses numbers in the tens of thousands. The best-funded dedicated effort, the [Medical Evidence Project](https://retractionwatch.com/2025/06/04/medical-evidence-project-grant-open-philanthropy-center-scientific-integrity-human-health/) ($900K from Open Philanthropy, June 2025, ~5 investigators), targets *ten* compromised meta-analyses per year. Journals take years to retract and [threaten critics with lawyers](https://retractionwatch.com/2021/08/10/critics-face-legal-threats-as-journal-takes-more-than-three-years-to-act/); universities protect their own; publishers earn nothing by retracting. This is Semmelweis-shaped: the data exist, the institutions that must act are the ones embarrassed by acting.

**(d) The AI-agent wedge.** This is desk forensics — almost entirely automatable triage. An agent running unsupervised for hours could: extract baseline tables and summary statistics from thousands of published RCTs and run Carlisle/GRIM-style plausibility checks; cross-reference ClinicalTrials.gov registrations against publications for impossible recruitment timelines, outcome switching, and duplicate cohorts; map which Cochrane reviews and WHO/NICE guideline recommendations cite already-retracted or flagged trials (extending [RetractoBot](https://retractobot.net)'s logic); and re-compute meta-analyses with suspect trials removed, flagging the subset whose conclusions flip — the shortlist human investigators like Heathers' team actually need. It cannot retract papers or compel journals, but it can convert a hundreds-of-thousands-paper haystack into a ranked, evidence-backed docket.

**(e) Closest to cracking it.** Jack Wilkinson / Cochrane (INSPECT-SR adoption); James Heathers' Medical Evidence Project at the Center for Scientific Integrity; Ben Mol (TRACT, women's health); John Carlisle; Guillaume Cabanac's Problematic Paper Screener; Lisa Bero on integrity in evidence synthesis. Tools are built; nobody is staffed to run them across the existing evidence base.
