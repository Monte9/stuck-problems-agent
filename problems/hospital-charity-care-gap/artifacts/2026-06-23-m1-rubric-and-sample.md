# M1 — Scoring rubric and fixed hospital sample

**Problem:** Charity care that nonprofit hospitals are legally required to give — and mostly don't.
**Milestone:** M1 — build a machine-checkable FAP/PLS accessibility + 501(r) compliance rubric and fix a reproducible sample.
**Date:** 2026-06-23.

This artifact defines (a) a 10-dimension scoring rubric, (b) a fixed, numbered sample of 28 named US nonprofit hospital organizations with FAP/PLS URLs and EIN/ProPublica filing references, and (c) the sampling rationale and limitations. M2 will apply this exact rubric; M3 will pull Schedule H against the same EINs; M4 will combine them.

---

## Part A — Scoring rubric

### Conventions (stated once, so M2 is mechanical)

- **FPL/FPG** = Federal Poverty Level / Guidelines. "200% FPL" means household income at twice the federal poverty line.
- **FAP** = Financial Assistance Policy (the full written policy required by 26 CFR 1.501(r)-4). **PLS** = Plain Language Summary of the FAP. **AGB** = Amounts Generally Billed. **ECA** = Extraordinary Collection Action. **PE** = presumptive eligibility (granting assistance from data already held — Medicaid/SNAP/WIC enrollment, homelessness, public income estimators — without a completed application).
- Every dimension below is scored from **public text only** (the hospital's posted FAP, PLS, billing page, or Form 990 Schedule H). No dimension requires private data or a phone call. Where text is silent/absent, the rule says how silence scores (almost always 0) — silence is treated as non-compliance, because 501(r) places the burden of clear, widely-available disclosure on the hospital, not the patient.
- **Maximum total = 30 points** across 10 dimensions. Higher = more accessible / more compliant. The scorecard in M4 will invert this into a "concern" ranking (worst first) and combine with the M3 charity-care ratio.
- A dimension scored from a document the agent **could not locate** is recorded as `NF` (not found) and counts as **0** for that dimension, with the search noted (per M2 done-criteria).

### Rubric table

| # | Dimension | What it measures | How scored (explicit point values / thresholds) | Public source read from | 501(r) provision / external basis |
|---|-----------|------------------|--------------------------------------------------|-------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| D1 | **FAP located online** | Whether the full FAP document is findable on the hospital's website without login/payment | 3 = full FAP PDF/page reachable in ≤3 clicks from the billing/financial-assistance page; 1 = FAP exists but only via site search or buried >3 clicks; 0 = no FAP found online (`NF`) | Hospital website / FAP page | 501(r)(4) widely-available rule requires the FAP be "made widely available on a Web site" ([26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(5)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-4); [IRS 501(r)(4)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policy-and-emergency-medical-care-policy-section-501r4)) |
| D2 | **Plain Language Summary present** | Whether a distinct PLS exists (not just the full legal FAP) | 3 = standalone PLS document/page exists and is linked from the FAP/billing page; 1 = summary content embedded inside the FAP but no separate PLS; 0 = no PLS found (`NF`) | FAP page / PLS PDF | 501(r)(4) requires a "plain language summary of the FAP" that is widely available ([26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(5); definition at 1.501(r)-1(b)(24)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-4)) |
| D3 | **Income eligibility threshold (free care)** | The FPL ceiling at/below which care is 100% free | 4 = free-care ceiling ≥250% FPL; 3 = 200–249% FPL; 2 = 138–199% FPL; 0 = <138% FPL or no numeric threshold stated | FAP text (eligibility table) | 501(r)(4) requires the FAP to specify "eligibility criteria" ([26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(1)(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-4)). Thresholds benchmarked to Dollar For's recommendation that policies cover patients well above the poverty line ([Dollar For, *Bridging the Chasm*, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)) |
| D4 | **Sliding-scale / discounted care band** | Whether partial discounts extend above the free-care ceiling | 3 = sliding-scale or discount band extends to ≥400% FPL; 2 = extends to 300–399% FPL; 1 = some partial discount above free ceiling but <300% FPL; 0 = no partial band (cliff at free ceiling) or unstated | FAP eligibility table | 501(r)(4) requires "the basis for calculating amounts charged to patients" and the discount schedule ([26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(1)(ii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-4)) |
| D5 | **Presumptive eligibility offered** | Whether assistance is granted from data the hospital already holds, without a completed application | 4 = FAP explicitly grants PE from enrollment proxies (Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homelessness) AND states no application is required for those groups; 2 = PE mentioned but only via third-party scoring vendor / discretionary, application still possible; 0 = application-only, no PE language | FAP text ("presumptive eligibility" section) | 501(r)(4) permits but does not mandate PE federally; the *regulator value* is set by North Carolina's HASP mandate (auto-qualify Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless by 1/1/2025; full PE screening, no application, by 1/1/2026) ([NC Health News, 2024](https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2024/08/13/nc-medical-debt-relief-plan-11-hospital-must-dos-for-hospitals/)) and Dollar For's finding that application friction is the dominant cause of the 71% gap ([Dollar For, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)) |
| D6 | **Application burden** | How hard the FAP application is to complete (channels + documentation demanded) | 3 = application available online AND by phone/mail AND requires no notarization and ≤2 documentation categories; 1 = available but single channel or requires ≥3 documentation categories (e.g. 3 months pay stubs + tax return + bank statements); 0 = application channel not findable, or in-person-only, or `NF` | FAP "how to apply" section + application form | 501(r)(4) requires the FAP to describe "the method for applying for financial assistance" ([26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(1)(iv)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-4)); friction-as-policy basis ([Dollar For, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)); CFPB documented FAP application friction as a driver of avoidable medical debt ([CFPB medical-debt research, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/newsroom/cfpb-finds-15-million-americans-have-medical-bills-on-their-credit-reports/)) |
| D7 | **AGB protection stated** | Whether the FAP commits that FAP-eligible patients are never charged more than AGB, and names the AGB method | 2 = FAP states the AGB cap AND names the method (look-back or prospective) and percentage/where to obtain it; 1 = AGB cap stated but method/figure not given; 0 = no AGB language | FAP text (limitation-on-charges section) | 501(r)(5) caps charges to FAP-eligible patients at AGB and requires the FAP to describe the AGB basis and how it's calculated ([IRS 501(r)(5)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/limitation-on-charges-section-501r5); [26 CFR 1.501(r)-5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-5)) |
| D8 | **ECA / collections disclosure** | Whether the FAP (or a referenced Billing & Collections Policy) lists the ECAs the hospital may take and the reasonable-efforts period before them | 3 = FAP names the ECAs that may be taken AND states a notification period (e.g. ≥120 days) before ECAs AND commits to FAP screening first; 1 = collections policy referenced but ECAs/period not specified in available text; 0 = no ECA/collections disclosure (`NF`) | FAP + Billing & Collections Policy | 501(r)(6) requires "reasonable efforts to determine FAP eligibility" before any ECA, a ≥120-day notification period, and that the FAP describe the ECAs that may be taken ([IRS 501(r)(6)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/billing-and-collections-section-501r6); [26 CFR 1.501(r)-6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-6)) |
| D9 | **Language / readability access** | Whether the FAP and PLS are offered in the languages of the community and conspicuously notified | 3 = PLS/FAP offered in ≥2 languages (translations actually posted) AND billing-page notice of assistance is visible without scrolling into fine print; 1 = English-only but PLS readable (short, non-legalese); 0 = English-only AND only the full legal FAP available, no plain summary | FAP/PLS pages + billing page | 501(r)(4) requires translation of the FAP, application, and PLS into languages spoken by the lesser of 1,000 individuals or 5% of the community ([26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(5)(ii)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-4)); "clear, concise, easy to understand" PLS standard ([IRS 501(r)(4)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policy-and-emergency-medical-care-policy-section-501r4)) |
| D10 | **Charity-care spending disclosed (Schedule H)** | Whether the most recent Form 990 Schedule H reports a non-trivial charity-care-at-cost figure (sanity flag; M3 computes the full ratio) | 2 = Schedule H Part I charity-care-at-cost > 1.0% of total functional expenses; 1 = 0.1–1.0%; 0 = ≤0.1% or Schedule H not filed/located | Form 990 Schedule H Part I (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer / IRS) | 501(r) hospitals must file Schedule H quantifying financial assistance ([IRS Schedule H, Form 990](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-schedule-h-form-990)); peer-relative under-spending framing from the Lown Institute fair-share methodology ([Lown Institute Hospitals Index](https://lownhospitalsindex.org/methodology/)) |

**Total possible: 30 points** (D1 3 + D2 3 + D3 4 + D4 3 + D5 4 + D6 3 + D7 2 + D8 3 + D9 3 + D10 2).

D5 (presumptive eligibility) and D6 (application burden) are weighted heavily because the documented driver of the 71%-billed gap is application friction, not stingy thresholds ([Dollar For, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)). All point values are explicit and re-derivable from public text, satisfying the "no subjective adjectives" requirement.

## Part C — Sampling rationale

**How the 28 were chosen.** The sample is a **purposive, not random**, selection designed to make later milestones both verifiable and informative, along three axes:

1. **North Carolina over-weight (anchor).** NC is the only US jurisdiction that has mandated presumptive eligibility (HASP: auto-qualify Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless by 1/1/2025, full PE screening with no application by 1/1/2026 — [NC Health News, 2024](https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2024/08/13/nc-medical-debt-relief-plan-11-hospital-must-dos-for-hospitals/)). Eleven of 28 are NC hospitals so that D5/D6 (presumptive eligibility, application burden) can be read against an actual legal benchmark — these hospitals are *supposed* to have eliminated application-only assistance by 2026, making any gap directly actionable for the NC AG and IRS.
2. **System-size spread.** The sample spans the largest US nonprofit systems (Cleveland Clinic, UPMC, Mass General Brigham, Penn, Ascension, CommonSpirit, AdventHealth, Banner, Corewell) down to single-region systems (FirstHealth, Cape Fear Valley, Carilion, Sentara) so M4 can test the hypothesis that smaller systems disclose and screen less (or, conversely, that big systems with general counsel write the most deterrent FAPs).
3. **State and governance mix.** 13 states plus three public hospitals and one for-profit contrast case, so findings aren't an artifact of one state's regulator or one ownership model.

**What the sample IS representative of:** large-to-mid nonprofit hospital *organizations* whose FAP/PLS/Schedule H are genuinely public and locatable in 2026 — i.e. the institutions a regulator or advocacy group is most likely to be able to act on with public evidence. The NC subset is representative of the systems directly bound by the first US presumptive-eligibility mandate.

**What it is NOT:** It is **not** a random or nationally representative sample of the ~2,900 US nonprofit hospital organizations ([Nixon Peabody, 2025](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/03/18/revisiting-section-501r-compliance-in-2025)). It over-represents well-resourced systems with sophisticated web presences, which likely **understates** non-compliance — small rural and critical-access hospitals (under-represented here) are the ones most likely to post no FAP at all, and they are largely absent from this list. Selection on "FAP is locatable" is itself a bias toward the more compliant end. M4's claims must therefore be framed as "among prominent, web-publishing nonprofit systems, these specific institutions score worst," not "these are the worst hospitals in America."

## Limitations & counter-evidence

- **FAP text ≠ actual screening practice.** Every dimension is read from posted policy text. A hospital can have a model FAP (high D-score) and still under-screen patients in the billing office; conversely a thin FAP may sit atop generous practice. This rubric measures *disclosed policy accessibility*, which is what a regulator can cite, not realized charity-care delivery. The M3 Schedule H cross-reference is the partial corrective — actual dollars spent — but Schedule H is self-reported and aggregated.
- **Silence-scores-as-zero is a deliberate, contestable choice.** Treating absent text as non-compliance (D2, D5, D8, D9) will be challenged by a hospital's counsel who argues the requirement is met elsewhere (e.g. PE handled internally without FAP language, or translations posted on a page the agent didn't crawl). M2 must record the exact pages searched so each 0 is defensible as "not findable by a diligent public reader," not "definitively absent."
- **EINs and filing entities are only partly verified.** Confirmed EINs: Duke 56-2070036, Cone/Moses Cone 56-0532302, Novant 56-1376950, ECU/UHSEC 56-2141073, Cleveland Clinic 34-0714585, UPMC 25-1423657, Penn-area 23-2810852, Mass General Brigham 22-2658209, Ascension Providence 38-1358212, CommonSpirit 47-0617373 (all via [ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/), 2026-06-23). Rows marked `[verify in M3]` were NOT independently confirmed in M1 and must be reconfirmed before any dollar figure is quoted. Several large systems file Schedule H under a parent/group EIN rather than per facility, so the M3 ratio may reflect a system aggregate, not the named hospital — M3 must state this per row.
- **Mixed governance breaks D10/M3 for ~6 rows.** The for-profit (Mission/HCA) and public hospitals (NYC H+H, Cook County, Parkland) do not file Form 990 Schedule H, so D10 and the entire M3 charity-care ratio are inapplicable to them. They are retained for FAP/PLS accessibility comparison only; M4 must not rank them on a metric they cannot have.
- **Counter-evidence on the framing itself.** The aggregate "$14B billed that should be charity / only 29% of eligible patients get assistance" figures come from a single advocacy source ([Dollar For, *Bridging the Chasm*, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)); they are the best available national estimate but are not government-audited, and a hostile reader will note the source has a campaign interest. This rubric does not depend on those aggregates being precisely right — it scores each named hospital on its own public text — but the problem motivation does, and that dependency should be stated in the final report.
- **Weighting is a judgment call.** Putting 4 points each on D3 and D5 encodes the view that thresholds and presumptive eligibility matter most. A different stakeholder (e.g. one focused on collections abuse) would weight D7/D8 higher. The point values are transparent precisely so a critic can re-weight and re-rank; M4 should show sensitivity to at least one alternative weighting.
