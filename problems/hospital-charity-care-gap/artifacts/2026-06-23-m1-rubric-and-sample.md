# M1 — Scoring rubric and fixed hospital sample

> Milestone 1 artifact for **hospital-charity-care-gap**. Date: 2026-06-23.
> Purpose: define a transparent, machine-checkable rubric for FAP / Plain Language Summary (PLS) accessibility and IRC §501(r) compliance, and fix a reproducible sample of US nonprofit hospitals to score in M2 (FAP/PLS text) and M3 (Schedule H charity-care vs. revenue).

This artifact does **not** score anyone. It builds the measuring stick (Part A), fixes the list of things to be measured (Part B), and states what that list does and does not represent (Part C). Every dimension is anchored to a specific regulatory provision or a named advocacy/research source; every hospital is anchored to a ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer organization page (whose organization ID is the entity's EIN) so its Schedule H is one click away and its FAP is locatable from the entity name.

---

## Part A — Scoring rubric

### Design principles
- **Near-binary or numeric only.** Each dimension resolves to a small integer score from an explicit threshold, never from an adjective. An evaluator re-reading the same FAP must land on the same number.
- **Read from public text only.** Every dimension names the public artifact it is read from — the FAP PDF, the PLS, the billing-statement notice, or the Form 990 Schedule H — so M2/M3 can quote the source location.
- **Two families.** Dimensions D1–D8 score the *FAP/PLS as written* (the M2 input). Dimension D9 scores *what the hospital actually spent* (the M3 input, included here so the rubric is complete; its data source differs). M4 combines them.
- **Total.** D1–D8 sum to a **0–20 accessibility/compliance score** (maxima below sum to exactly 20). D9 is reported separately as a ratio and peer flag, not folded into the 0–20, because spending and policy-text are different evidence types that regulators act on differently.

(138% FPL = the ACA Medicaid-expansion line, a natural "bare minimum" reference.) |
| D4 | **Discounted-care (sliding scale) ceiling** | The income ceiling for any *partial* discount above the free-care line | **3** = partial discount available to ≥400% FPL; **2** = partial discount to 250–399% FPL; **0** = no sliding scale, or ceiling <250% FPL / unstated | FAP eligibility section | §501(r)(4) eligibility-criteria requirement; sliding-scale generosity is a core Dollar For / [Lown Institute fair-share](https://lowninstitute.org/projects/hospital-fair-share-spending/) accessibility concern |
| D5 | **Presumptive eligibility offered** | Whether the FAP grants assistance *without* a full application based on external indicators (Medicaid/SNAP/WIC enrollment, homelessness, prior FAP grant, or a third-party income estimator), vs. application-only | **3** = presumptive eligibility for **free** care via named programs (Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless) AND a public estimator, no application; **2** = presumptive eligibility offered but only for partial discounts, or only via a vendor "propensity" score; **1** = FAP mentions presumptive determination only at the back-end (collections), not at intake; **0** = application-only, no presumptive pathway stated | FAP "presumptive eligibility" / "how to apply" section | Friction-is-the-policy thesis in the problem brief; presumptive eligibility is the central fix per [Dollar For "Bottom Line" report](https://dollarfor.org/press/bottom-line-report-press-release/) and is being *mandated* by [North Carolina's HASP program](https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2024/08/13/nc-medical-debt-relief-plan-11-hospitalmust-dos-for-hospitals/) (auto-qualify Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless by 1/1/2025; full presumptive screening by 1/1/2026). Reg. [§1.501(r)-5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.501(r)-5) and [IRS §501(r)(5) guidance](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/limitation-on-charges-section-501r5) require FAP-eligible patients not be charged more than AGB and require the FAP to describe the AGB method |
| D7 | **Billing-statement notice of financial assistance** | Whether the hospital states (in FAP or on a sample bill, where visible) that billing statements carry a conspicuous notice of FAP availability with phone + URL — the §501(r)(6) "reasonable efforts" intake | **2** = FAP explicitly commits to a billing-statement FAP notice with phone + URL; **1** = FAP references notice but omits phone or URL detail; **0** = no billing-notice commitment found | FAP "billing and collections" section / sample billing statement if public | §501(r)(6); Treas. **Note on weighting.** The maxima sum to exactly 20 (D1=2, D2=3, D3=3, D4=3, D5=3, D6=2, D7=2, D8=2). The four highest-weighted dimensions (D2 PLS completeness, D3 free-care threshold, D4 sliding-scale ceiling, D5 presumptive eligibility, each up to 3) encode the design judgment that *intake accessibility and generosity* matter more than the procedural-disclosure dimensions (D1, D6, D7, D8, each up to 2). This weighting is a defensible choice, not a derived constant — see Limitations. M2 must use these exact maxima and tiers verbatim.

### Worked scoring example (illustrative, not a real score)
A hospital whose FAP is posted with application + PLS (D1=2), has a complete PLS (D2=3), gives free care to 200% FPL (D3=2), sliding scale to 400% (D4=3), is application-only with no presumptive pathway (D5=0), states AGB cap with look-back % (D6=2), commits to a billing notice with phone+URL (D7=2), and offers Spanish PLS only (D8=1) scores **2+3+2+3+0+2+2+1 = 15 / 20**, with its single most actionable issue = "no presumptive eligibility (D5=0)." D9 would be reported separately, e.g. "charity care 1.1% of expenses, below sample median."

## Part C — Sampling rationale

**How the 27 were chosen.** The sample is **purposive and stratified into three strata**, not random:

1. **North Carolina (8).** NC is the problem's natural experiment: its HASP program is the only US jurisdiction *mandating* presumptive eligibility (auto-qualify Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless from 1/1/2025; full presumptive screening from 1/1/2026, per [NC Health News, 2024](https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2024/08/13/nc-medical-debt-relief-plan-11-hospitalmust-dos-for-hospitals/)). Scoring NC's largest systems lets later milestones test whether FAP *text* has actually moved to match the mandate, and gives regulators (the NC AG, who negotiated HASP) an immediately actionable in-state list. All major NC system types are represented: academic (UNC, Duke), large secular nonprofit (Novant, Atrium, WakeMed, Cone), eastern rural-serving (ECU Health), and a small rural affiliate (UNC Rockingham).
2. **Largest national nonprofit systems (11).** High patient reach and high tax-exemption value, so any FAP-text or charity-care shortfall affects the most people and is the highest-yield target for IRS TE/GE audit selection. These are also the systems most studied by the [Lown Institute](https://lownhospitalsindex.org/hospital-fair-share-spending-2024/), enabling external cross-checks of our M3 figures.
3. **Mid-size / regional / Southeast peers (8).** A comparison band so NC hospitals are scored *relative to* like-sized non-NC peers (Prisma in SC, Carilion in VA) and so the sample is not entirely flagship academic centers. Including smaller affiliates (Advocate Condell, Spectrum United) tests whether system-level FAP generosity propagates to subsidiaries.

**Reproducibility.** The list is fixed and frozen as of 2026-06-23. Each entry is pinned to an EIN-bearing ProPublica URL, so any reviewer reaches the same 990 and can locate the same FAP. No entry depends on a transient search ranking.

**What the sample IS representative of:** large- and mid-size nonprofit *system* behavior, and the current state of FAP/PLS text and charity-care spending among the highest-reach institutions and across NC specifically.

**What it is NOT representative of:** (a) the full ~2,900-hospital nonprofit universe ([Nixon Peabody, 2025](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/03/18/revisiting-section-501r-compliance-in-2025)) — it is a deliberate purposive sample, so prevalence rates from it are **not** national estimates; (b) small standalone rural and critical-access hospitals (only two small entities included), which face different FAP economics; (c) for-profit and government hospitals, which are outside §501(r) entirely; (d) *actual screening practice* — the rubric scores the FAP text and Schedule H dollars, which can diverge from what front-desk staff actually do (a threat carried into M4). Several entries (#1 Atrium, #3 UNC Hospitals) are hospital *authorities* with hybrid public/nonprofit status; their §501(r) obligations attach to their 501(c)(3) filings and are in-scope, but their tax-exemption-value comparisons in M3 may warrant a footnote.

## Limitations & counter-evidence

- **FAP text ≠ practice (the central validity threat).** A generous FAP can coexist with aggressive billing, and a thin FAP with good front-line screening. The rubric measures the *paper*, which is what §501(r) actually regulates and what a regulator can subpoena — but it is a proxy for patient experience, not a measure of it. M4's limitations section must restate this.
- **The 0–20 weighting is a defensible choice, not a derived constant.** Point values (e.g. presumptive eligibility worth 3, language access worth 2) encode a judgment that intake friction matters more than translation breadth. A hospital's general counsel could argue different weights; the counter-defense is that every dimension maps to an actual regulatory requirement or a named advocacy criterion, and the *components* are reported transparently so anyone can re-weight. The total is a convenience, not a law.
- **§501(r) sets few hard thresholds.** Critically, §501(r) does **not** mandate any particular income-eligibility level — it only requires that the FAP *state* its criteria ([IRS §501(r)(4)](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/financial-assistance-policy-and-emergency-medical-care-policy-section-501r4)). So D3/D4/D5 thresholds (200% FPL, presumptive eligibility) are **advocacy/best-practice benchmarks** (Dollar For, NC HASP), not federal legal minima. A hospital meeting §501(r) to the letter can still score low on D3–D5; that is the intended signal, but it must be framed as "below best practice," not "illegal," to survive a hostile read. D1, D2, D6, D7, D8 *do* track binding regulatory requirements and can support a stronger compliance claim.
- **AHA's counter-position.** The American Hospital Association rejects the Lown fair-share framing entirely, arguing it excludes Medicaid shortfall, bad debt, and research/education that are genuine community benefits ([AHA, 2024](https://www.aha.org/news/blog/2024-03-25-there-nothing-fair-about-lown-institutes-fair-share-report)). D9 deliberately uses **Schedule H Line 7a charity-care-at-cost**, a narrower and less contestable figure than Lown's composite, to reduce exposure to this critique — but M3/M4 must acknowledge that a low charity-care ratio is not by itself proof of under-service.
- **Sample selection bias.** Purposive sampling toward large systems and NC means the scorecard cannot claim "X% of US nonprofit hospitals fail." It can only name *these* hospitals. The 71%-billed / ~$14B figures from [Dollar For 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf) are the national backdrop motivating the work; our sample does not re-estimate them.
- **EIN/entity granularity.** Large systems file dozens of subsidiary 990s; the parent EIN's Schedule H may consolidate or may cover only the parent. Entry #27 (Trinity/Saint Joseph Mercy) is flagged as a placeholder to be resolved in M3, and several NC "authority" entities (#1, #3) have hybrid status. Mis-mapping an EIN to the wrong filing would corrupt the M3 ratio — every EIN here was read from ProPublica on 2026-06-23 but must be re-confirmed at the Schedule H line level in M3, where the specific filing year and line items are recorded.
- **Staleness.** FAPs and 990s lag. M2 will record the FAP "last revised" date and M3 the 990 filing year; a 2-3 year gap between policy text and spending data is expected and will be noted, not hidden.
