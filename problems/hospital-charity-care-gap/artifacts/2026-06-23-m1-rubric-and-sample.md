# M1 — Scoring rubric and fixed hospital sample

**Problem:** Charity care that nonprofit hospitals are legally required to give — and mostly don't
**Milestone:** M1 (build the scoring rubric and fix the hospital sample)
**Date:** 2026-06-23
**Author:** generator (autonomous loop)

This artifact defines (a) a machine-checkable scoring rubric for FAP/Plain Language Summary (PLS) accessibility and 501(r) compliance, grounded in the actual regulatory text of 26 CFR 1.501(r) and the published criteria of Dollar For, the Lown Institute, and the CFPB; (b) a fixed, numbered sample of 26 real US nonprofit hospitals to be scored verbatim in M2 and M3; and (c) a sampling rationale. Nothing here is scored yet — M2 applies the rubric to FAP/PLS text and M3 pulls Schedule H dollars.

---

## (a) Scoring rubric

### How the rubric works

- **Unit of analysis:** one hospital facility's FAP + Plain Language Summary (PLS), read from public text only (FAP PDF, PLS PDF, and the hospital's financial-assistance web page).
- **Each dimension yields an integer score** with explicit thresholds — no subjective adjectives. Where a threshold cannot be determined from the public text, the dimension scores **0** and is flagged "unstated" (an unstated/buried policy is itself a 501(r)-4 "widely available" failure, so 0 is substantively correct, not just a data gap).
- **Maximum total score = 22 points** across 8 dimensions. Higher = more accessible / more compliant. (Lower scores are the regulatory-concern signal the scorecard exists to surface.)
- Dimensions D1–D6 read **accessibility and 501(r) FAP-content compliance** from text. D7 is read from text. D8 is the only dimension requiring the Schedule H dollar figure and is the bridge to M3 (it is defined here but its denominator/peer logic is finalized in M3).

### Rubric table

| # | Dimension | What it measures | How scored (point values / thresholds) | Public source it is read from | 501(r) provision / advocacy basis |
|---|-----------|------------------|------------------------------------------|-------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **D1** | FAP "widely available" online | Whether the FAP, FAP application form, and PLS are conspicuously downloadable from the hospital website free, with no account or PII required | **3** = all three (FAP + application + PLS) reachable in ≤2 clicks from a financial-assistance page, free, no login; **2** = FAP + PLS found but application missing or >2 clicks; **1** = only one document found; **0** = no FAP/PLS locatable via the hospital site | Hospital financial-assistance web page; FAP PDF; PLS PDF | 26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(5): FAP, application, and PLS must be "widely available on a Web site," downloadable "free of charge" without "creat[ing] an account or otherwise provid[ing] personally identifiable information." CFPB notes buried/inaccessible policies as a core barrier ([CFPB research report, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/understanding-required-financial-assistance-in-medical-care/)). |
| **D2** | Free-care income threshold (FPL) | The maximum household income, as a % of Federal Poverty Level, at which the patient receives **100% free** care | **3** = free care at ≥250% FPL; **2** = free care at 200–249% FPL; **1** = free care at 138–199% FPL; **0** = free-care threshold <138% FPL or unstated | FAP PDF eligibility section; PLS | Dollar For benchmark: hospitals "generally have a policy that gives free care to patients up to 100%–200% of FPL" and the advocacy target is ≥200% ([Dollar For, "Bridging the Chasm," 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)). 501(r)-4(b)(1)(ii) requires the FAP to state eligibility criteria. |
| **D3** | Discount-care income ceiling (FPL) | The maximum % FPL at which the patient still receives **any** discount (partial assistance) | **3** = discounts extend to ≥400% FPL; **2** = 300–399% FPL; **1** = 200–299% FPL; **0** = no sliding scale above the free-care line, or unstated | FAP PDF sliding-scale / discount schedule | Dollar For: discounts commonly run "up to 300%–400% FPL"; a broader ceiling reduces the cliff that pushes eligible patients into debt ([Dollar For, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)). 501(r)-4(b)(1)(ii)–(iii). |
| **D4** | Presumptive eligibility offered | Whether the FAP grants assistance without a full application based on third-party data (Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homelessness/prior determination) | **3** = presumptive eligibility explicitly offered AND lists ≥2 qualifying signals (e.g. Medicaid + homelessness/SNAP); **2** = presumptive eligibility mentioned but vague or single-signal; **1** = only an "uninsured automatic discount" (not means-based presumptive); **0** = application required in all cases / not mentioned | FAP PDF presumptive-eligibility section; PLS | 26 CFR 1.501(r)-6(c)(2): a hospital makes "reasonable efforts" if it determines FAP-eligibility "based on information other than that provided by the individual or based on a prior FAP-eligibility determination." NC mandates presumptive screening (Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless auto-qualify by 1/1/2025; full screening 1/1/2026) ([NC Health News, 2024](https://www.northcarolinahealthnews.org/2024/08/13/nc-medical-debt-relief-plan-11-hospitalmust-dos-for-hospitals/)). Dollar For's core fix. |
| **D5** | Application-barrier / documentation burden | How much paperwork the patient must supply to apply | **3** = application accepted online (portal) AND required documentation is light (income attestation OR a single proof, or none if presumptive); **2** = mail/fax/in-person application with limited documentation (e.g. recent pay stubs OR prior tax return); **1** = multiple mandatory documents required (pay stubs AND tax return AND bank statements, etc.); **0** = documentation requirements unstated or applicant must prove ineligibility for other programs first as a gate | FAP PDF application section ("information and documentation the hospital facility may require") | 26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(1)(iv) requires the FAP to describe "the information and documentation the hospital facility may require." Dollar For: "nearly a quarter of patients describe the application process as somewhat or very hard," and 24% of denials are paperwork issues ([Dollar For, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)). CFPB flags burden-shifting to the patient ([CFPB, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/understanding-required-financial-assistance-in-medical-care/)). |
| **D6** | Plain Language Summary completeness | Whether the PLS contains the elements the regulation requires | **3** = PLS present AND contains all four checked elements: (i) eligibility + how to apply, (ii) website URL + physical location to get the FAP/application, (iii) FAP-office contact phone, (iv) AGB statement ("may not be charged more than amounts generally billed"); **2** = PLS present, 2–3 elements; **1** = PLS present, ≤1 element; **0** = no PLS locatable | PLS PDF | 26 CFR 1.501(r)-4(b)(5)(i): PLS must be "clear, concise, and easy to understand" and include eligibility/how-to-apply, the URL and physical locations for FAP copies, contact info, translation availability, and the AGB statement. |
| **D7** | AGB / collections protection stated | Whether the FAP limits FAP-eligible patients to Amounts Generally Billed and describes nonpayment / extraordinary-collection-action (ECA) handling | **3** = FAP states AGB limit AND describes ECAs AND states a notification/grace period before ECAs; **2** = AGB limit stated but ECA process thin/absent; **1** = ECA process described but no explicit AGB limit; **0** = neither AGB limit nor ECA handling stated | FAP PDF (or referenced billing & collections policy) | 26 CFR 1.501(r)-5 (AGB) and 1.501(r)-4(b)(1)(iv)/(v) (FAP must describe basis for amounts charged and nonpayment actions); 1.501(r)-6(c)(4) requires ≥30 days written notice and a 120-day period before most ECAs. CFPB flags hospitals sending FAP-eligible bills to collectors ([CFPB, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/medical-debt-and-non-profit-hospital-billing-practices/)). |
| **D8** | Charity-care fair-share signal | Whether the hospital's charity care spend is plausibly at or above a fair-share floor (computed in M3) | **4** = charity-care-at-cost ≥5.9% of total expenses; **3** = 3.0–5.89%; **2** = 1.0–2.99%; **1** = >0 but <1.0%; **0** = ~0% / not separately reported on Schedule H | Form 990 Schedule H, Part I (charity care at cost) ÷ total functional expenses — **finalized in M3** | Lown Institute "fair share" threshold: a nonprofit hospital is meeting its obligation if it dedicates ≥**5.9%** of overall expenses to financial assistance + community investment; 80% of hospitals fall short ([Lown Institute, Fair Share Spending 2024](https://lownhospitalsindex.org/hospital-fair-share-spending-2024/)). This dimension's denominator and peer logic are fixed in M3; M1 only defines the point bands. |

**Maximum total = 3+3+3+3+3+3+3+4 = 22 points.** D1–D7 (18 pts, all text-readable in M2) + D8 (4 pts, Schedule H, in M3). The M4 scorecard ranks worst-first, i.e. lowest totals = highest regulatory concern.

**Note on the Lown criticism (counter-evidence built into the rubric):** the 5.9% fair-share threshold in D8 is contested — hospital associations argue Lown excludes Medicaid shortfall and other IRS-recognized community-benefit categories, producing an incomplete picture ([Keckley, 2024](https://paulkeckley.com/the-keckley-report/2024/4/1/the-lown-institute-aha-showdown-does-it-make-a-difference/)). D8 is therefore deliberately weighted modestly (4 of 22) and uses charity-care-at-cost specifically (not total community benefit), so a low D8 means "low *charity care*," not "low total community benefit." This is revisited in M3/M4 limitations.

## (c) Sampling rationale

**How hospitals were chosen.** The sample is a **purposive, reproducibility-first** selection, not a random draw, designed to (1) be retrievable from public text in M2/M3, (2) span geography and system type, and (3) over-weight North Carolina because NC is the only jurisdiction with a presumptive-eligibility mandate (HASP: Medicaid/SNAP/WIC/homeless auto-qualify by 2025-01-01, full screening by 2026-01-01), which lets D4 (presumptive eligibility) be tested against an actual legal floor.

Selection axes:
- **Geography (17 states + DC-adjacent):** NC (7 hospitals — Rex, Duke, Atrium/CMC, WakeMed, Wayne UNC, UNC Medical Center, Nash UNC), plus OH, MN (×2), MA, TX, CA, NY, IL, FL, GA, TN, PA, MO (×2), WA, AZ, MI, WI, MT. The Midwest/Mountain inclusions (Gundersen, Billings Clinic, Regions) deliberately reach beyond coastal academic flagships.
- **System size mix:** national mega-systems (Cleveland Clinic, Mayo, Mass General Brigham, UPMC, AdventHealth, Banner, Atrium/Advocate Health), large regional systems (Northwestern, Emory, Vanderbilt, BJC, SSM, HealthPartners, UNC Health), and smaller/rural-serving nonprofits (Gundersen, Billings Clinic, Wayne UNC, Nash UNC) so the scorecard isn't only "big famous academic centers."
- **Academic vs. community:** mix of major AMCs (Duke, UNC, Vanderbilt, Northwestern, Emory, Mass General, UPMC) and community/regional hospitals (WakeMed, Regions, Nash UNC, Billings Clinic).
- **Public, faith-based, and secular nonprofit:** public-authority (Atrium/Charlotte-Mecklenburg Hospital Authority, University of Michigan), faith-based (AdventHealth, SSM Health, Virginia Mason Franciscan/CommonSpirit), and secular 501(c)(3)s.

**What the sample IS representative of.** A cross-section of large- and mid-sized US nonprofit hospitals and systems that publish FAP/PLS documents online, weighted toward NC to exploit the presumptive-eligibility mandate as a benchmark. It is well-suited to **comparing FAP/PLS text quality and charity-care ratios across named, prominent institutions** that a regulator (IRS TE/GE, a state AG) or advocate (Dollar For, Lown) could plausibly act on by name.

**What the sample is NOT.**
- **Not statistically representative** of the ~2,900 nonprofit hospitals nationally. It over-represents large systems and academic flagships and under-represents small rural critical-access hospitals and the very smallest single-facility nonprofits, which often have the thinnest public FAP documentation.
- **Not random** — purposive selection means findings cannot be extrapolated to "X% of all nonprofit hospitals." The scorecard names *these* hospitals; it is not a population estimate.
- **Geographically skewed toward NC and the Eastern/Midwestern US**; no hospital from several large states (e.g. no current entry from the Mountain West besides Billings, none from the Deep South besides GA/FL, none from New England besides MA).
- **Text ≠ practice.** A hospital scoring well on FAP text (D1–D7) may still under-screen patients in practice; D8 (Schedule H dollars) partially counterbalances this but is itself contested (see the Lown counter-evidence note in the rubric). The whole rubric reads *policy as written*, which is a known limitation the M4 scorecard must state.

## Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **The rubric scores text, not behavior.** 501(r) compliance on paper is necessary but not sufficient; the CFPB and Dollar For both document hospitals with adequate written FAPs that still fail to screen eligible patients or send FAP-eligible bills to collections ([CFPB, 2024](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/medical-debt-and-non-profit-hospital-billing-practices/); [Dollar For, 2024](https://dollarfor.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Dollar_For.Bridging_the_Chasm.pdf)). A high score is "good policy," not "good outcomes." D8 (actual charity-care dollars) is the only behavioral proxy and is weighted only 4 of 22.
2. **D8's fair-share threshold (5.9%) is contested.** Hospital associations argue the Lown methodology omits Medicaid shortfall and other IRS-recognized community-benefit categories ([Keckley, 2024](https://paulkeckley.com/the-keckley-report/2024/4/1/the-lown-institute-aha-showdown-does-it-make-a-difference/)). The rubric mitigates this by using **charity-care-at-cost specifically** (not total community benefit) and finalizing the denominator in M3, but the band cutoffs (5.9 / 3.0 / 1.0%) are a defensible choice, not a regulatory bright line. Only the 138%, 200%, and ≥250% FPL cutoffs in D2 and the 501(r)-4/-5/-6 content checks in D1/D5/D6/D7 are tied to actual legal text.
3. **EINs and filing entities are not all finalized.** Several flagship hospitals (Houston Methodist's "The Methodist Hospital," AdventHealth Orlando, Banner Phoenix, the UNC Health affiliates, SSM/Regions) file under system, umbrella, or public-university EINs; the rows marked "(confirm in M3)" must be reconciled to the specific Schedule-H-filing entity before any dollar figure is attributed. Attributing one facility's text to the wrong filer's Schedule H would be a real error, flagged here so M3 catches it.
4. **URL drift.** FAP/PLS PDFs are frequently relocated (Atrium, Houston Methodist, and Cleveland Clinic all use hash-versioned asset URLs that rotate on policy updates). M2 must treat a 404 as "not found, search attempted," not silently substitute a different document — and the live-on-2026-06-23 URLs here may already differ by the time M2 runs.
5. **Selection bias toward documented hospitals.** Hospitals that publish nothing retrievable are exactly the worst D1 cases, yet they are hardest to *include* in a desk-research sample — so a purposive sample built from findable documents structurally under-counts the most non-compliant institutions. The sample therefore likely *understates* the population-level accessibility problem.
6. **Single-language scoring.** D6 checks the English PLS; 501(r)-4(b)(5) requires translations where a language group exceeds the lesser of 1,000 individuals or 5% of the community. Non-English availability is not separately scored in this rubric and could be a blind spot for hospitals in heavily multilingual service areas.
