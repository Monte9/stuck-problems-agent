# Spec: the sidelined immigrant physician (brain waste in a doctor shortage)

## Objective
"Unstuck" means producing the synthesis document that lets the next ~40 states adopt a foreign-physician provisional-licensure pathway *without* repeating Tennessee's structural mistake — the teaching-hospital employer tie that confines eligible jobs to urban hospitals while the shortage bites in rural/underserved counties. The end artifact is a desk-built, citation-backed package: a 50-state crosswalk of provisional-licensure rules flagging the specific "strand-the-law" provisions, a county-level HPSA-to-sidelined-IMG match that quantifies how many physicians a clean statute would unlock and where, and a ready-to-file model-bill redline plus board-rulemaking comment that fixes the defect. It serves state legislators and their staff drafting 2026-2027 bills, the medical-board officials writing implementing rules, and the advocacy coalitions (Niskanen, Cicero, Upwardly Global, AMA/FSMB) who would hand it to them.

## Milestones

### M1: Anchor case — dissect the Tennessee defect and define the "strand-the-law" taxonomy
- **Task:** Document, from primary sources, exactly how Tennessee's 2023 provisional-licensure statute and its board rulemaking restrict eligible employment (the teaching-hospital/residency-sponsor tie, specialty limits, supervision terms), and from that derive a named, reusable taxonomy of provision types that strand such laws.
- **Skill:** none — freeform
- **Artifact format:** A memo with (a) a Tennessee provision table — column per restrictive clause (sponsor-eligibility, supervision duration, specialty scope, exam/residency prerequisites, license-conversion path), each row quoting the operative statutory or rule text with a citation; (b) a numbered taxonomy of 4-8 "strand-the-law" provision types, each with a one-line definition and a check for how to detect it in a statute; (c) a sourced statement of the rural-residency mismatch (the ~2% figure or equivalent) that explains why the teaching-hospital tie matters.
- **Done-criteria:**
  - Every Tennessee provision row quotes statute or board-rule text and cites a working primary-source URL (TN Code, board rule, or board document), not just secondary commentary.
  - The taxonomy contains between 4 and 8 named provision types, each with a definition AND a concrete detection check phrased so it can be applied to another state's statute.
  - The teaching-hospital/residency-sponsor restriction is one of the named taxonomy types and is explicitly traced to a specific clause in the Tennessee text.
  - The rural-vs-urban residency mismatch claim cites at least one primary or peer-reviewed/think-tank source with a URL.

### M2: 50-state crosswalk of provisional-licensure rules
- **Task:** Using the M1 taxonomy, build a row-per-state crosswalk of every US state's status on foreign-physician provisional licensure (enacted law, pending bill, or none), coding each enacted/pending statute against the strand-the-law taxonomy.
- **Skill:** none — freeform
- **Artifact format:** A master table with one row per state (50 states + DC) and columns: status (enacted / bill introduced / none), citation (bill or statute number + URL), effective or introduction date, sponsor-eligibility restriction (coded to M1 taxonomy), supervision term, exam/residency prerequisites, specialty scope, and a "strand flags" column listing which taxonomy provision types are present. Plus a short methods/limitations section describing how currency was checked and what is uncertain.
- **Done-criteria:**
  - Every state row is present (51 rows incl. DC); no row is blank — "none found" states are explicitly marked, not omitted.
  - Every row coded "enacted" or "bill introduced" cites a specific bill/statute identifier AND a working URL to the legislative or board source.
  - The "strand flags" column for each enacted/pending state uses only provision-type names defined in M1, and at least the states named in the brief (Tennessee plus the other early adopters) are coded.
  - A limitations section states the as-of date of the legislative sweep and names at least two sources of residual uncertainty (e.g., pending amendments, rules not yet promulgated).
  - The enacted-state count and the bill-introduced count are stated as explicit totals and are internally consistent with the table rows.

### M3: County HPSA-to-sidelined-IMG match — quantify the unlock
- **Task:** Join county/state-level physician-shortage (HPSA) data against best-available estimates of where sidelined foreign-trained physicians live, to estimate, per state, how many physicians a clean (defect-free) provisional statute could plausibly route into shortage areas, with an explicit, reproducible method.
- **Skill:** none — freeform
- **Artifact format:** A ranked table (one row per state, highest unlock first) with columns: HPSA shortage measure (e.g., designated HPSAs or population in shortage, with source), estimated sidelined-IMG pool serving that state/metro (with source and derivation), an estimated "unlockable physicians" figure, and the assumption set used. Accompanied by a clearly labeled methodology section showing every formula and source, and a sensitivity note giving a low/high range.
- **Done-criteria:**
  - HPSA / shortage figures cite a government or AAMC/HRSA source with a URL and a stated data vintage.
  - The sidelined-IMG estimate states its national anchor (e.g., the ~65,000 figure) with a citation and shows the explicit allocation method used to distribute it to states/metros — no unsourced per-state numbers.
  - Every derived "unlockable" number is reproducible from the stated inputs and formulas (a skeptic could recompute at least three rows by hand).
  - A sensitivity/limitations section gives an explicit low-high range and names the largest source of estimation error.
  - The table is sorted by the unlock measure and identifies the top 5 states by name.

### M4: Model-bill redline + board-rulemaking comment that fixes the defect
- **Task:** Draft a copy-ready model statute (as a redline against the Tennessee text) and a board-rulemaking comment letter that together remove every strand-the-law provision identified in M1, justified by the M2 crosswalk and the M3 unlock estimates.
- **Skill:** none — freeform
- **Artifact format:** Two documents in one file: (1) a model-bill redline showing struck/added statutory language section by section, with a margin note per change naming which M1 taxonomy defect it cures; (2) a board-rulemaking public comment letter (1-3 pages) citing the M2 crosswalk and M3 numbers, addressed to a medical board, that argues for clean implementing rules. Plus a short "how to use" header for legislators/staff.
- **Done-criteria:**
  - Every strand-the-law provision type named in M1 is explicitly addressed by at least one redline change, and each redline change carries a margin note naming the defect it cures.
  - The teaching-hospital/residency-sponsor tie is removed or replaced in the redline with substitute supervision/safety language, and the substitution is justified in prose.
  - The rulemaking comment cites at least one concrete figure from the M3 artifact and at least one cross-state comparison from the M2 artifact, each with the source carried through.
  - Both documents are self-contained and reference no information that is not sourced in M1-M3 or a cited primary source; safety/quality claims (e.g., IMG outcome comparability) carry a citation.
  - The redline is structured as discrete sections matching a real statutory skeleton (eligibility, supervision, license conversion, employer scope), not free prose.
