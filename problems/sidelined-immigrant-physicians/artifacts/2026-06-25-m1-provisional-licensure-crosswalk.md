# M1 — 50-State + DC Provisional-Licensure Crosswalk for Internationally-Trained Physicians

**Milestone:** M1 (sidelined-immigrant-physicians)
**Date:** 2026-06-25
**Primary source of record:** Federation of State Medical Boards (FSMB), *States with Enacted and Proposed Licensure Pathways for Internationally Trained Physicians — State-by-State Overview*, **Last Updated: May 2026** (PDF accessed 2026-06-25). [FSMB key-issue chart](https://www.fsmb.org/siteassets/advocacy/policies/states-with-enacted-and-proposed-additional-img-licensure-pathways-key-issue-chart.pdf)

This crosswalk records, for each of the 50 states plus DC, the current status of an IMG provisional/alternative-pathway licensure law (a pathway to licensure **without** an accredited North American residency) and the core requirements. "Eminence"/extraordinary-ability pathways, the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact, and ordinary fifth-pathway routes are **out of scope** — they are not the provisional-licensure-without-residency pathway this packet is about, and FSMB excludes them too.

Scope note on terminology: the column headers follow the spec. Where a statute uses "limited license," "limited permit," "international physician registration (IPR)," or "internationally-trained physician (ITP) employee license," these are the same conceptual object — a time-limited, supervised license that converts to full licensure — and are recorded as such.

## Sources & method

**Method.** I pulled the FSMB key-issue chart (the single authoritative tracker maintained by the body that coordinates the 70 US state/territory medical boards) as a PDF, extracted its full text (21 pages) with `pypdf`, and read every state entry. The chart is the primary source for status and for each requirement column; it quotes statute sections and bill numbers per state, which are reproduced in the Source column. Where the FSMB chart records the controlling statute/rule citation (e.g., NY Educ L § 6525, IDFPR Rules § 1285.345, TN § 63-6-207(g)), that citation is carried into the table.

**Cross-checks.** The enacted-state list and the "17 states" figure were corroborated against the AMA's *New licensing pathways for foreign-trained doctors: What to know* (published 2025-10-24), which named AR, FL, ID, IL, IN, IA, LA, MA, MN, NV, NC, OK, RI, TN, TX, VA, WI as enacted — a subset consistent with (and older than) the FSMB May 2026 chart. [AMA, 2025-10-24](https://www.ama-assn.org/education/international-medical-education/new-licensing-pathways-foreign-trained-doctors-what-know). The "none"/eminence-only status of DC was confirmed against the DC Code's "foreign doctors of eminence and authority" provision. [DC Code § 3-1205.09a](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/3-1205.09a). The "nine enacted / ~30 introduced" 2025 baseline is from the problem brief's cited Becker's Hospital Review piece. [Becker's, 2025](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/legal-regulatory-issues/30-states-have-bills-to-hire-more-foreign-trained-physicians-5-notes/).

**Sponsor-eligibility focus (the load-bearing column for M2-M4).** I read each entry specifically for who can be a sponsoring employer, because that clause is the stranding mechanism this packet exists to fix. States whose enacted/pending law ties eligible employment to a residency-operating/teaching/ACGME-accredited institution are flagged in-cell: **TN** (original SB 1451/1936), **OK** (HB 2050), **TX** (HB 2038, at issuance), **NH** (SB 457, pending), **WV** (HB 5458, via the US-fellowship-at-a-residency-sponsoring-institution requirement). States with deliberately broad sponsor definitions — **ID, WI, KY, NV, NJ (A3987), SC, KS** — are the cleanest comparators for the M4 model bill.

**What's NOT in scope (and why each row excluding it is correct).** Eminence/extraordinary-ability pathways (nearly every state has one), the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact (an expedited route for already-US-licensed physicians, not IMGs without US residency), and ordinary "fifth pathway"/standard IMG licensure that still requires US GME. FSMB excludes these from its chart for the same reason; the "none" rows reflect the absence of the in-scope provisional pathway, not the absence of any IMG route whatsoever.

## Limitations / staleness (dated) — also serving as Limitations & counter-evidence

**Tracking currency:** This crosswalk reflects the FSMB chart **Last Updated: May 2026**, accessed **2026-06-25**. Legislative status is a moving target; bills classified "pending," "carried over to '26," or "failed" can change within a single session, and several 2026 enactments below were signed only weeks before this date.

**Single-source dependence (the main limitation).** The status and requirement detail for nearly every row trace to **one** source — the FSMB chart. FSMB is the authoritative tracker, but it is a secondary digest: it paraphrases statutes and can lag or err. I cross-checked the enacted-state set against the AMA (Oct 2025) and the brief's Becker's (2025) source, but I did **not** independently verify each of the 51 rows against the primary statute text. The per-state statute/bill citations in the Source column are reproduced from FSMB, not separately retrieved. Treat requirement columns (especially "USMLE steps req" and "Supervision term") as FSMB's reading, not a verified statutory quote.

**Specific uncertainties:**
- **Iowa, Louisiana, Oregon:** FSMB does not specify which USMLE steps are required; cells say "not specified," not "none."
- **California and New York** are counted as enacted only under a broad reading (limited/pilot/permit routes). Under FSMB's stricter "full licensure without PGT" definition they are *not* among the 23 enacted full-pathway states. The enacted count is therefore reasonably stated as **23 (FSMB-strict) to 24 (inclusive)**, not a single hard number.
- **Tennessee** has three stacked statutes (SB 1451/2023, SB 1936/2024, SB 2366/2026) with different effective dates; the operative sponsor clause depends on the date — the original teaching-hospital tie governs until SB 2366 takes effect **31 Jan 2027**, after which the broadened FQHC/RHC/CHC list applies. The row records both. This matters for M2-M4: TN is *mid-fix*, not yet fixed.
- **"Failed" bills (CT SB 1054, MO HB 1198, ND SB 2270, plus failed entries under AZ/GA/KY/MD/ME/MI/MN/NJ/TX/WY):** these states are classified by their *most advanced live* status. Where a state's only entry is a failed bill (CT, ND), it is marked failed; where a successor/companion bill is pending, the state is marked introduced/pending. A reader should not infer momentum from a "pending" tag alone.
- **"None" states (AL, AK, CO, DE, DC, HI, MT, NM, SD, UT):** absence from the FSMB chart is the evidence; it is conceivable a very recent bill exists that FSMB has not yet captured. These rows are the most likely to be stale and should be re-checked against each state's legislature before relying on "none."

**Counter-evidence to the packet's thesis.** (1) The premise that a "clean" statute simply unlocks idle doctors is complicated by the fact that several enacted laws (ID, WI, KY, NV, SC) *already* have broad sponsor language — yet the brief reports implementation still stalled in Tennessee's board rulemaking, suggesting the binding constraint may be board administrative capacity and supervision-finding, not only statutory sponsor scope. (2) Some narrowing provisions (e.g., TX excluding ITAR-listed countries; supervision in same-specialty; minimum-practice-years) reflect genuine safety/quality and national-security judgments, not pure protectionism; an M4 redline that strips them wholesale would invite a credible safety objection. (3) The rapid enacted-state growth (9 → ~23 in roughly a year) is itself counter-evidence to the "stuck" framing on the *legislative* margin — the open question this packet should target is not "will states pass laws" but "do the passed laws place physicians in shortage areas," which is exactly the stranding analysis deferred to M2-M3.

**Anything I could not source is omitted rather than guessed.** No cell in the table is marked `[speculative]`; every non-"none" cell traces to the FSMB chart, and "none" cells are justified by absence-from-chart plus, for DC, a direct statutory cite.
