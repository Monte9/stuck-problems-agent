Redlining the real codified subsection makes the fix copy-pasteable by any state staffer who wants to enact a clean pathway or repair an enacted defective one.

## Part 0 — The verbatim defective text (so the strikes target real language)

Below is the operative enacted language of **Tenn. Code Ann. § 63-6-207(g)**, as added by SB 1451 / Public Chapter 211 (2023), quoted exactly from the enrolled act. The two stranding clauses M2 named are **(g)(2)** and **(g)(3)**:

> **(g)(1)** The board may issue a temporary license of limited duration to an international medical school graduate upon finding sufficient evidence that the international medical school graduate:
> &nbsp;&nbsp;**(A)** Demonstrated competency as determined by the board;
> &nbsp;&nbsp;**(B)** Completed a three-year post-graduate training program in the graduate's licensing country; or
> &nbsp;&nbsp;**(C)** Has otherwise practiced as a medical professional performing the duties of a physician for at least three (3) of the last five (5) years outside the United States.
> **(2)** An applicant under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) must submit sufficient evidence that the applicant is an international medical school graduate and has an offer for employment as a physician **at a healthcare provider that operates in this state and has a post-graduate training program accredited by the accreditation council for graduate medical education in place.**
> **(3)** An international medical school graduate who is granted a temporary license under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) **must only provide medical services at a healthcare provider that has in place a post-graduate training program accredited by the accreditation council for graduate medical education.**
> **(4)** The board shall grant a full and unrestricted license to practice medicine to a temporary licensee under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) who is in good standing two (2) years after the date of temporary licensure. A temporary licensee who obtains a full and unrestricted license is not subject to the limitation of practicing at a healthcare facility with a post-graduate training program.
> **(5)** As used in this subsection (g), "healthcare provider" means an individual, entity, corporation, person, or organization, whether for profit or nonprofit, that furnishes, bills, or is paid for a healthcare procedure or service delivery in the normal course of business, and includes, but is not limited to, a health system, hospital, hospital-based facility, freestanding emergency facility, and urgent care clinic.

— *Source: [TN Public Chapter 211 / SB 1451 (2023)](https://publications.tnsosfiles.com/acts/113/pub/pc0211.pdf), verbatim.*

**Diagnostic note (the irony the redline exploits).** Subsection **(g)(5)** already defines "healthcare provider" broadly — health systems, hospitals, hospital-based facilities, freestanding emergency facilities, urgent care clinics. The stranding defect is **not** in the definition; it is the *additional* qualifier bolted onto that definition in (g)(2) and (g)(3): "...**that operates...** a post-graduate training program accredited by the [ACGME]." Strike that qualifier and the statute's own broad definition does the work. That is what makes this a clean, minimal redline rather than a rewrite.

**Pending-amendment status (current).** Tennessee's own 2026 amendment (referred to in the M-artifacts and the FSMB May 2026 chart as **SB 2366**, reported effective **31 Jan 2027**) is described as broadening sponsors to FQHCs/RHCs/CHCs **plus** ACGME-PGT facilities, but adding an **on-site same-specialty board-certified supervisor** requirement — which M2 re-classifies from `RESIDENCY-TIE` to `SUPERVISOR-SCARCITY`, i.e., Tennessee moves from High to **Medium**, not to clean ([M2, Medium table, "Tennessee (post-2027)" row](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). *Sourcing caveat:* I retrieved the verbatim **2023** act but could **not** independently retrieve the SB 2366 (2026) enrolled text via the TN legislature site in this session; the SB 2366 description here is carried from M1/M2, which trace to the [FSMB May 2026 chart](https://www.fsmb.org/siteassets/advocacy/policies/states-with-enacted-and-proposed-additional-img-licensure-pathways-key-issue-chart.pdf). The redline below repairs the **2023** defect *and* pre-empts the **2027** supervisor-scarcity successor defect, so it is robust to which version a state copies.

## Part A — Model-bill redline

Convention: ~~struck text~~ is deleted from the baseline; **bold-bracketed [INSERTED]** text is added. Each numbered change carries a one-line rationale grounded in M2/M3. The redline keeps SB 1451's structure and numbering so a drafter can swap it in.

### § 63-6-207(g) — as amended (clean version)

**(g)(1)** The board may issue a temporary [**provisional**] license of limited duration to an international medical school graduate upon finding sufficient evidence that the international medical school graduate:
&nbsp;&nbsp;**(A)** Is certified by the Educational Commission for Foreign Medical Graduates (ECFMG) **[or has otherwise demonstrated competency as determined by the board]**; ~~Demonstrated competency as determined by the board;~~
&nbsp;&nbsp;**(B)** Completed a three-year post-graduate training program in the graduate's licensing country; or
&nbsp;&nbsp;**(C)** Has otherwise practiced as a medical professional performing the duties of a physician for at least three (3) of the last five (5) years outside the United States.

> **Change A1 — keep ECFMG as the competence floor.** M2's clean-statute reference (feature 5) calls for an applicant pool "defined by competence, not nationality or narrow specialty," with ECFMG certification as the floor ([M2, clean-statute reference](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). Naming ECFMG explicitly closes the "demonstrated competency as determined by the board" loophole that lets a board re-import a residency requirement through the back door (the administrative-bottleneck risk M2 counter-evidence #3 flags). "Temporary"→"provisional" is cosmetic alignment with the packet's vocabulary; no substantive effect.

**(g)(2)** An applicant under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) must submit sufficient evidence that the applicant is an international medical school graduate and has an offer for employment as a physician at a healthcare provider ~~that operates in this state and has a post-graduate training program accredited by the accreditation council for graduate medical education in place.~~ **[in this state, as "healthcare provider" is defined in subdivision (g)(5).]**

> **Change A2 — strike the residency/teaching-hospital sponsor tie (THE defect).** This deletes the `RESIDENCY-TIE` clause M2 names as the High-severity mechanism: requiring the employer to "operate... a post-graduate training program accredited by the [ACGME]" confines eligible jobs to the urban academic medical centers where residencies cluster — only ~2% of Medicare-funded residency training sits in rural/underserved areas ([Niskanen Center, 2025-03-20](https://www.niskanencenter.org/unlocking-potential-how-states-can-remove-barriers-for-internationally-trained-physicians/); [M2, Tennessee row & tag `RESIDENCY-TIE`](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). The replacement relies on the statute's *own* broad (g)(5) definition, so any health system, hospital, FQHC, rural clinic, or independent practice qualifies — matching M2's "open, function-based sponsor eligibility — no residency tie" (clean-statute feature 1).

**(g)(3)** ~~An international medical school graduate who is granted a temporary license under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) must only provide medical services at a healthcare provider that has in place a post-graduate training program accredited by the accreditation council for graduate medical education.~~ **[A physician who is granted a provisional license under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) must provide medical services under the supervision of a physician who holds a full and unrestricted Tennessee license. The supervising physician need not practice in the same specialty as the provisional licensee and need not be physically present on-site at all times; the board shall by rule permit supervision through a written collaboration plan, including the use of telesupervision, where an on-site or same-specialty supervisor is not reasonably available, provided patient safety is protected.]**

> **Change A3 — replace the practice-site restriction with supervisor-based, geography-neutral supervision.** This strikes the second `RESIDENCY-TIE` clause (the place-of-practice limit) and, critically, **does not** substitute the `SUPERVISOR-SCARCITY` clause that M2 says will strand Tennessee even *after* SB 2366 — the "on-site board-certified supervisor in the same specialty" requirement that "can still strand placements in thin rural markets where no such specialist works" ([M2, "Tennessee (post-2027)" row; tag `SUPERVISOR-SCARCITY`](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). Instead it defines supervision by the *supervisor's qualification* (full TN license) with explicit permission for non-same-specialty and telesupervision — M2's clean-statute feature 3. This is the single most important difference between this redline and Tennessee's own 2027 fix: it cures the defect **without** opening a new one.

**(g)(4)** The board shall grant a full and unrestricted license to practice medicine to a [**provisional**] licensee under subdivision (g)(1)(B) or (g)(1)(C) who is in good standing **[and has passed USMLE Step 3]** two (2) years after the date of [**provisional**] licensure. **[The board shall act on the application for a full and unrestricted license within sixty (60) days of the licensee's completion of the two-year supervised term and submission of a complete application; if the board does not act within that period, the provisional license shall continue in effect until the board acts.]** A [**provisional**] licensee who obtains a full and unrestricted license is not subject to the limitation of practicing at a healthcare facility ~~with a post-graduate training program.~~ **[or under supervision.]**

> **Change A4 — preserve the short, *converting* 2-year term and add a board-action deadline.** M2's clean-statute features 4 and the implementation lesson (the Tennessee board "was slow to enforce the new law," per the brief; [Niskanen Center implementation note](https://www.niskanencenter.org/implementing-new-licening-pathways-that-work-for-international-doctors-and-american-patients/)) both demand a *bounded* term with *automatic, non-discretionary* conversion. Keeping the 2-year auto-conversion (already SB 1451's strength) and adding a 60-day board-action deadline directly attacks the "implementation stalled in board rulemaking" failure mode that M2 counter-evidence #3 and M3 limitation #5 both flag as possibly the *binding* constraint. Adding the Step 3 condition codifies the competence checkpoint M2 feature 4 specifies; it was implicit in board practice but is made statutory here so it cannot be inflated into a fuller exam barrier by rule.

**(g)(5)** As used in this subsection (g), "healthcare provider" means an individual, entity, corporation, person, or organization, whether for profit or nonprofit, that furnishes, bills, or is paid for a healthcare procedure or service delivery in the normal course of business, and includes, but is not limited to, a health system, hospital, hospital-based facility, freestanding emergency facility, **[a federally qualified health center, a rural health clinic, a community health center, an independent or group physician practice, and a primary care clinic,]** and urgent care clinic.

> **Change A5 — make the broad sponsor universe explicit, naming the shortage-area employers.** SB 1451's (g)(5) was already broad but illustrative; this enumerates the exact employer types that serve HPSAs and that the `RESIDENCY-TIE` excluded — FQHCs, RHCs, CHCs, independent/group practices, primary-care clinics. This is M2's clean-statute feature 1 made unambiguous, and it mirrors the cleanest enacted comparator, Idaho H 542, whose sponsor definition expressly reaches "independent physician practices, primary care clinics, urgent care clinics" ([M2, Idaho `none found` row](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md); [M1, Idaho row](./2026-06-25-m1-provisional-licensure-crosswalk.md)).

**[(g)(6) — NEW.] [Shortage-area direction.] [On each renewal or at conversion to full licensure, the board may require a provisional licensee to certify that a majority of the licensee's clinical practice during the supervised term was provided in a geographic area designated as a health professional shortage area, a medically underserved area, or a rural county. This subdivision directs, but does not restrict, eligible employment; no provisional license shall be denied or revoked solely because the sponsoring healthcare provider is not located in such an area.]**

> **Change A6 — add shortage-area *direction* without employer-type *restriction*.** M2's clean-statute feature 2 is the linchpin distinction: steer supply to need via a renewal-must-serve-shortage rule (the Texas/Virginia model M2 cites as a partial structural cure), **not** by narrowing the eligible employer universe. The "directs, but does not restrict" sentence is the firewall that keeps this from becoming a new stranding clause: a board cannot weaponize it to confine licensees the way the residency tie did. This is what converts the bill from "remove a defect" to "actively place doctors where M3 shows the shortage is" — without re-creating one.

**[(g)(7) — NEW.] [Self-executing.] [This subsection takes effect on its stated effective date and is not contingent on any appropriation. The board shall promulgate any implementing rules within one hundred eighty (180) days of the effective date, and the board's failure to promulgate rules shall not prevent the issuance of provisional licenses under the standards stated in this subsection.]**

> **Change A7 — no appropriation contingency; rules cannot indefinitely delay licensing.** M2's clean-statute feature 6 warns against Georgia's "effective only on appropriation" trigger, which "can indefinitely delay any supply at all" ([M2, Georgia row](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). Pairing a self-executing clause with a 180-day rulemaking deadline *and* a fallback that licenses can issue on the statutory standard even absent rules directly closes the Tennessee implementation-stall failure mode at the statutory level — belt and suspenders with Part B.

### What was deliberately NOT changed (and why)

- **The 3-year-foreign-PGT-or-3-of-5-years-practice experience floor (g)(1)(B)/(C)** is kept. M1/M2 counter-evidence notes minimum-recent-practice floors reflect a genuine competence rationale; stripping them invites a credible safety objection ([M1, counter-evidence #2](./2026-06-25-m1-provisional-licensure-crosswalk.md)).
- **The 2-year supervised term** is kept, not shortened. M2 flags `LONG-SUPERVISION` (4-8 yrs) as the defect; 2 years is already at the clean end, so there is nothing to fix.
- **No country-of-origin exclusion is added.** Texas's ITAR-country exclusion is a national-security judgment some states may want; this model omits it to stay maximally clean, and flags it as a state-by-state policy choice rather than a recommendation (M2 clean-statute feature 5 disfavors nationality gates).

## Part B — Board-rulemaking comment letter

> **To:** [State] Board of Medical Examiners, in re: Proposed Rules Implementing the Provisional Licensure Pathway for Internationally Trained Physicians (Tenn. Code Ann. § 63-6-207(g) and analogues)
> **From:** [Advocacy coalition / commenter — e.g., a Niskanen/Cicero/Upwardly Global-aligned coalition]
> **Date:** 2026-06-25
> **Re:** Implementing the pathway without stalling — recommended rule provisions

**Summary.** Passing a clean statute is necessary but not sufficient. The single best-documented failure of the first-in-nation Tennessee law was not its text alone but that implementation "stalled in board rulemaking" and the board "was slow to enforce the new law" well after its effective date ([Niskanen Center implementation note](https://www.niskanencenter.org/implementing-new-licening-pathways-that-work-for-international-doctors-and-american-patients/); echoed in [M2 counter-evidence #3](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md) and [M3 limitation #5](./2026-06-25-m3-county-shortage-sidelined-img-match.md) as the possibly *binding* constraint). The following rule provisions let the board operationalize the pathway quickly and prevent rulemaking from silently re-creating, through definitions and discretion, the very stranding defect the statute removed. We recommend the board adopt these as numbered rule sections.

### Recommended rule provisions

**1. Decision timeline for provisional-license applications (anti-stall).**
The board shall issue a decision on a complete provisional-license application within **45 days** of receipt. An application is "complete" upon submission of ECFMG certification, evidence of the experience floor, and a qualifying employment offer; the board shall notify an applicant of any deficiency within **15 days** of receipt, and a deficiency notice tolls the 45-day clock only as to the missing item. *Rationale:* directly answers the documented Tennessee enforcement lag; a default-grant or default-continuation backstop (statute § 63-6-207(g)(4), Change A4) gives the deadline teeth.

**2. Sponsor-approval criteria that cannot re-impose the residency tie.**
A "qualifying healthcare provider" eligible to sponsor a provisional licensee is **any provider within the statutory definition** (§ 63-6-207(g)(5)), including federally qualified health centers, rural health clinics, community health centers, and independent and group physician practices. The board **shall not** by rule, policy, form, or accreditation-code requirement condition sponsor eligibility on the provider operating, hosting, or being affiliated with an ACGME- or AOA-accredited residency or graduate-medical-education program. Sponsor approval shall be limited to verifying (a) the provider's licensure/operation in the state and (b) the existence of a written supervision plan under provision 3. *Rationale:* M2's High-severity defect was administrable precisely because the original statute required an "ACGME accreditation code" on the employment letter ([TN BME IMG temp-license policy](https://www.tn.gov/content/dam/tn/health/healthprofboards/medicalexaminers/Demonstrated_Competency_IMG_Temp_License.pdf)). A board could quietly resurrect the tie through its application form even after a clean statute; this provision forecloses that. (Clean-statute feature 1, [M2](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md).)

**3. Supervision definition — qualification-based, geography-neutral (anti-scarcity).**
"Supervision" means a documented professional relationship with a supervising physician who holds a full and unrestricted state license and has agreed in writing to a collaboration plan covering case review, availability, and escalation. The supervising physician (a) **need not** practice in the same specialty as the provisional licensee, and (b) **need not** be continuously physically present on-site. Where a same-specialty or on-site supervisor is not reasonably available in the licensee's practice area, the board shall accept supervision via telehealth/telesupervision and periodic in-person review, provided the collaboration plan documents how patient safety is maintained. *Rationale:* this is the provision that prevents the `SUPERVISOR-SCARCITY` cap M2 says will strand Tennessee even after SB 2366's 2027 broadening ([M2, "Tennessee (post-2027)" row](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). A same-specialty-on-site mandate fails in exactly the rural HPSAs the law is meant to serve. (Clean-statute feature 3.)

**4. Shortage-area direction, implemented as a reporting/renewal condition (not an eligibility bar).**
At renewal and at conversion to full licensure, the board shall collect (and may publish in aggregate) the HPSA/MUA/rural status of each provisional licensee's practice site, and may give priority processing to applications whose sponsoring provider serves a designated shortage area. The board **shall not** deny, condition, or revoke a provisional license solely because the sponsor is not located in a shortage area. *Rationale:* operationalizes statutory Change A6 — direction without restriction — and produces the placement data that M3 notes does not currently exist ("no public dataset counts sidelined foreign-trained physicians by state," [M3 limitation #1](./2026-06-25-m3-county-shortage-sidelined-img-match.md)), so the state can measure whether the pathway is actually reaching shortage areas.

**5. Automatic, non-discretionary conversion to full licensure.**
Upon a provisional licensee completing the 2-year supervised term in good standing and passing USMLE Step 3, the board **shall** issue the full and unrestricted license; conversion is ministerial, not discretionary, absent a pending disciplinary matter. The board shall act within **60 days** (mirroring statute § 63-6-207(g)(4)). *Rationale:* M2's clean-statute feature 4 and the `NO-CONVERSION` defect (Maryland SB 489, California legacy MPP) show that a dead-end or discretionary conversion defeats the supply purpose ([M2, `NO-CONVERSION` tag](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)). Discretionary conversion is also where a slow board can re-introduce delay.

**6. Published, plain-language application checklist and rolling acceptance.**
The board shall publish a single-page checklist of provisional-license requirements and accept applications on a rolling basis (no fixed windows). *Rationale:* low-cost administrative provision that addresses the friction documented in states requiring in-person board appearances (Arkansas) and reduces the deficiency-driven delays provision 1 targets ([M2, Arkansas row](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md)).

(Provisions 1–6 satisfy the milestone's "at least three" floor; 1, 3, and 5 are the load-bearing trio for "act without stalling.")

## Why this matters (one-page framing)

**Audience.** This packet has two named audiences, and each part is addressed to one of them:
- **Part A (the redline) → a state legislator or legislative-services drafter** who wants to introduce a clean provisional-licensure bill, or amend an enacted defective one, within a single legislative session. It is written to be copy-pasted into a bill.
- **Part B (the rulemaking comment) → a state Board of Medical Examiners** (and the advocacy coalitions — Niskanen, Cicero, Upwardly Global, AMA/FSMB-aligned — who file comments before it). It is written to be filed as a public comment and adopted as rule sections.

**The stakes, in numbers.** M3's central leverage finding: even on conservative assumptions (a clean statute capturing only 10–20% of a state's sidelined-IMG pool over ~5 years), fully unlocking sidelined internationally-trained physicians could add roughly **6,000 to 12,500 practicing physicians nationally** — enough to close **~40–80% of the measured national primary-care practitioner gap of 15,604** ([M3, Methodology Step 3](./2026-06-25-m3-county-shortage-sidelined-img-match.md), built on [KFF/HRSA HPSA data as of 2025-12-31](https://www.kff.org/other-health/state-indicator/primary-care-health-professional-shortage-areas-hpsas/) and the [MIRA ~65,000 sidelined-physician figure](https://theworld.org/stories/2018-03-26/highly-trained-and-educated-some-foreign-born-doctors-still-can-t-practice)). The national physician shortfall is projected at up to 86,000 by 2036 ([AAMC, 2024](https://www.aamc.org/news/press-releases/new-aamc-report-shows-continuing-projected-physician-shortage)).

**Where this redline buys the most.** M3's Top-10 leverage table points the legislative audience at specific targets where statute change (not just implementation) still moves the needle ([M3, Top-10 table](./2026-06-25-m3-county-shortage-sidelined-img-match.md)):
- **Texas** (#2 shortage, ~5,700 sidelined IMGs, **Medium residency-tie-at-issuance defect**): Change A2 alone — striking the residency tie from issuance — is the targeted fix; ~550–1,150 physicians in play.
- **New York** and **California** (~5,400 and ~14,800 pools): pass the pending converting bills (A7319/S7840; AB2386) using this redline's structure; together the largest single pools in the country.
- **Ohio** (#6 shortage, clean pending HB763) and **Arizona** (#5 shortage, pending HB2435): the redline is a template for finishing already-clean bills.
- **Oklahoma, West Virginia, New Hampshire, Maryland** (enacted/pending **High-severity residency/fellowship ties**, [M3 "cautionary" note](./2026-06-25-m3-county-shortage-sidelined-img-match.md)): smaller pools, but Change A2/A3 is the exact repair their defect needs.

**The one-sentence pitch.** Tennessee proved the politics work (9 → ~23 enacted states in roughly a year, [M1 status roll-up](./2026-06-25-m1-provisional-licensure-crosswalk.md)) but also proved the trap: a law can pass and still strand its doctors in cities. This packet hands the next 40 states the two documents that fix it — a five-line statutory strike and six rule provisions — so the doctors land where the [~8,467 primary-care HPSAs](https://www.kff.org/other-health/state-indicator/primary-care-health-professional-shortage-areas-hpsas/) actually are.

## Limitations & counter-evidence

**1. The strongest objection: the residency tie had a real patient-safety rationale, and the redline must not look like pure deletion.** Legislators in Tennessee, Oklahoma, and Texas tied sponsorship to residency-operating institutions partly because those institutions have built-in supervision and assessment infrastructure ([M2 counter-evidence #2](./2026-06-25-m2-stranding-defect-analysis.md); [Challenges in Removing US Residency Training Requirements, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12096114/), which frames the requirement as a structural limitation but also reflects the assessment-infrastructure logic). Change A3 deliberately *replaces* the tie with a supervisor-qualification + collaboration-plan + Step-3 regime rather than merely deleting it — but a critic can fairly argue that a written collaboration plan in a small rural practice is weaker oversight than embedded residency-program supervision. The honest response is that the alternative the defect produces is **no** physician in the rural area at all; the redline trades a high-supervision-but-urban-only regime for an adequate-supervision-anywhere regime, and stakes that trade on the supervisor-qualification floor and the 2-year monitored term.

**2. Fixing the statute may not fix the bottleneck.** M2 (counter-evidence #3) and M3 (limitation #5) both flag that several states with *already-clean* sponsor language (Idaho, Wisconsin, Kentucky) have not visibly produced large placement numbers, suggesting the binding constraint may be board administrative capacity, supervisor availability, or **employer willingness**, none of which a redline reaches. Part B addresses board capacity; neither part can compel an FQHC to hire, nor manufacture a supervising physician in a county that has none. The "unlocked" numbers in the framing are therefore upper bounds conditioned on the non-statutory frictions clearing.

**3. The leverage numbers carry M3's full uncertainty.** The 6,000–12,500 figure rests on a 2018 advocacy estimate (~65,000) distributed by 2013–17 MPI state shares and a disclosed 10–20% capture assumption — every one an analyst construct or aging source, not a current measurement ([M3 limitations #1–5](./2026-06-25-m3-county-shortage-sidelined-img-match.md)). The *relative* state ranking is more robust than the absolute counts. This framing should not be cited as a precise forecast.

**4. The board comment cannot fix what the statute leaves discretionary, and a hostile board can still slow-walk.** Part B's provisions presume a board that wants to implement. A board determined to resist can comply with a 45-day clock while raising the deficiency bar, or interpret "reasonably available" supervisor narrowly. The statutory backstops (default-continuation in A4, the rules-not-required-to-license fallback in A7) are the only hard guardrails; rule provisions alone are advisory until adopted. Where a board is the obstacle, the legislative audience, not the board comment, is the lever.

**5. SB 2366 (2026) text was not independently verified this session.** The redline baseline (SB 1451 / Public Chapter 211, 2023) is verbatim-verified from the enrolled act. The description of Tennessee's *own* in-flight 2027 fix (SB 2366) is carried from M1/M2 → the FSMB May 2026 chart and was **not** confirmed against the enrolled 2026 bill text (the TN legislature site did not surface it in this session). If SB 2366's actual supervisor language differs from the M2 paraphrase, Change A3's framing as "pre-empting the 2027 supervisor-scarcity defect" should be re-checked — but the redline stands on its own against the verified 2023 text regardless.

**6. Scope honesty.** This is a *model* redline against Tennessee's section numbering; a drafter in another state must conform it to that state's medical-practice-act structure and definitions. The substantive moves (strike the residency tie, replace with qualification-based supervision, enumerate shortage-area employers, add direction-not-restriction, make it self-executing) transfer; the exact subsection citations do not.
