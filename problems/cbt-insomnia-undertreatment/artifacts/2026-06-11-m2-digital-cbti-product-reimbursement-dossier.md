# M2 — Digital CBT-I product and reimbursement dossier

**Problem:** cbt-insomnia-undertreatment · **Milestone:** 2 · **Date:** 2026-06-11
**Builds on:** [M1 cost-of-inaction baseline](./2026-06-11-m1-cost-of-inaction-baseline.md) (per-100,000 cost of status quo: ~$92M/yr, range $17M–$146M)

**Bottom line:** Exactly two digital CBT-I products hold FDA 510(k) clearance under 21 CFR 882.5801 / product code QVO: **Somryst** (K191716, 2020; survived its maker's bankruptcy and is commercially alive under Nox Health) and **SleepioRx** (K233577, 2024; Big Health). Both carry large-effect RCT evidence (Cohen's d ≈ 0.95–1.11 on sleep outcomes in trials of N=164 to N=3,755). As of January 1, 2025, Medicare pays for them through three new HCPCS codes — **G0552** (device supply, contractor-priced), **G0553/G0554** (monthly management, ~$20 nationally priced) — when furnished incident to ongoing behavioral health treatment; the CY2026 final rule kept these codes and expanded the device categories. The main coverage gaps: G0552's actual payment amount is set MAC-by-MAC and remains opaque, the device is not payable as a standalone direct-to-consumer benefit, and the older per-course code A9291 still has no national Medicare payment.

## 2. The CMS reimbursement mechanism, 2025–2026

### 2.1 What existed before 2025: a code with no payment

CMS established HCPCS Level II code **A9291** — "Prescription digital cognitive and/or behavioral therapy, FDA cleared, per course of treatment" — effective 2023, following an application initiated by Pear Therapeutics ([Digital Therapeutics Alliance statement, Feb 2022](https://dtxalliance.org/2022/02/25/the-digital-therapeutics-alliance-welcomes-decision-by-cms-to-establish-a-hcpcs-code-for-prescription-digital-behavioral-therapy/); [MedTech Dive, 2022](https://www.medtechdive.com/news/pear-therapeutics-cms-code-reimbursement-digital-health/619435/); code descriptor at [AAPC Codify: A9291](https://www.aapc.com/codes/hcpcs-codes/A9291)). A9291 lets Medicaid programs and commercial payers process PDT claims, but it carries **no national Medicare payment amount** because CMS had not identified a Medicare benefit category for standalone PDTs ([EVERSANA analysis](https://www.eversana.com/insights/prescription-digital-therapeutics-coding-a-good-first-step-by-cms/)). This was the stuck state M2 inherits: cleared products, proven trials, a billing code, no Medicare dollars.

### 2.2 CY 2025 Physician Fee Schedule: Medicare starts paying

In the **CY 2025 Medicare Physician Fee Schedule final rule** (effective January 1, 2025), CMS created three G-codes for "digital mental health treatment" (DMHT) devices ([CMS MM13887, CY2025 PFS final rule summary](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mm13887-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-summary-cy-2025.pdf); [AAPC summary](https://www.aapc.com/blog/93026-medicare-implements-digital-mental-health-treatment-codes/); [Nixon Law Group analysis](https://www.nixonlawgroup.com/resources/new-reimbursement-opportunities-for-digital-mental-health-treatment-in-2025-cms-final-rule)):

| Code | Descriptor (abridged) | CY2025 payment |
|---|---|---|
| **G0552** | Supply of DMHT device + initial education and onboarding, per course of treatment, augmenting a behavioral therapy plan | **Contractor-priced** — each Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) sets the amount |
| **G0553** | First 20 min/month of treatment-management services using DMHT device data, incl. ≥1 interactive patient communication that month | ≈ **$20.06** (work RVU 0.62 × 2025 conversion factor $32.35) |
| **G0554** | Each additional 20 min/month of the same | ≈ **$19.73** (work RVU 0.61) |

Key conditions of payment:

- **Device eligibility:** G0552 is payable only for devices FDA-cleared (510(k)) or De Novo–authorized **under 21 CFR 882.5801** ([AAPC](https://www.aapc.com/blog/93026-medicare-implements-digital-mental-health-treatment-codes/)). Somryst and SleepioRx are both classified under exactly this regulation (openFDA records above), so **both qualify** — chronic-insomnia CBT-I is, in practice, the first condition with FDA-cleared products that fit the benefit.
- **Care-model condition:** the device must be furnished **incident to professional behavioral health services, under an ongoing behavioral-health treatment plan of care** ([CMS MM13887](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mm13887-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-summary-cy-2025.pdf)). A standalone prescription with no ongoing behavioral-health management is not billable.

### 2.3 CY 2026 final rule: codes retained and expanded

The **CY 2026 PFS final rule** (CMS-1832-F, issued October 31, 2025, effective January 1, 2026) **retained G0552–G0554** (G0552 still contractor-priced; G0553/G0554 still nationally priced) and **expanded** the DMHT payment policy to devices for **ADHD** in addition to the behavioral-health conditions already covered; the billing practitioner need not be the clinician who made the qualifying diagnosis ([CMS CY2026 PFS final rule fact sheet](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/calendar-year-cy-2026-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-cms-1832-f); [CMS MM14315 summary](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/mm14315-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule-summary-cy-2026.pdf); [Holland & Knight, Nov 2025](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/cms-releases-cy-2026-medicare-physician-fee-schedule-final-rule); [Federal Register, 2025-11-05](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/11/05/2025-19787/medicare-and-medicaid-programs-cy-2026-payment-policies-under-the-physician-fee-schedule-and-other)). Direction of travel is toward broader digital-therapeutic coverage ([Nixon Law Group on the CY2026 proposals](https://www.nixonlawgroup.com/resources/cms-proposes-expanded-coverage-for-digital-mental-health-devicesand-seeks-input-on-broader-digital-therapeutics-use-cases); [Sidley, Nov 2025](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/11/cms-finalizes-key-medicare-reimbursement-policies-for-telehealth-chronic-disease-and-digital-health)).

### 2.4 What is and is not covered (decision-relevant summary)

**Covered (traditional Medicare, from 2025-01-01):**
- The device itself (Somryst or SleepioRx) via **G0552**, when prescribed within an ongoing behavioral-health plan of care — but at a MAC-set price.
- Monthly clinician management of the digital treatment via **G0553/G0554** at ~$20 per 20 minutes.
- MACs have published implementation guidance (e.g., [Noridian JE article "Understanding Digital Mental Health Treatments"](https://med.noridianmedicare.com/web/jea/article-detail/-/view/10521/understanding-digital-mental-health-treatments)).

**Not covered / not resolved:**
- **G0552 payment amounts**: no national rate; MAC-specific published fee amounts for G0552 were **not found** in this research; legal/industry commentators describe the MAC pricing process as "opaque, inconsistently applied, and logistically difficult" ([Health Advances, 2025](https://www.healthadvances.com/insights/blog/2025-medicare-pfs-a-positive-evolution-of-reimbursement-for-digital-health); [Nixon Law Group](https://www.nixonlawgroup.com/resources/cms-proposes-expanded-coverage-for-digital-mental-health-devicesand-seeks-input-on-broader-digital-therapeutics-use-cases)). A payer writing policy should expect to set its own device fee (the published anchors: Somryst's $899 Pear-era list; NICE's £45 Sleepio price).
- **Standalone/direct-to-consumer use**: no Medicare payment without the incident-to behavioral-health service wrapper.
- **A9291**: still no national Medicare payment amount (no benefit category) — it functions as a claims-processing vehicle for Medicaid/commercial payers ([EVERSANA](https://www.eversana.com/insights/prescription-digital-therapeutics-coding-a-good-first-step-by-cms/)).
- **Medicare claims volume under G0552–G0554** (i.e., whether the codes are actually being used at scale in year one): **not found** — CY2025 utilization data were not yet published at research time.

**Non-Medicare payer adoption (examples, not exhaustive):**
- **Highmark** became the first large commercial insurer with an explicit medical-necessity policy covering FDA-cleared PDTs prescribed on-label (policy in place from 2022, rebooted 2024) — [STAT News, 2022-10-19](https://www.statnews.com/2022/10/19/highmark-insurers-decision-to-cover-prescription-digital-therapeutics/); [STAT News, 2024-09-09](https://www.statnews.com/2024/09/09/highmark-insurance-digital-therapeutics-coverage-policy/); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/health-tech/highmark-expands-coverage-prescription-digital-therapeutics-app-makers-see-momentum).
- Pear reported (pre-bankruptcy, 2022) PDT coverage from ~10 state Medicaid programs, multiple Blues plans, and SelectHealth — [STAT News, 2022](https://www.statnews.com/2022/10/19/highmark-insurers-decision-to-cover-prescription-digital-therapeutics/); Medicaid PDT value-assessment experience is reviewed in [JMCP 2023, DOI 10.18553/jmcp.2023.29.6.685](https://www.jmcp.org/doi/full/10.18553/jmcp.2023.29.6.685). These data points predate the 2025 G-codes; a current comprehensive list of commercial adopters was **not found**.
- The SleepioRx claims study (M1 Row 16) demonstrates that US insured populations are already receiving prescribed SleepioRx at scale (11,027 users in one matched cohort) — [JHEOR 2025](https://doi.org/10.36469/001c.146434).

### 2.5 Why this matters for the M1 numbers

M1 found ~29,800 untreated chronic-insomnia patients per trained behavioral-sleep-medicine provider. The 2025 mechanism resolves the supply constraint differently than workforce growth would: the scarce clinician input shrinks to ~20–40 minutes/month of billable management (G0553/G0554) wrapped around a software course whose marginal cost is near zero — and whose published price anchors ($899 US list; £45 UK contract price) bracket a per-course cost that is 0.4%–11% of the $2,083 first-year per-patient savings observed in the SleepioRx claims cohort ($45-equivalent ≈ 2–3% of savings; $899 ≈ 43%; arithmetic: 899/2083 = 0.43, 45×1.27 USD/GBP ≈ $57/2083 ≈ 0.03 [exchange-rate conversion is illustrative, [speculative]]).

## 4. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Pivotal-trial populations are not Medicare populations.** Espie 2012, Ritterband 2017, and Freeman 2017 enrolled mostly middle-aged, internet-using, self-selecting adults; OASIS (d=1.11) enrolled **university students** with insomnia and psychotic-like experiences — its huge N buys precision, not generalizability to a 70-year-old long-term zolpidem user. Effect sizes in M3's target population (65+, deprescribing context) should be assumed smaller. A 2025 RCT of dCBT-I in older adults exists ([npj Digital Medicine, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-025-01847-0)) but was not appraised here.
2. **The SleepioRx savings figure ($2,083) is observational and vendor-adjacent.** It is a matched-cohort claims study of *prescribed and engaged* patients (selection bias toward motivated users), and such studies frequently involve sponsor funding; M1 already flagged this, and Section 2.5's price-to-savings ratios inherit the bias. The Pear-era Somryst real-world data were a conference presentation, not a peer-reviewed comparative study.
3. **Market-withdrawal risk is demonstrated, not hypothetical.** The category's flagship company went bankrupt in 2023 despite FDA clearances and growing payer coverage. Somryst survived via a $3.9M distressed-asset sale — a price that signals how little the market then valued the asset. A coverage policy should anticipate vendor failure (data portability, mid-course continuity), and the M4 objections section must treat this as a live risk, not a closed chapter.
4. **G0552 economics are unresolved.** The single most decision-relevant number for a payer — what Medicare actually pays for the device — is contractor-priced, and no MAC-specific G0552 fee amounts were found in public fee schedules during this research (**not found**, searched June 2026). The ~$20/month management fees alone are unlikely to motivate clinician adoption; if MACs price G0552 near zero, the 2025 "coverage" is nominal. This is the strongest counter-argument to the dossier's optimistic framing.
5. **Pricing data are stale or foreign.** The only published US list price ($899) is from the defunct Pear era (2020); Nox Health's current pricing and Big Health's US contract pricing are unpublished (**not found**). The NICE £45 price is a UK national-procurement price unlikely to be reproducible in US commercial contracting.
6. **Trial-brand continuity caveat.** Somryst's pivotal evidence (SHUTi trials) and SleepioRx's pivotal evidence (Sleepio trials) were generated on the pre-clearance research/consumer versions of each program. FDA accepted these as supporting the cleared devices, but neither cleared *prescription* product has a completed post-clearance pivotal RCT under its current brand and ownership [this absence is an inference from the absence of such trials in searches, not from a systematic registry review — [speculative]].
7. **Payer-adoption picture is anecdotal.** Highmark, "16 Blues plans," and ~10 Medicaid programs are 2022-era data points from company statements reported by STAT; no current, systematic census of commercial/Medicaid PDT coverage for these two products was found (**not found**).
