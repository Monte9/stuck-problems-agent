## 1. Executive summary (one page)

**The problem you are paying for.** Chronic insomnia affects ~8% of adults; fewer than 10% of insomnia patients are referred to the guideline-recommended first-line treatment, CBT-I, and only ~2.6% of US adults used any CBT-I in the past year (M1 Rows 1, 4–5). Instead, members receive hypnotics — ~92M benzodiazepine and ~11M zolpidem prescriptions per year nationally (M1 Rows 6–7) — which in adults 65+ carry meta-analytic odds ratios of 1.57 for falls, 1.63 for fractures, and 1.51 for dementia (M3 §1). The binding constraint is workforce: ~659 credentialed behavioral-sleep-medicine providers nationally, i.e., ~0.26 per 100,000 adults against ~7,600 untreated sufferers per 100,000 (M1 §3 scale reference).

**The cost of inaction: ~$92M per year per 100,000 adult members (sensitivity range $17M–$146M)** — central case $92.49M, low $16.7M, high $146.1M (M1 §§2–3). If your committee credits only medical spend (excluding productivity), the modifiable-healthcare and fracture components alone are **~$5.7M–$68.1M per 100,000 adults** (central ~$16.2M **[derived in M4]**: M1 Component B $15.83M + Component C $0.34M).

**What changed.** Two digital CBT-I products hold FDA 510(k) clearance under 21 CFR 882.5801 (Somryst K191716; SleepioRx K233577), with RCT effect sizes of d≈0.95–1.11 on sleep outcomes, and since 2025-01-01 Medicare pays for them via HCPCS G0552/G0553/G0554 — but G0552 (the device itself) is contractor-priced with no findable published fee, so "Medicare coverage" is real in code and nominal in dollars until a payer sets a rate (M2 §2). A matched claims cohort (N=21,797) showed **−$2,083/patient (−42%) first-year total cost** after SleepioRx prescription (M2 §1; observational — see Objection 4).

### Recommended actions

1. **Add HCPCS G0552, G0553, and G0554 to your fee schedule and publish an explicit G0552 device rate** (for MA plans, set your own allowed amount rather than waiting on MAC pricing; for commercial/Medicaid lines, additionally activate A9291 with a stated per-course payment). Published price anchors bracket the decision: £45/person (NICE UK procurement) to $899/course (2020 US list) — 3%–43% of the observed $2,083 first-year savings. → Section 3.
2. **Issue a medical-necessity policy covering the FDA-cleared device category by regulation (21 CFR 882.5801, product code QVO), naming both current products** — Somryst (K191716) and SleepioRx (K233577) — on-label, prescribed within an ongoing behavioral-health plan of care, following the Highmark precedent. Covering the category, not one brand, is the hedge against vendor failure. → Sections 2–3, Objection 2.
3. **Stand up a claims-triggered deprescribing program for members 65+ dispensed a benzodiazepine or z-drug for >4 weeks:** mail a Sleepwell/EMPOWER-style package (RCT discontinuation 26.2% vs 7.5% usual care with zero clinician time) and reimburse pharmacist "pharmaceutical opinions" with a proposed taper (RCT discontinuation 43.2% vs 9.0%). → Section 4.
4. **Pair, don't block: add a refill-point prompt (not a hard edit) on hypnotic refills beyond ~4 weeks that offers covered CBT-I and a taper pathway,** consistent with 2023 Beers Criteria "avoid" status in 65+ — and do **not** impose step therapy requiring hypnotic failure before CBT-I, which would invert the guideline ordering (ACP 2016/AASM 2021 make CBT-I first-line). → Sections 1 body, 4.
5. **Contract for vendor failure and measure engagement:** require data-portability and mid-course continuity clauses (the category's flagship company went bankrupt in 2023), and track G-code claims volume, course completion, and ISI change from launch, with payment structured per course rather than per download where contractually possible. → Section 3, Objections 1–2.

Each action is supported in the section cited after the arrow; objections your committee will hear are pre-answered in Section 5.

## 3. The reimbursement mechanism — and the hole your committee must fill (from M2)

Full detail: [`./2026-06-11-m2-digital-cbti-product-reimbursement-dossier.md`](./2026-06-11-m2-digital-cbti-product-reimbursement-dossier.md) §2.

- **G0552** — supply of the digital mental-health-treatment device + onboarding, per course. **Contractor-priced; no national rate, and no MAC-published fee amount was findable as of June 2026.** Payable only for devices cleared under 21 CFR 882.5801 and only when furnished incident to professional behavioral-health services under an ongoing plan of care.
- **G0553 / G0554** — monthly treatment management, ≈$20.06 / ≈$19.73 per 20 minutes (CY2025 PFS).
- **A9291** — per-course PDT supply code; processes claims for Medicaid/commercial but has **no national Medicare payment**.
- The CY2026 final rule (CMS-1832-F, effective 2026-01-01) retained the G-codes and expanded eligible device categories — the policy direction is toward more digital-therapeutic coverage, not less.

**Decision-relevant consequence:** traditional Medicare "covers" the device in code but the dollar amount is opaque; commentators describe MAC pricing as "opaque, inconsistently applied" (M2 §2.4). A payer that publishes an explicit G0552/A9291 rate — bracketed by the £45 NICE and $899 Pear-era anchors, and sensibly below the $2,083 observed first-year savings — converts nominal coverage into an orderable benefit. (**Figure correction, flagged:** M2 §2.5's prose summarized the price-to-savings ratio as “0.4%–11%”, but its own stated arithmetic — $899/$2,083 = 43%; £45 ≈ $57/$2,083 ≈ 3% — supports **3%–43%**. M4 uses the arithmetic-consistent 3%–43% throughout; no underlying input changed.) This is the evidence base for executive-summary **Action 1** and the "incident-to wrapper" condition payers must build into the policy of **Action 2**.

Economics of the clinician wrapper: the scarce-clinician input shrinks to ~20–40 minutes/month at ~$20 per 20 minutes (G0553/G0554), against M1's ~29,800 untreated patients per trained behavioral-sleep-medicine provider — the supply problem is resolved by software plus brief management, not workforce growth (M2 §2.5).

## 4. The highest-yield target population: members 65+ on long-term hypnotics (from M3)

Full pathway, harm table, and contraindications: [`./2026-06-11-m3-deprescribing-substitution-brief.md`](./2026-06-11-m3-deprescribing-substitution-brief.md).

**Why this subgroup first:** both drug classes are "avoid" medications in the 2023 AGS Beers Criteria for adults 65+; harms include falls OR 1.57, fractures OR 1.63 (>300% increased zolpidem fracture risk over age 85), hip fracture HR 1.96 in dementia, and dementia OR 1.51 (M3 §1; observational caveats in M3 Limitation 3). M1 Component C prices hypnotic-attributable hip fractures alone at ~$0.34M per 100,000 adults per year, deliberately excluding falls without fracture, strokes, crashes, and dementia.

**What works, by trial, with the deliverer named (M3 §2):**

| Program element | RCT result (older long-term users) | Who delivers | Payer cost driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mailed self-help package (Sleepwell/EMPOWER materials) | YAWNS NB (N=565): discontinuation 26.2% vs 7.5% usual care; EMPOWER (N=303): 27% vs 5%, NNT 4 | Health plan mailroom / community pharmacist | Printing + postage; no clinician time |
| Pharmacist pharmaceutical opinion + proposed taper | D-PRESCRIBE (N=489): 43.2% vs 9.0% discontinuation at 6 months | Community pharmacist → GP/NP | Pharmacist professional fee |
| Gradual taper (~25% q1–2 weeks) + CBT-I | Taper+CBT-I 85% vs 48% drug-free (Morin 2004); 77% vs 38%, and 70% vs 24% at 12 months (Baillargeon 2003) | GP/NP writes taper; CBT-I via digital program, nurse-delivered BBTI, or therapist | G0552–G0554 (digital) or ~2.5 h nurse time (BBTI: response 67% vs 25%, NNT 2.4) |

**Honest flag, carried forward from M3 Limitation 1:** the trials proving the taper+CBT-I combination used 8–10 weeks of *therapist-delivered* CBT-I. **No published RCT combines an FDA-cleared digital CBT-I product with a structured taper in adults 65+** (not found, searched 2026-06-11). The digital substitution rests on (a) an older-adult RCT of the research adaptation underlying Somryst (SHUTi OASIS, N=311, ages 55–95, ISI d −1.04 to −1.43, 83% completion, greater sleep-medication reduction as a *secondary* outcome) and (b) the RCT-backed nurse-BBTI alternative for digitally excluded members. Recommendation 3 is therefore built on the mailed-package and pharmacist steps — which *are* RCT-proven in 65+ — with digital CBT-I as one of three delivery options for the CBT-I step, not the load-bearing element.

Contraindications that the program design must respect (M3 §3): BZRA prescribed for anxiety/seizures/alcohol withdrawal is out of scope; screen for untreated OSA and psychiatric comorbidity; sleep-restriction cautions (early-phase vigilance impairment, effect sizes 0.69–0.78, transient); dementia and suspected sedative use disorder route to specialist care; never stop abruptly.

This section supports executive-summary **Actions 3 and 4**.

## 6. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **This dossier inherits every limitation of M1–M3 and adds synthesis risk.** The headline $92.49M is 83% productivity loss, the softest component (M1 Limitation 1); the $2,083 savings anchor is observational and vendor-adjacent (M1 Limitation 2, M2 Limitation 2); the harm ORs carry confounding-by-indication and reverse-causation risk (M1 Limitation 3, M3 Limitation 3). A committee that rejects any one of those inputs should re-derive from the M1 sensitivity table rather than discard the case — the floor scenario ($16.7M, or $5.7M medical-only) survives all three objections simultaneously.
2. **The central extrapolation is flagged but real:** no RCT combines an FDA-cleared digital CBT-I product with a structured BZRA taper in adults 65+ (M3 Limitation 1, restated in Section 4 and Objection 6). If the digital option underperforms therapist delivery in that context, Recommendation 3's yield falls toward the mailed-package-only rates (~26% discontinuation), which is still 3.5× usual care but far below the 77–85% combination-trial rates.
3. **G0552 economics remain unresolved, which could moot Action 1's Medicare leg.** If MACs ultimately price G0552 near zero and a plan mirrors that, nominal coverage persists (M2 Limitation 4). The recommendation to publish an explicit rate is a judgment that a payer-set price is better than waiting — but no payer that has done so was identified in this research (**not found**), so Action 1 has no named precedent for the G0552 rate-setting step specifically (Highmark's policy precedent in Action 2 predates the 2025 G-codes).
4. **Pricing inputs are stale or foreign.** The $899 list price is from the defunct Pear era (2020); the £45 NICE price is a UK national-procurement artifact; current US contract prices for both products are unpublished (**not found**, M2 Limitation 5). The 3%–43% price-to-savings ratios in Objection 3 inherit this staleness.
5. **Dropout evidence cuts both ways.** The 59.33% average completion figure (Objection 1) comes from trials; real-world, payer-population completion is plausibly lower (trial volunteers are motivated), and the SleepioRx savings cohort was conditioned on engagement — so the same selection bias that inflates the savings estimate may deflate the projected dropout. No real-world completion rate for either cleared product in a general payer population was found (**not found**).
6. **Adoption-evidence vacuum for the new codes.** CY2025 Medicare claims volume under G0552–G0554 was unpublished at research time (M2 §2.4, **not found**); it is possible uptake is near zero, which would support the dossier's thesis (coverage without published pricing doesn't move clinicians) but would also mean no early-adopter experience exists to copy.
7. **Counter-evidence that the problem is partially self-correcting:** zolpidem prescriptions have declined since 2013 (M1 Row 6, Limitation 4), and the CY2026 rule's expansion suggests CMS may eventually resolve the pricing gap without payer action. A committee could defensibly wait 12 months for MAC pricing and CY2025 utilization data — the cost of that delay is bounded by the per-100,000 figures above.
