Insomnia drains $207.5B a year from the US economy, and the pills seniors get instead of the recommended therapy raise their odds of falls (1.57x) and fractures (1.63x). The therapy that beats the pills, CBT-I, reaches under 10% of patients.

The twist: Medicare "covered" digital CBT-I in January 2025, but the device code is contractor-priced and no published fee exists 18 months later. Coverage in code, not in dollars.

The fixes are cheap. A mailed self-help package got 26.2% of older long-term users off the drugs vs 7.5% usual care (JAMA Psychiatry 2024), for printing and postage. A payer that publishes a device rate (anchors: £45 to $899 per course, against $2,083 observed first-year savings per patient) turns nominal coverage into something a doctor can order.
