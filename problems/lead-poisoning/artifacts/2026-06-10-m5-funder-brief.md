# M5 — Funder-Facing Action Brief: Where the Next Marginal Lead Dollar Should Go

**Date:** 2026-06-10
**Milestone:** M5 (Funder-facing action brief)
**Audience:** A philanthropic funder in the Open Philanthropy / Lead Exposure Action Fund (LEAF) / LEAP mold deciding where marginal dollars go now that the US government's lead-program multiplier has receded.
**Synthesizes:** `artifacts/2026-06-10-m1-data-landscape.md`, `artifacts/2026-06-10-m2-priority-shortlist.md`, `artifacts/2026-06-10-m3-source-profiles.md`, `artifacts/2026-06-10-m4-evidence-dossier.md` (full appendix at end).

---

## 1. The case, in one page

Childhood lead poisoning in LMICs is a large, measurable, and unusually tractable problem — but the binding constraint on the *next* dollar is no longer "is this a real problem?" It is **attribution**: in most high-burden countries we have a modeled burden number and no idea which source is poisoning the children. The four prior artifacts in this project trace a path from that gap to a single shovel-ready target.

**The burden is real but mostly modeled.** Of ~137 LMICs, pooled child blood-lead means exist for only **34** (Ericson et al., *Lancet Planet Health* 2021;5(3):e145–e153 — https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanplh/article/PIIS2542-5196(20)30278-3/fulltext) [`artifacts/2026-06-10-m1-data-landscape.md`, Table B + limitation #2]. M1 flags five high-burden countries — DRC, Afghanistan, Yemen, Myanmar, Madagascar — with *no usable blood-lead survey at all*, and shows even the giants (India, Nigeria, Pakistan, Egypt) have only localized or outbreak data, never a nationally representative child survey [`...m1-data-landscape.md`, "No usable blood-lead data" section].

**The marginal opportunity is deepening, not greenfield.** M2's scoring rubric (Burden×0.25 + Data-gap×0.25 + Tractability×0.30 + Whitespace×0.20) ranked Indonesia (#1, 3.60), Egypt (#2, 3.50), and the Philippines (#3, 3.40), and surfaced the central surprise: Pure Earth's LEAF-funded program already covers seven of the most tractable high-burden countries, so genuine greenfield is scarce — the realistic win is national surveys plus *source-to-blood attribution*, not first entry [`artifacts/2026-06-10-m2-priority-shortlist.md`, §2 and limitation #2].

**One target is shovel-ready.** M3 found that across all three top countries, the dominant uncertainty is which source carries the *national* burden — but Indonesia has the one source with direct, locally-measured, source-linked child blood-lead: informal used lead-acid battery (ULAB) recycling (grade A) [`artifacts/2026-06-10-m3-source-profiles.md`, Country 1]. M4 turned that into a regulator-ready dossier and found the decisive fact for a funder: **this is not a missing-law problem, it is a non-enforcement problem.** Indonesia already classifies spent batteries as hazardous (B3) waste and bans unlicensed smelting under Government Regulation PP No. 22/2021 and MoEF Reg. No. 6/2021, yet KLHK has issued ~5 permits against an estimated >200 illegal smelters, and its own hazardous-waste director-general has said authorities "can't close" them for fear of job loss and displacement [`artifacts/2026-06-10-m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §3 and §7; National Geographic — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/indonesia-s-toxic-toll].

**So the marginal dollar should buy the three things money *can* buy that unlock enforcement:** (a) the georeferenced contamination-plus-blood map that makes enforcement politically survivable, (b) a formal-sector off-ramp so closures don't just displace smelters, and (c) the rapid product-screening in the other two top countries that converts "we have a modeled number" into "we know what to regulate." The Bangladesh turmeric precedent shows the payoff: enforcement of an *existing* prohibition drove turmeric-lead prevalence from 47% (2019) to 0% (2021) at roughly **US$1 per DALY averted** (EA Forum CEA summary — https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/aFYduhr9pztFCWFpz/; Stanford GSB — https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/food-safety-policy-enforcement-associated-actions-reduce-turmeric).

## 2. Fundable line items

Cost figures are order-of-magnitude planning anchors, each tied to a cited real-world comparator. They are deliberately rough — see §4.

### Line item 1 — Indonesia ULAB contamination-plus-blood map (the enforcement-unlock)
- **What:** Handheld-XRF soil/dust/slag survey of Jabodetabek informal smelter clusters paired with child blood-lead sampling in the same households, across smelter and non-smelter areas — M4 intervention step 1 [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §5].
- **Who could execute:** Pure Earth/LEAF (already operative in-country) with Indonesian university partners; KPBB as local CSO [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §6].
- **Rough cost: ~US$150k–350k.** Anchored to Pure Earth's Rapid Market Screening at **~$36,800 per country** (~$920,000 / 25 countries; GiveWell incubation-grant analysis — https://www.givewell.org/research/incubation-grants/Pure-Earth-lead-exposure-July-2021), scaled up several-fold because this adds venous child-BLL collection and lab analysis (Bhutan's national survey required ~Nu 18M / ≈US$215k of donated lab equipment alone; UNICEF Bhutan — https://www.unicef.org/bhutan/press-releases/nu-18million-worth-equipment-handed-over-unicef-lead-free-bhutan).
- **Expected output:** A defensible, georeferenced map apportioning point-source vs product burden — the single thing M3 says only field work can resolve [`...m3-source-profiles.md`, Country 1, "what field testing must resolve"] — and the politically survivable evidence base M4 says enforcement requires [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §1 bottom line].

### Line item 2 — Formal-sector ULAB off-ramp pilot (the displacement firebreak)
- **What:** A buy-back / smelter-consolidation pilot routing informal collectors to licensed or state-linked (Indonesia Battery Corporation) smelters *before* enforcement — M4 step 4, the one step with no Bangladesh analog and flagged as the single most important adaptation [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §5 step 4].
- **Who could execute:** Pure Earth (has floated consolidating smelters at one scrubber-equipped facility) with licensed recyclers and KLHK [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §6; National Geographic — https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/indonesia-s-toxic-toll].
- **Rough cost: ~US$0.5M–2M** for a multi-site pilot. Anchored to LEAF's own scale — LEAF has committed >$20M including "improving lead-acid battery recycling in Indonesia," within $104M to allocate by end-2027 (Coefficient Giving / Open Philanthropy — https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/lead-exposure-action-fund/) — so a sub-$2M pilot is a small slice of an already-committed envelope.
- **Expected output:** Evidence that closures can stick without displacement — directly answering M4's dominant, Indonesia-specific failure mode (KLHK's stated fear that operators relocate to hidden sites) [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §7 risk #1].

### Line item 3 — Egypt + Philippines rapid product screening and child-BLL baseline (close the attribution gap in #2 and #3)
- **What:** A Pure Earth-style multi-product market screen plus a first representative child-BLL baseline in Egypt (where M3 found public product-testing the thinnest of the three and *no* child-BLL survey at all) and a household child-BLL study in the Philippines (which knows what is contaminated on the shelf — 33% of cosmetics, 24% of cookware over threshold — but not what is in children's blood) [`...m3-source-profiles.md`, Country 2 and Country 3].
- **Who could execute:** Pure Earth/EcoWaste (Philippines product-survey track record) and a LEAF implementer in Egypt with the Egyptian Drug Authority / EEAA [`...m2-priority-shortlist.md`, §3].
- **Rough cost: ~US$75k–150k for screening + ~$100k–250k for a child-BLL baseline,** anchored to the RMS ~$36,800/country screening cost (GiveWell — https://www.givewell.org/research/incubation-grants/Pure-Earth-lead-exposure-July-2021) and the Bhutan survey equipment figure above.
- **Expected output:** Converts two modeled-burden countries into ones with a named dominant source and a measured child baseline — the precondition M3 sets for any M4-style dossier in country #2 or #3.

### Line item 4 (optional, higher-risk) — Scoping survey in a data-dark high-burden country (DRC)
- **What:** A first localized child-BLL plus environmental survey in DRC, M1's highest-priority *no-data* country and M2's #4 (burden 4, data-gap 5, whitespace 4 — but tractability 1) [`...m2-priority-shortlist.md`, §2; `...m1-data-landscape.md`, Table B #7].
- **Who could execute:** Pure Earth TSIP plus an in-country research partner; security/access is the gating risk.
- **Rough cost: ~US$200k–400k,** carrying a tractability premium over the RMS anchor because of operating difficulty [`...m2-priority-shortlist.md`, tractability=1].
- **Expected output:** The first measured number for a country that today exists only as a modeled GBD estimate. Flagged optional and higher-risk precisely because M2 scored DRC untractable.

**Recommended allocation:** Fund line items 1–3 as the core package (~$0.9M–2.9M); treat line item 4 as a separately-justified exploratory bet.

## 3. What field labor and regulator will look like in dollars

Items 1 and 3 are bounded, deliverable-defined purchases. Items 2 and 4 are *bets on conditions money alone cannot create* (see §4). A funder should expect items 1 and 3 to produce artifacts on schedule, and items 2 and 4 to produce *learning* — including the possibility that the off-ramp or the access does not materialize.

---

## 4. What these recommendations do NOT solve

Money buys measurement, screening, pilots, and convening. It does not buy the two things the original problem brief identified as the true constraints, and both reappear sharply here.

1. **Field labor and XRF-team throughput.** Every line item above bottlenecks on a scarce resource: trained operators who can run handheld XRF, draw venous child blood, and navigate local markets and smelter villages. M1's paywall/access friction (HTTP 403s on primary sources; figures pulled from mirrors) is the desk-research version of a problem that gets worse in the field [`...m1-data-landscape.md`, limitation #4]. M4 names KLHK's limited inspection throughput across the Jabodetabek fringe (~5 permits issued against >200 sites) as a binding constraint even *with* political will [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §7 risk #4]. A funder can finance teams but cannot conjure the trained-operator pipeline overnight; this is a residual bottleneck a grant does not retire.

2. **Regulator will and the political economy of enforcement.** Indonesia's is explicitly a non-enforcement problem: the law exists, and KLHK has declined to act for ~20 years since KPBB first counted 71 smelters, citing job loss [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §3, §7 risks #1 and #3; The Jakarta Post 2018 via M4]. No line item *guarantees* the regulator acts — the map (item 1) and the off-ramp (item 2) are designed to *lower the political cost* of acting, but the decision stays with KLHK. If the formal off-ramp is captured by the higher-prestige EV/nickel battery narrative, the unglamorous lead-acid stream may simply be deprioritized by the very state actors best placed to provide it [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §7 risk #6].

3. **(Residual, methodological) The attribution itself may disappoint.** M3 and M4 both warn that ULAB is grade-A *near smelters* but its share of Indonesia's modeled ~7.89M >5 µg/dL children is unknown; if ubiquitous cookware/ceramics drive the population mean, a ULAB-only program could collapse the worst local exposures yet barely move the national figure — and be judged a failure on the wrong metric [`...m3-source-profiles.md`, limitation #2; `...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §7 risk #2]. Funding item 1 is partly a hedge against this — but the funder should hold the national-burden claim loosely.

The honest framing for the funder: these dollars buy the *evidence and the off-ramp* that make enforcement possible and make the next dossier (Egypt or the Philippines) writable. They do not buy enforcement, the field-labor pipeline, or a guarantee on national-burden share. Those remain the named residual bottlenecks.

## 5. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **Cost anchors are thin and cross-applied.** The single cleanest comparator — RMS at ~$36,800/country (GiveWell — https://www.givewell.org/research/incubation-grants/Pure-Earth-lead-exposure-July-2021) — is a *product-screening* cost; line items 1 and 3 add blood-lead collection and lab work that the RMS did not, so my several-fold scale-up is a judgment, not a quoted budget. I found no published per-program budget for the Bangladesh turmeric intervention (only its ~$1/DALY ratio) or for LEEP's individual country programs (only ~$1.66/child and ~$14/DALY-equivalent; Founders Pledge / LEEP — https://leadelimination.org/how-cost-effective-are-leeps-paint-programs/), so several anchors are cost-*effectiveness* ratios rather than line-item budgets. Treat every dollar figure as order-of-magnitude.

2. **The ranking that drives this brief encodes a contestable value judgment.** M2 weighted tractability highest (30%), deliberately pushing the highest-*burden* giants (India, Nigeria, Pakistan) out of the top 3 [`...m2-priority-shortlist.md`, limitation #1]. A funder optimizing for raw children-affected, or comfortable funding hard-to-operate places, would reweight and might fund India or Nigeria over Indonesia — making line items 1–2 the wrong first buy. The whole brief inherits that weighting choice.

3. **Whitespace double-counts program presence.** M2's own limitation #3 notes that Pure Earth choosing Indonesia is simultaneously evidence of *low whitespace* (a negative) and *high tractability* (a positive); the rubric scored it only as a negative. The same fact that makes Indonesia "already covered" is what makes it executable — so the marginal-value argument is softer than it looks.

4. **The grade-A foundation is one seven-year-old study.** Indonesia's ULAB child-BLL signal (47% ≥5 µg/dL) rests on a single 2019 Greater Jakarta cross-sectional study [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §8 limitation #1; Suprapto et al. 2019 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6480953/]. Line item 1 is partly an effort to *re-establish* this signal at scale — but if the original is unrepresentative, the priority ordering shifts.

5. **Several supply-chain and enforcement facts are journalism, not peer review.** The >200-vs-5 smelter ratio is Pure Earth's own estimate, and the KLHK quote and 34-Jakarta-sites figure come from National Geographic and The Jakarta Post [`...m4-evidence-dossier.md`, §8 limitation #4]. The non-enforcement framing that anchors line item 1's rationale is credible but not independently verified.

6. **LEAF may already be funding parts of this.** LEAF has committed >$20M including Indonesia ULAB recycling work (Coefficient Giving — https://coefficientgiving.org/funds/lead-exposure-action-fund/) and Pure Earth received Audacious Project funding in early 2026 to reach 500M children by 2033 (BusinessWire, 2026-02-24 — https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260224853445/en/). A marginal funder should check whether these line items are *additional* to, or duplicative of, commitments already in motion — the same whitespace caveat M2 raised, now at the dollar level.

7. **English-language / NGO-linked source bias** carries through from M1–M4 [`...m1-data-landscape.md`, limitation #7]: Indonesian-language KLHK enforcement records and provincial environmental-agency budgets are under-represented; "no published budget found" means not found in this search, not that none exists.
