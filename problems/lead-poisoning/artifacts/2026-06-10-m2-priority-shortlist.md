# M2 — Priority-Country Shortlist for New Source-Attribution Field Work

**Date:** 2026-06-10
**Milestone:** M2 (Priority-country shortlist)
**Primary input:** `artifacts/2026-06-10-m1-data-landscape.md` (the M1 data-landscape inventory). All burden figures and data-availability flags below are reconciled against M1's Table B; bracketed `[M1-Bn]` pointers refer to that table's row numbers and `[M1-refN]` to its reference key. New facts gathered for this milestone carry their own inline citations.
**Question being answered:** Of the high-burden LMICs, where should a *new, marginal* field team point its XRF guns for source-attribution work that can plausibly produce a regulator-ready dossier (M3/M4)? This is deliberately **not** "which country has the worst lead problem" — it is "where does an additional team add the most value that is not already being captured."

---

## 1. Scoring rubric (stated before any scores)

Four criteria, each scored on an integer **1–5** scale (5 = most favourable for selecting this country). The weighted total is reproducible arithmetic:

> **Total = Burden×0.25 + Data/attribution gap×0.25 + Tractability×0.30 + Whitespace×0.20**

Tractability carries the single largest weight because the end goal is field work *and* a usable regulator dossier (M4); an untractable country yields neither, regardless of burden. The total lands on the same 1–5 scale (so ~3.0 is the mid-point).

| Criterion (weight) | What it measures | 1 (low) | 3 (mid) | 5 (high / most favourable) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Burden** (25%) | Magnitude of child lead exposure: absolute affected child population and/or measured/modeled BLL severity | Low or unmeasured burden; mostly inference | Moderate modeled burden, some localized confirmation | Very large affected child population and/or extreme measured prevalence |
| **Data / attribution gap** (25%) | How much a *new* team adds: is burden plausibly real but the dominant source(s) unidentified and no national blood-lead survey done? | Sources already well-characterized AND a survey exists (little to add) | Some product data or one signature source documented, but no national survey / incomplete attribution | Strong burden signal but sources essentially unresolved and no national survey — XRF adds clear discovery value |
| **Tractability** (30%) | Can a team actually operate and convert findings into regulation? Security/access, functioning regulator, a local partner/data base to build on, market access | Conflict / failed governance; no workable regulator | Functioning but bureaucratic/centralized state; partial access | Stable governance, receptive/active regulator, existing partner and accessible markets |
| **Whitespace** (20%) | Marginal value given existing programs (LEEP, Pure Earth, Stanford/Project Unleaded, UNICEF MICS Pb) | 3+ active programs / saturated | One program, or only a just-started program | No major source-attribution program found |

**Whitespace scoring key (explicit, since it drives the surprising part of the result):** 5 = none of the four orgs active; 4 = only LEEP paint *or* only a Pure Earth contaminated-sites entry; 3 = one substantive program (e.g. RMS sampling done, or a Pure Earth program that began only in late 2024); 2 = two programs; 1 = three or more.

**Tie-break rule:** equal totals are broken by (a) Tractability, then (b) Whitespace, then (c) Burden.

A note carried forward from M1's limitation #1: burden is not one comparable metric. Burden scores below are ordinal judgments triangulating the modeled GBD/IHME burden, the UNICEF/Pure Earth "Toxic Truth" affected-child counts, and any direct measurement — they are not a precise cardinal ranking.

## 3. Top-3 rationale (per-criterion scores justified)

### #1 — Indonesia (3.60)

- **Burden = 4.** IHME GBD 2021 estimates **7,890,945 Indonesian children (7.1%) with BLL >5 µg/dL** — a very large absolute affected population, the world's 4th-most-populous country [IHME 2021, as reported by Pure Earth Indonesia — https://www.pureearth.org/mitigating-lead-exposure-in-low-and-middle-income-countries/; corroborated by M1-B6, M1-ref1]. Localized surveys confirm severity (geometric-mean 5.5 µg/dL in a tin-mining area, 2023) [M1-B6]. Scored 4 not 5 because there is no national prevalence measurement to confirm the modeled count.
- **Data/attribution gap = 4.** No nationally representative child blood-lead survey exists; only localized studies [M1-B6]. ULAB recycling is a documented driver — "only five licensed ULAB smelters... but it is estimated that there are more than 200 ULAB smelting locations" [Pure Earth, same URL above] — yet consumer-product sources (cookware, ceramics, spices, cosmetics) beyond ULAB are largely uncharacterized. A new team can both run the missing survey and attribute non-ULAB sources.
- **Tractability = 4.** Stable middle-income state with functioning sector regulators (BPOM for food/drugs, KLHK for environment) and accessible urban markets; Pure Earth already works "with national ministries, the Basel and Stockholm Conventions Regional Centre for Southeast Asia and leading Indonesian universities" [Pure Earth, same URL], a revealed signal that the operating environment is workable.
- **Whitespace = 2.** Two-plus active programs: the Pure Earth/LEAF ULAB program (Q4 2024–) [Pure Earth URL above], a LEEP paint program [M1-B6; M1-ref5], and prior Pure Earth RMS sampling [M1-B6; M1-ref4]. Scored 2 (not lower) because all existing work is paint/ULAB-focused, leaving consumer-product source-attribution open.
- **Existing-program check (as of 2026-06-10):** Pure Earth — **yes**, ULAB program under LEAF since Q4 2024 [Pure Earth URL above]; LEEP — **yes**, paint program [M1-ref5]; Stanford/Project Unleaded — **none found** [M1-ref11 lists only Bangladesh, Georgia, India, Pakistan]; UNICEF MICS Pb module — **none found as of 2026-06-10** [M1-ref10 lists Bangladesh, Bhutan, Georgia].

### #2 — Egypt (3.50)

- **Burden = 4.** Egypt is among the countries with the **highest age-standardized lead-attributable ischaemic-heart-disease burden** in GBD 2021 [M1-B10; M1-ref1], in a population of ~110 million — a large affected pool. Scored 4 because the evidence is modeled cardiovascular burden rather than a direct child-BLL count.
- **Data/attribution gap = 4.** Only localized studies and no national child blood-lead survey; public product-testing data is limited and the paint-limit status is uncertain [M1-B10; M1-ref6]. The dominant source is genuinely unresolved — close to a discovery-grade opportunity.
- **Tractability = 3.** A centralized, bureaucratic but functioning state with national regulators (Egyptian Drug Authority; Ministry of Environment / EEAA). Pure Earth's decision to include Egypt in its LEAF program is itself a revealed-preference signal that the country is workable [Pure Earth URL above]. Scored 3 (not higher) for bureaucratic friction and limited civil-society openness relative to Indonesia/Philippines.
- **Whitespace = 3.** Only one substantive program found, and it started recently: the Pure Earth/LEAF program (Q4 2024–) [Pure Earth URL above]. No confirmed LEEP study, RMS sampling, Stanford, or UNICEF MICS Pb activity in Egypt was found, so a new team enters relatively early.
- **Existing-program check (as of 2026-06-10):** Pure Earth — **yes**, via the LEAF program since Q4 2024 [Pure Earth URL above]; LEEP — **none confirmed** (Egypt not listed among LEEP's named country programs in M1-ref5; treat as none found as of 2026-06-10); Stanford/Project Unleaded — **none found** [M1-ref11]; UNICEF MICS Pb module — **none found as of 2026-06-10** [M1-ref10].

### #3 — Philippines (3.40)

- **Burden = 3.** Modeled elevated BLL with a leaded-gasoline legacy and ULAB recycling [M1-B9; M1-ref1]; no exceptional absolute count is flagged in "Toxic Truth," so burden scores moderate.
- **Data/attribution gap = 3.** No recent nationally representative child blood-lead survey [M1-B9]; however, product sources are already partly characterized — a Pure Earth 2021–22 market survey (17 markets, 144 vendors, 856 samples) found **24% of metal cookware, 13% of ceramic foodware, 16% of household paints and 33% of cosmetics above lead thresholds** [EcoWaste/Pure Earth reporting — https://www.pureearth.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/PE-PH-Lead-Briefer_2024.pdf; product-survey figures via Pure Earth Philippines survey 2021–2022]. Because candidate sources are already screened, new field value is more confirmatory (linking sources to measured BLL) than discovery — hence 3, not 4.
- **Tractability = 5.** The most tractable of the shortlist: a democracy with an English-language regulatory system, an active Food and Drug Administration that has issued enforceable spray-paint bans (97+ lead spray paints flagged; FDA public-health advisories), and a strong civil-society partner in the EcoWaste Coalition [https://ipen.org/news/philippines-lead-safe-paint-advocates-ramp-collaboration-vs-lead-containing-paints; https://journal.com.ph/ecowaste-coalition-uncovers-12-more-spray-paints-with-banned-lead-additives/].
- **Whitespace = 2.** Multiple active programs: Pure Earth product survey + the LEAF program (Q4 2024–) [Pure Earth URL above; PE-PH briefer], plus a LEEP paint program [M1-B9; M1-ref5]. Scored 2: the gap left open is a national survey and source-to-blood attribution, not greenfield discovery.
- **Existing-program check (as of 2026-06-10):** Pure Earth — **yes**, 2021–22 product survey and LEAF program since Q4 2024 [PE-PH briefer; Pure Earth URL above]; LEEP — **yes**, paint program [M1-ref5]; Stanford/Project Unleaded — **none found** [M1-ref11]; UNICEF MICS Pb module — **none found as of 2026-06-10** [M1-ref10].

## 4. Limitations & counter-evidence

1. **The rubric encodes a value judgment that could be contested.** Weighting tractability highest (30%) deliberately pushes the highest-*burden* countries (India, Nigeria, Pakistan — all burden 5) out of the top 3. A funder optimizing for raw children-affected, or one comfortable funding hard-to-operate places, would re-weight and get India or Nigeria at the top. The ordering is a function of the weights, not a fact about the world.

2. **"Whitespace" is the most fragile criterion and it moved the answer.** The discovery that Pure Earth's LEAF-funded program (begun Q4 2024) already covers Colombia, Egypt, Ghana, India, Indonesia, Peru, and the Philippines [https://www.pureearth.org/mitigating-lead-exposure-in-low-and-middle-income-countries/] cut the whitespace scores of five of my own shortlist countries — including all three of the top 3. Genuine greenfield among tractable high-burden LMICs is scarce; the realistic marginal opportunity is *deepening* (national surveys + source-to-blood attribution) rather than first entry. If those programs are broader than reported, even these whitespace scores are too generous.

3. **Program presence cuts both ways and I scored it as a negative only.** Pure Earth choosing Egypt/Indonesia/Philippines is also evidence those countries are tractable and high-priority — i.e., the same fact that lowers whitespace *raises* tractability. The rubric does not net these out; a reviewer could argue I am double-counting program presence.

4. **Burden scores rest partly on modeled GBD/IHME estimates.** Indonesia's "7.9M children >5 µg/dL" and Egypt's lead-IHD ranking are modeled, inheriting M1's circularity caveat (limitation #2 there): countries with no national survey carry imputed numbers. A country could be over- or under-ranked because its *model* says so, not its measurements.

5. **Tractability scores are judgments with thin citation.** Security, regulator receptivity, and partner availability are scored on qualitative evidence (e.g., the existence of EcoWaste, FDA bans, functioning ministries). I did not find a quantitative governance index applied uniformly across all ten; Egypt's "3" vs Vietnam's "4", in particular, is a defensible-but-arguable call.

6. **Staleness of supporting evidence.** Vietnam's signature Dong Mai data (28% of children ≥45 µg/dL) is from ~2012 fieldwork [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4637436/], and Philippines product figures are 2021–22. Both informed scores but may not reflect 2026 conditions after remediation/regulation.

7. **Egypt's #2 rank rests on thin primary data.** Its burden score leans on a cardiovascular (adult) GBD metric rather than child BLL, and almost no public product-testing exists [M1-B10]. The high gap score is partly "we don't know," which is simultaneously the case *for* sending a team and a reason its true burden could be lower than modeled.

8. **English-language and NGO-linked source bias persists from M1** (M1 limitation #7): "none found" for Stanford/UNICEF activity in the top-3 countries means none found in this search, not none exists.
