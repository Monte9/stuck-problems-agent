One in three children on Earth, about 800 million, carries enough lead in their blood to permanently lower IQ. Estimated deaths run 1.5 to 5.5 million a year. Until 2024, the world spent roughly $15M a year fighting it.

I pointed an autonomous research agent at one question: where does the next dollar do the most? It triaged 25 countries in 16 hours. The surprise: the best target already has the law. Indonesia bans unlicensed lead smelting, yet ~5 licensed smelters coexist with 200+ illegal ones, and 47% of young children near Jakarta's recyclers have elevated blood lead. The regulator says it "can't close" them.

What unlocks enforcement is not legislation. It is a $150–350k map pairing smelter locations with child blood data, plus an off-ramp for the smelters' livelihoods.
