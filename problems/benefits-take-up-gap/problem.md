# Problem: the benefits take-up gap — eligible people who never get the help

> Intake brief from the scout routine, 2026-06-23. Category: economics and inequality.
> Bottleneck type: administrative burden + misaligned incentives (no agency is paid for the person it *fails* to enroll), compounded by unglamorous data-matching grind.

**(a) The problem.** The US runs a large anti-poverty system that systematically misses the people it is designed to reach — not because they are ineligible, but because of paperwork. Roughly [$60 billion in benefits go unclaimed each year](https://www.chicagofed.org/publications/profitwise-news-and-views/2022/millions-tax-refunds-unclaimed). For the EITC alone, about [5 million eligible taxpayers don't claim it, leaving $7+ billion on the table](https://taxpolicycenter.org/briefing-book/do-all-people-eligible-eitc-participate); ~$1,500 average per household. For SNAP, [nearly 9 million eligible older adults (50+) aren't enrolled — only ~30% participation in that group, 16 million eligible non-enrollees overall](https://www.aarp.org/pri/topics/health/food-insecurity/16-million-eligible-adults-not-enrolled-snap/). The mechanism is not ignorance. During Medicaid "unwinding," [more than 25 million people were disenrolled by mid-2025, and about 69% lost coverage for *procedural* reasons](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/lessons-from-unwinding-offer-opportunities-to-streamline-medicaid-improve) — a missed form, a returned letter — not because they stopped qualifying. The human cost is missed meals, untreated illness, and forgone cash for working-poor families during the exact months they need it.

**(b) The half-known solution.** The cure is data the government already holds. SNAP income data can complete a Medicaid renewal automatically ([ex parte renewal](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/using-snap-data-for-medicaid-renewals-can-keep-eligible-beneficiaries-enrolled)); prior-year tax records verify eligibility without asking the applicant for anything ([Dorn/Urban](https://www.urban.org/sites/default/files/publication/24026/412920-Using-Past-Income-Data-to-Verify-Current-Medicaid-Eligibility.PDF)). Pre-populated tax returns work: a [simulation found tens of millions of returns could be auto-filled from IRS data](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w30008/w30008.pdf), and IRS Direct File [began pre-populating W-2, interest, and prior-year data in 2025](https://taxpayer-rights.org/2025-direct-file-report/). Because [two-thirds of EITC non-claimants simply don't file](https://taxpolicycenter.org/briefing-book/do-all-people-eligible-eitc-participate), a pre-filled return that just needs a signature would capture most of the gap. The fix is grind, not invention — the theory (Herd & Moynihan's [administrative burden](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/how-to-address-the-administrative-burdens-of-accessing-the-safety-net/): learning, compliance, psychological costs) is settled.

**(c) Why it's stuck.** A dramatic inversion sits at the center: the one federal tool that worked was killed. IRS [Direct File grew from 141,000 returns (2024) to 300,000+ across 25 states (2025) and was popular](https://fedscoop.com/direct-file-future-of-tax-filing-fellows/) — then the administration [ended it in 2025](https://mnbudgetproject.org/blog/trump-administration-plans-to-end-direct-file-removing-a-vital-option-to-save-time-and-money-when-filing-taxes). Beyond that, incentives are perverse: agencies are budgeted and audited on *improper payments* (enrolling someone ineligible), never on *eligible people excluded*, so every system is tuned to make enrollment hard. Data-sharing between tax, SNAP, and Medicaid systems is legally and technically balkanized state by state. The grind — building the matches, cleaning addresses, reconciling rolls — is real work nobody is staffed to do, and the 2025 reconciliation law's new procedural requirements (more frequent redeterminations) push churn the wrong way. This is scurvy-shaped: the remedy is known and cheap, adoption lags by decades.

**(d) The AI-agent wedge.** Pure desk synthesis on public data. An agent could: build the by-program, by-state take-up league table (EITC, SNAP, Medicaid, WIC, LIHEAP) from IRS, USDA FNS, KFF, and CBPP figures, normalized so a stranger sees the worst gaps; compute the dollars-and-lives cost of each state's procedural-disenrollment rate (the 69%-procedural figure, rebuilt per state); assemble the evidence dossier that quantifies how much of the gap pre-filled returns and ex-parte renewal would close (synthesizing the Goldin/NBER simulations and the CalEITC administrative-data study); and draft the auto-enrollment playbook a state Medicaid director or USDA could act on — which datasets to match, what each match captures, modeled on the states already doing it. It can't sign returns, but it can convert a $60B abstraction into a ranked, costed, state-specific docket.

**(e) Closest to cracking it.** Jacob Goldin and co-authors (auto-file / pre-populated returns, the empirical case); the [California Policy Lab](https://www.capolicylab.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Measuring-Take-up-of-the-CalEITC-with-State-Administrative-Data.pdf) and New America Chicago (state pre-filled EITC pilots with IL Revenue); [CBPP](https://www.cbpp.org/research/health/looking-across-the-landscape-state-opportunities-to-increase-access-to-snap) and the Commonwealth Fund (Medicaid–SNAP ex parte coordination); the former IRS Direct File team, now regrouping outside government; Code for America (GetCalFresh, file-your-state-taxes tooling). The tools exist and individual states run pieces of the playbook. What's missing is the synthesized, costed, national map that names which lever each agency should pull first.
